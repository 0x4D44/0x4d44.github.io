Orders of magnitude

Three and a half decades of bitrate, on one axis.

The span from a military vocoder to an uncompressed studio master is a factor of about three thousand. Almost every argument in this study is really an argument about where on this axis a given trick stops working.

Six confusions worth fixing

The words that regularly get used for the wrong thing.

1 · "MP3" is not a synonym for "lossy"

MP3 is one 1993 codec — MPEG-1/2 Audio Layer III. AAC, Vorbis, Opus and the rest are not "MP3 variants"; two of them do not share a single coding tool with it. The word survives as a generic because of what happened to the file extension, not because of the technology.

2 · Bark, ERB and the critical band

All three describe the ear's frequency resolution and none is interchangeable with the others. The critical band is the phenomenon. The Bark scale is Zwicker's 24-band quantisation of it, from the 1960s, with fixed edges. The ERB scale is Glasberg and Moore's later, continuous and narrower estimate from notched-noise experiments. An ERB at 1 kHz is about 133 Hz; the Bark band containing 1 kHz is 160 Hz wide. See chapter 02.

3 · Four different "frames"

An MP3 frame is 1152 samples and a byte-aligned header; an MDCT frame (block) is the window the transform sees, which overlaps its neighbours; an Opus frame is 2.5 to 60 ms of audio, and a packet may contain several; a FLAC frame is an independently decodable block of typically 4096 samples. Only the last of those is what most people picture.

4 · Codec is not container

.m4a, .ogg, .mkv and .wav are containers; AAC, Vorbis, Opus and PCM are codecs. A .ogg file may hold Vorbis, Opus, FLAC or Speex, and "converting to Ogg" is therefore not a statement about quality at all. See chapter 12.

5 · "Lossless" and "transparent" are different claims

Lossless is arithmetic: the decoder returns the exact input samples, and you can prove it with a checksum. Transparent is perceptual: a listener cannot reliably tell the coded version from the original under blind conditions. A transparent file is usually nowhere near lossless — its waveform may differ by tens of decibels — and transparency depends on the listener, the programme and the playback chain.

6 · Sample rate is not "resolution"

Sample rate sets the bandwidth — the highest frequency that can be represented, at fs/2. Bit depth sets the noise floor. A 44.1 kHz recording is not a "low resolution" version of a 96 kHz one in the way a small image is of a large one: below 22 kHz, the sampling theorem says the two describe the same waveform exactly. See chapter 01.

Units and symbols

Which decibel is meant, and what the letters stand for.

The decibel is a ratio, so it means nothing without its reference. Four of them appear in this study and mixing them up is the commonest arithmetic error in the field.

Who worked it out, and when

Two centuries, condensed.

Perceptual audio coding is unusual in how directly it depends on nineteenth- and twentieth-century laboratory psychology. The transform mathematics and the hearing measurements arrive from completely different traditions and meet, for commercial purposes, in about 1988.

A dated timeline
YearWhoWhat
1822Joseph FourierThéorie analytique de la chaleur publishes the decomposition first submitted, and rejected, in 1807: any reasonable function is a sum of sinusoids.
1843Georg OhmThe acoustic law: the ear analyses a complex tone into its component frequencies. Later heavily qualified, but the starting point for treating hearing as a filterbank.
1863Hermann von HelmholtzOn the Sensations of Tone proposes resonance in the cochlea as the mechanism of that analysis.
1928Harry NyquistEstablishes the sampling rate needed to carry a band-limited signal over a telegraph channel.
1933Fletcher & MunsonThe first equal-loudness contours, at Bell Labs — the ancestor of ISO 226.
1940Harvey FletcherNames the critical band, from masking experiments with band-limited noise.
1948Claude ShannonA Mathematical Theory of Communication: entropy, the source coding theorem, and the reconstruction formula that makes sampling exact.
1952David HuffmanAs a graduate student, derives the optimal prefix code — reportedly while trying to avoid sitting the exam.
1960Georg von BékésyExperiments in Hearing collects three decades of travelling-wave measurements on the basilar membrane; Nobel Prize in 1961.
1961Eberhard ZwickerThe Bark scale: 24 critical bands with published edges, still embedded in MPEG's psychoacoustic models.
1972ITU-T (CCITT)G.711 standardises µ-law and A-law companding: 8-bit samples with the dynamic range of about 13 bits.
1974Ahmed, Natarajan & RaoThe discrete cosine transform — the ancestor of every transform codec in this study.
1979Manfred SchroederPublishes a spreading function and an early perceptual coder at Bell Labs.
1980Sony & PhilipsThe Red Book fixes 44.1 kHz and 16 bits, numbers the world is still living with.
1986–87Princen & BradleyTime-domain alias cancellation and the lapped transform that becomes the MDCT: critically sampled, overlapping, and perfectly reconstructing.
1988James D. JohnstonPerceptual entropy: an estimate, in bits per sample, of how much of a signal is actually audible. The number that made the business case.
1988ISO/IECMPEG is founded; the audio subgroup calls for proposals.
1990Glasberg & MooreThe ERB formulation that supersedes the Bark scale in most of the hearing-science literature.
1992–93ISO/IEC 11172-3MPEG-1 Audio, with Layers I, II and III. Layer III merges the ASPEC and MUSICAM proposals; Karlheinz Brandenburg and the Fraunhofer IIS group at Erlangen are the names most attached to it.
1995Fraunhofer IISThe .mp3 extension is chosen for Layer III files, reportedly on 14 July.
1997ISO/IEC 13818-7MPEG-2 AAC: a clean 1024-line MDCT, temporal noise shaping and switchable window shapes.
1999–2000Xiph.OrgVorbis, an unencumbered transform codec, in reaction to MP3 licensing.
2001Josh CoalsonFLAC: linear prediction plus Rice coding, with a format frozen in 2007 and finally written up as RFC 9639 in 2024.
2003Coding TechnologiesSpectral band replication is standardised into HE-AAC: describe the top octaves instead of coding them.
2012IETFRFC 6716 defines Opus — Skype's SILK and Xiph's CELT in one royalty-free bitstream, 6 to 510 kbit/s, and mandatory to implement in WebRTC.
2012ISO/IECMPEG-D USAC (xHE-AAC): speech and music coding unified in one decoder, switching between ACELP and MDCT paths.
2020sGoogle, Meta and othersLearned codecs — Lyra, SoundStream, EnCodec, DAC — reach intelligible speech at a few kilobits per second using residual vector quantisation and adversarial training.

The pattern is worth noticing. Every major advance came from taking something that had been measured about hearing and finding the arithmetic that exploits it — and each time, the arithmetic had been sitting in the literature for a decade or more, waiting for the computing power to make it cheap. That is still the shape of the field: chapter 12 argues that the learned codecs are the same story again, with the measurement replaced by a training set.
