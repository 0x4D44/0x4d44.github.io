The format that won is a compromise with a filterbank it was not allowed to replace.

Layer III was standardised alongside two simpler layers and made to share their front end, so that a 1992 decoder chip could serve all three. Almost every strange thing about an MP3 file follows from that one decision: 576 spectral lines rather than a round number, eight butterflies that partly undo the stage before them, a granule of 13 milliseconds, and a pointer that runs backwards past the start of its own frame. It is the last audio format most people can still read by hand, and it repays being read.

What Layer III inherited

MP3 is one of three layers that had to share a syntax, and it is the one that lost the argument about the front end.

By 1989 two camps had working perceptual audio coders and no agreement. MUSICAM, from Philips, CCETT and the IRT, was built on a 32-band polyphase filterbank: cheap, robust, easy to decode, and — with only 32 bands across the spectrum — too coarse to place quantisation noise precisely under a masking threshold. ASPEC, from Fraunhofer's Erlangen institute with AT&T, Thomson and the University of Hannover, was a transform coder: far better frequency resolution, far more arithmetic, and descended from Karlheinz Brandenburg's doctoral work on optimum coding in the frequency domain.

MPEG's resolution was not to pick one. It defined a family of three layers sharing a single frame syntax, with Layers I and II essentially MUSICAM at two levels of ambition, and Layer III built on ASPEC's ideas — but required to run those ideas through MUSICAM's filterbank, so that the three layers could share silicon and a decoder could step down gracefully. ISO/IEC 11172-3 was technically frozen at the end of 1992 and published in August 1993. The layer that nobody expected to matter outside broadcast contribution was the one that would be given a three-letter extension on 14 July 1995, replacing the .bit that Fraunhofer's own tools had been writing until then.

The test material has become part of the folklore, and the technical reason for it is sound. Brandenburg's group used Suzanne Vega's Tom's Diner — the 1987 recording, unaccompanied voice, no instruments at all. An a cappella voice is the worst case for a masking-driven coder because there is nothing to mask with: every decibel of quantisation noise the encoder adds is sitting in clear air next to a single, highly tonal source. Anything the model gets wrong is audible immediately. The group reportedly worked on that same passage for two years.

A coder that hides its errors under the music is best tested on music that has nowhere to hide them. Why the reference track was unaccompanied

The layers did not compete so much as separate. Layer II — MUSICAM, essentially unchanged — became the workhorse of European digital broadcasting: it is the original DAB audio codec and is still carried in DVB and in studio contribution links, where its simplicity and its tolerance of repeated decode/re-encode cycles matter more than its efficiency. Layer III went to the consumer, where a 10:1 ratio against CD was the difference between a song that downloaded overnight and one that did not. Layer I effectively went nowhere.

The patents ran their course. Technicolor and Fraunhofer's joint mp3 licensing programme was terminated on 23 April 2017, once the last patents in the pool had expired, which is the event that a good deal of press coverage reported as "MP3 is dead". Nothing about the format changed that day except that anyone could ship an encoder for it without a licence.

Two transforms in series

576 lines is 32 × 18, and the 32 is the part Layer III could not choose.

The encoder's front end is MUSICAM's: a polyphase quadrature-mirror filterbank that splits the input into 32 equal-width subbands using a 512-tap prototype filter, each subband decimated by 32. At 44.1 kHz each subband is 689.06 Hz wide — a full critical band or more, everywhere below about 4 kHz, which is far too coarse to shape noise against the threshold curve of Chapter 03.

Layer III's answer was to put a second transform behind the first. Each subband's decimated stream is cut into blocks of 18 and passed through an MDCT with a 36-sample window and 50 per cent overlap, producing 18 coefficients per subband. That gives

32×18 =576 spectral lines per granule, from 576 new input samples — 38.28 Hz apart at 44.1 kHz, against 689.06 Hz for the polyphase stage alone

An eighteen-fold improvement in frequency resolution, bought with a second transform roughly an order of magnitude cheaper than the filterbank in front of it, and without touching that filterbank at all — so the other two layers were undisturbed. It also bought a problem. A polyphase filterbank of this kind is only nearly perfectly reconstructing: adjacent bands overlap heavily, so a tone sitting near a band boundary appears in two subbands at once, and the copy in the wrong subband is a mirror image, folded about the boundary by the decimation. In MUSICAM that aliasing is harmless, because the synthesis filterbank cancels it exactly. In Layer III it is not harmless, because the MDCT and the quantiser sit in between, and a quantiser that damages one copy destroys the cancellation.

Layer III's fix is a set of alias-reduction butterflies: eight two-by-two rotations applied across every one of the 31 subband boundaries, each pairing line 17−i of the lower subband with line i of the upper one. The coefficients collapse fast: the first butterfly turns through 31 degrees and the eighth through a fifth of one. That tells you how localised the aliasing is, because each butterfly touches exactly one pair of lines — the correction is confined to three or four lines either side of each boundary — 115 to 150 Hz at 44.1 kHz — and that it reaches no further into the spectrum than that.

The figure below builds the whole chain and measures it. It designs a 512-tap prototype whose squared magnitude falls as a raised cosine across the band edge — the power-complementary condition that makes a cosine-modulated filterbank cancel its own aliasing — modulates it into subband filters, runs a sinusoid through the polyphase stage at 170 frequencies, takes the 18-point MDCT of the result, and reads off the magnitude at one line you choose. What comes back is the composite frequency response of a single MP3 spectral line: the thing the quantiser is actually operating on, main lobe, skirts, alias image and all.

Notice the width of that main lobe. The nominal line spacing is 38.28 Hz, but the response of a single line is several times wider than that and its skirts run for hundreds of hertz. Two stages in series do not multiply their resolutions; they convolve their impulse responses. And the impulse-response length is the number that matters for transients:

512+ (36−1)×32 =1632 samples 37.0 ms at 44.1 kHz — the span of input a single long-block MP3 coefficient sees, against the 1152 samples (26.12 ms) the frame nominally advances
The number that follows you into Chapter 08

MP3's short blocks replace the 18-point MDCT with three 6-point ones, cutting the MDCT window from 36 subband samples to 12. But the 512-tap polyphase filter is still in front, so the composite span only falls from 1632 samples to 512 + 11 × 32 = 864, or 19.6 ms. Short blocks halve MP3's temporal smear; they do not divide it by three, and they cannot get below the prototype filter's own length. AAC, which threw the polyphase stage away entirely, gets a 256-sample short window — about 5.3 ms at 48 kHz. That single comparison is most of the case for the second generation.

The frame, field by field

Four bytes of header describe the transport and say nothing whatever about the audio.

An MPEG-1 Layer III frame carries 1152 PCM samples per channel — two granules of 576 — and begins with a 32-bit header. The header is shared with Layers I and II and was designed for a broadcast bitstream that a receiver might join halfway through, so it is repeated every frame, it starts with a run of ones you can hunt for, and every field in it is about how to find and size the frame rather than what is in it.

Frame length is arithmetic, not a field. A frame carries a fixed number of samples, so at a fixed bitrate its length in bytes is fixed too — except that the division rarely comes out whole, which is what the padding bit is for:

At 128 kbit/s and 44.1 kHz that is 417.959 bytes, so frames alternate between 417 and 418 with the padding bit set about 96 per cent of the time. That last column is the reason low-bitrate MP3 is so poor. The header and side information are a fixed 36 bytes for a stereo MPEG-1 frame whatever the bitrate, so at 32 kbit/s more than a third of the file is bookkeeping before a single spectral coefficient is written. MPEG-2's low-sampling-frequency extension exists aimed squarely at that: it halves the frame to 576 samples and cuts the side information from 32 bytes to 17 for a stereo stream.

The CRC protects the wrong half

When the protection bit is clear, a 16-bit CRC follows the header, computed with the generator x16+x15+x2+1. It covers the last two bytes of the header and the side information — not the main data, which is the overwhelming majority of the frame. That is a deliberate broadcast-era choice: a corrupt spectral coefficient makes a click, but a corrupt part2_3_length desynchronises the bit-reader and destroys everything after it. Almost nothing in the wild sets the bit; the two bytes were not judged worth their cost.

Where the decisions live

The side information is a fixed 17 or 32 bytes, and it is the encoder's entire confession.

Immediately after the header — and, unlike the main data, exactly where you expect it — sits a block of side information whose length depends only on the version and the channel count: 17 bytes for MPEG-1 mono, 32 for MPEG-1 stereo, 9 and 17 respectively for MPEG-2 and 2.5. It is fixed-length by design, because the decoder has to read it before it knows where anything else is, and because the two branches of the one conditional in it were deliberately balanced to the same 22 bits.

Put those together and the decoder's requantisation is a single expression, the one derived in Chapter 05 and implemented in this site's library as DSP.dequantPow:

xr= sgn(is)·|is|4/3 ·2(global_gain−210)/4 ·2−m(scalefac+preflag·pretab) m = ½ when scalefac_scale is 0, 1 when it is 1. The 4/3 exponent is the inverse of the encoder's ¾ power law; the 210 is an offset chosen so global_gain's eight bits span the useful range.

Everything a decoder needs is in that expression and the tables it indexes. Note what is not there: no masking threshold, no tonality estimate, no window-switching decision logic, no rate loop. The standard normatively specifies the bitstream and the decoder. The encoder is left entirely free, and that freedom is the single most consequential thing about the format.

The backwards pointer

An MP3 frame usually does not contain its own audio.

A constant-bitrate channel gives every frame the same number of bytes. The music does not cooperate: at a constant perceptual quality the bit demand of successive granules varies by a factor of two or three, and a castanet strike can want five times what the silence before it wanted. Layers I and II simply accepted the mismatch and coded each frame to fit. Layer III did not.

The mechanism is main_data_begin: nine bits, at the very front of the side information, giving a negative byte offset from the start of this frame's sync word to the place where this frame's main data actually begins. Nine bits caps it at 511 bytes, or 4088 bits, and that cap is the size of the bit reservoir. An easy frame spends less than its allowance and leaves the remainder in the channel; a hard frame reaches backwards into that remainder and starts its main data inside its predecessors' byte ranges.

0≤ main_data_begin≤511 bytes =4088 bits at 128 kbit/s and 44.1 kHz that is 1.22 frames' worth of allowance held in hand — enough to absorb one bad granule, not a bar of music

The consequence is that the header and side information are laid out at fixed frame positions while the main data is a separate, contiguous stream threaded through the gaps between them. An MP3 file is two interleaved streams, not a sequence of packets. Cut one at a frame boundary and the first frames of the result will refer to bytes that are no longer there; a well-behaved decoder emits silence until the pointer chain becomes consistent again. It is also why the IETF had to define a loss-tolerant RTP payload format specifically for MP3, and why frame-accurate seeking in a variable-bitrate file needs either an index or a scan of the whole thing.

The reservoir is also the reason Layer III can run in variable-bitrate mode at all without a format change. A VBR encoder simply writes a different bitrate index in each frame; the reservoir machinery is already there to smooth what remains. Nothing in the 1993 standard anticipated VBR as a product feature, and nothing in it forbade it.

One blunt defence

Block switching is all MP3 has against a transient, and it arrives two granules late.

Quantisation noise added to an MDCT coefficient is spread uniformly across that coefficient's whole time support. When the support is 1632 samples and the signal inside it is 30 ms of silence followed by a castanet, the noise fills the silence too. That is pre-echo: a smear of hiss arriving before the attack that caused it, and the ear is far more sensitive to noise preceding a transient than following one, because backward masking lasts only a few milliseconds while forward masking lasts hundreds.

Layer III's remedy is the four block types encoded in block_type. A normal long block is the default. When the encoder's psychoacoustic model predicts an attack, it must first emit a start block — a long block with an asymmetric window whose right half is shortened — then one short block granule containing three 6-point MDCTs per subband, then a stop block to get back to long windows. Three granules of machinery for one transient, and the decision must be made a granule in advance, because the window shapes have to meet.

3× (32×6) =576 a short-block granule is three windows of 192 lines each rather than one window of 576 — the same coefficient count, traded from frequency resolution into time resolution

There is a further concession, mixed_block_flag, which keeps the two lowest subbands long while the other thirty go short. Transients are broadband but their perceptual damage is at high frequencies, and low-frequency lines need the resolution. It is a reasonable compromise and it is also the sort of thing a format accumulates when it cannot change its filterbank.

What MP3 does not have is window shape switching. Every long block uses the same sine window; there is no option to swap in a more selective shape for tonal passages, as AAC does with its Kaiser–Bessel-derived alternative. Nor is there temporal noise shaping, which attacks the same problem by filtering along the frequency axis instead of shortening the window. Both arrive in Chapter 08.

Two kinds of joint stereo

One of them is an exact rotation and the other throws your stereo image away.

Setting mode to 01 turns on joint stereo, and mode_extension's two bits then enable two quite different tools, which may be used together or separately. Conflating them causes more confusion about MP3 than any other single thing.

Mid/side replaces the left and right channels with their sum and difference, normalised so the transform is orthonormal:

M= L+R√2 , S= L−R√2 ⇒ M2+S2 =L2+R2 a 45° rotation of the channel pair: energy-preserving, perfectly invertible, and by itself completely lossless

Nothing is discarded. The gain is statistical: on most recordings the two channels are strongly correlated, so S is much quieter than M, and a quiet signal quantised to the same absolute noise floor costs far fewer bits. When the channels are uncorrelated, M and S cost exactly what L and R would, which is why the flag is per-granule and why encoders switch it off on wide material. The one real hazard is that noise introduced in M and S reappears in L and R spread across both channels, so a mono-summed listener hears the side channel's noise doubled; that is a masking question, not a loss of information.

Intensity stereo is the destructive one. Above a chosen scalefactor band, the side information stops carrying two channels at all: it carries one, plus a per-band position, coded into the right channel's otherwise unused scalefactor field. The decoder reconstructs both channels as scaled copies of the single transmitted one. Per-band energy in each ear is preserved; the phase and time relationship between the ears — which is what localises a source below about 1.5 kHz and what makes reverberation sound like a room — is destroyed outright. It nearly halves the cost of the affected bands, and it is why a certain generation of 96 kbit/s files sound like the band is playing inside your skull.

What modern encoders actually do

LAME has defaulted to joint stereo for years, but its joint stereo is mid/side only — it decides per granule whether M/S or L/R costs fewer bits and writes the flag accordingly. Intensity stereo is in the format and LAME does not use it. So the folk advice to "force full stereo for quality" is advice to switch off a lossless, usually beneficial transform on the strength of a reputation earned by a different tool entirely.

What people get wrong

Six confident claims about MP3 that the bitstream contradicts.

"MP3 cuts everything above 16 kHz"
The format does not. A Layer III bitstream can carry non-zero coefficients up right up to the Nyquist limit — 22.05 kHz at 44.1 kHz. The lowpass is an encoder policy: spending bits above 16 kHz at 128 kbit/s is usually a bad trade, so encoders zero those lines and let the zero-run coding make them nearly free. It appears in the file as a run of zeros, not as a field. Raise the bitrate or the VBR quality and the same encoder passes 19 kHz and beyond.
"Joint stereo is lossy, so turn it off"
Mid/side is an orthonormal rotation and loses nothing — Fig 7.5 measures the round-trip error and finds it at the floating-point floor. Intensity stereo is the destructive tool, it is a separate bit in the same field, and current encoders do not enable it. Turning joint stereo off usually costs quality at a fixed bitrate.
"Every frame is independent, so you can cut on a frame boundary"
The bit reservoir makes main data a separate stream from the frame headers. A frame's main_data_begin can point up to 511 bytes into its predecessors. Cut there and the first frames of the fragment reference bytes that no longer exist. Editors that do this properly re-write the pointers.
"320 kbit/s constant is always better than a high VBR setting"
LAME's own documentation states that nobody has produced ABX results showing −b 320 to be audibly better than its top VBR profiles, which average roughly 245 kbit/s at −V0 and 190 at −V2. A constant bitrate spends the same bits on a cymbal crash and a held organ note; VBR does not. See Chapter 11 for how to run that test on yourself properly.
"Re-encoding at a higher bitrate improves the file"
Every zeroed line stays zero and every quantisation error becomes signal that the second encoder now has to spend bits describing. Generational loss in a transform codec is not symmetrical with generational gain, because there is no such thing.
"MP3 died in 2017"
What ended on 23 April 2017 was a patent licensing programme, because the patents had expired. The format is unchanged, every decoder still decodes it, and it became more available that day, not less. What is true is that the last substantive change to the format was in the mid-1990s, and every improvement since has been in encoders.
The encoder kept moving

The standard froze the decoder in 1993, and that is why the format got better for twenty years.

Because ISO/IEC 11172-3 specifies only what a compliant bitstream is and how to decode it, every psychoacoustic decision — where the threshold is, when to switch blocks, how to run the rate loop, which Huffman region split to choose, whether to enable M/S this granule — is unconstrained. Two encoders can produce wildly different files that both decode correctly on the same 1994 hardware.

A standard that specifies only the decoder is a standard its own users are allowed to keep beating for twenty years. Why a 2017 encoder outruns a 1997 one at the same bitrate

LAME is the clearest case. Begun in 1998 as a patch set against the ISO reference source and eventually a complete rewrite, it shipped its own psychoacoustic model, its own absolute-threshold curves, a much better block-switching decision and, crucially, a quality-based variable-bitrate mode. Its presets are stated as qualities rather than rates — −V0 averages around 245 kbit/s, −V2 around 190, with −−preset insane as a plain 320 kbit/s constant rate — and its documentation's position is that anything from −V0 to −V3 is normally transparent. None of that required a single change to the format.

Two pieces of metadata had to be bolted on from outside, because the format has no place for them. The first is the Xing/Info frame: a complete but silent first frame carrying the total frame count, the byte count, a 100-entry seek table and a quality figure, written as Xing for variable-rate files and Info for constant-rate ones (containers, tags and the rest of that apparatus are Chapter 12's subject). Without it a player cannot report the duration of a VBR file without scanning it; Fraunhofer's equivalent is called VBRI. The second lives in the same frame: LAME's extension fields recording the encoder delay and padding. An MP3 cannot have a length that is not a multiple of 1152 samples, and the filterbank adds its own latency at both ends — conventionally 576 samples of encoder delay plus 529 samples that the decoder's own filterbank contributes. Gapless playback is only possible if those two counts are written down and the player trims them, which is why the same album rips gaplessly in one program and clicks between tracks in another.

What MP3 could not fix from the encoder side is everything structural: the fixed 576 lines, the polyphase stage and its 864-sample floor on temporal resolution, the single window shape, the absence of any tool for shaping noise in time, and a stereo toolbox with nothing between "code both channels" and "throw one away". Each of those is a named feature of the format that replaced it — and that format had the luxury of starting from an empty page in 1997, with four years of listening tests on MP3 to learn from. Chapter 08 takes it apart, and the first thing it does is delete the filterbank this chapter has spent nine sections explaining.
