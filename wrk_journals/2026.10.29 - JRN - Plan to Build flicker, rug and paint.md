# Plan to Build: the wall flicker, the rug, and painted interior walls

The user reported three things on the live film and asked about a fourth: the block walls flicker, the interior walls stay as bare block, the living-room rug is drawn over the sofa and the coffee table on the plan, and could the film end with a camera tour through the front door? The first three are fixed in `plan-to-build/index.html` (two commits on the task branch: `bb68322`, then `003b75a` for what an independent review found). The tour is not built: it needs a design go (see the end).

**The flicker had three causes**, found with a supersampled error map, 1:1 crops and a scan of coplanar box faces (all headless, lowest priority):
- A column's outer faces lay exactly in the plane of the wall faces they are flush with, so the two surfaces z-fought. The scan found 18 same-side coplanar overlapping face pairs among block, insulation and concrete (up to 1.12 m² each). Columns are now 2 mm bigger in plan (`pad`) and, while the section plane cuts, 1 mm taller than the walls' cut tops (`lip`). The scan now finds 0, at S+30 (walls up), S+50 (section cut) and S+78 (cut lifted).
- The 1.3 m section cut was a clipping plane: its edge was hard-aliased and the back faces it exposed fought the faces behind. Block, insulation and concrete are now cut by making the box shorter (`growBox`, `dropBox`, from `CUTU`), so the cut face is a real, anti-aliased polygon; a top face at exactly the cut height is drawn flat in the poché colour. Only the thin window and door parts keep a clipping plane.
- `fwidth` for the block and insulation shaders came from a coordinate that jumps at each course, and the speckle noise faded out too late. Both fixed.
- Aliasing error against a 2x supersampled reference (1280 px, mean of max-channel error, share of pixels over 12 levels), old page against new at the same film phase: furniture phase with the section cut (old t=172, new t=181.07) 1.656 to 1.092, 3.31 % to 2.74 %; pre-paint cut (t=160) 1.194 to 1.150; walls going up (t=138.5, 150) 0.910 to 0.849 and 0.823 to 0.784. A copy of the new page with the two `paintWall` calls removed measures identically before the paint, so the paint stage adds nothing to earlier frames.

**The rug.** `sortElements` keyed the 2D draw order with `layer + kind * 0.01`; equal inputs compared unequal and creation order was lost. It is now an integer key with the creation index as the tie-break, and rugs get their own slot below the other furniture (the corridor rug is created last and would otherwise cover the console and shoe cabinet). The plan-order check's rug checks fail 4 of 5 on the old page (rug 157 over sofa 156, 0 outline pixels showing) and pass now (the full check is listed below). The cooker hood and wall units no longer hide the hob (dashed above-1.3 m outlines are see-through). The 2D film is identical to the old one up to t=68 and differs after that only in furniture regions.

**Plaster and paint.** A new chapter, 16 PLASTER & PAINT, 9 s after the floors, with a `MATERIALASSIGN` command and a line per room. Every wall and column face that looks into a room is plastered (a warm beige, no block courses), then painted from the floor up in the room's colour, with a wavy wet edge, a white skirting, and white lining on reveals and window boards. Colours: LIVING sage, KITCHEN butter, CORRIDOR terracotta, BED 1 powder blue, BATH aqua, BED 2 blush, STUDY denim. It is done in the block and concrete shaders from the room rectangles (`PAINT_GLSL`, `paintWall`), one progress value per room (`PAINTU`, from `T3.paintT`), so it stays a pure function of t. Faces outside the building rectangle, wall tops and undersides are left alone. Everything after it moved 9.0 s later: the film is 200.93 s (12,056 frames at 60 fps), 19 chapters. The camera goes closer for the paint (visible height 6.4 to 7.2 m instead of 17) and follows the rooms; the old wide pull-back before the kitchen fixtures was dropped so the camera does not whip. The catalogue tagline names the new stage.

**Independent review.** A second model read the diff cold and found four real problems and one nit. All four were checked against the page; three are fixed in `003b75a`, one is logged.
1. Chairs drawn over their tables (confirmed, my regression). With the integer key a chair created after its table lay over the table's edge: the four kitchen chairs and the study chair are each tucked 25 mm under. Chairs now get their own slot below the other furniture, like rugs. The order check looks at the edge pixels of every overlap strip (the table's edge must run unbroken, the chair's front line must be hidden): 11 of its 20 checks fail on `bb68322`, all pass now.
2. Exterior reveals lined white (confirmed). `paintWall` lined every reveal, so the 55 mm of block beyond a window frame, which is seen from outside, was white. It now returns when the point in front of the face is more than 245 mm from the room, the frame's outer face. A probe over that reveal changed 82 % of its pixels when the paint went on (`bb68322`) and 0 % now; the inner reveal still changes 92 %.
3. Frames fighting the reveals (confirmed). A window or door frame as wide as its opening has side faces in the plane of the block reveals; the frame is double-sided (the section plane clips it), so through the cut its back face fought the wall. The coplanar scan had skipped `RoundedBoxGeometry`, which frames use: 104 coplanar pairs at S+30. Measured as the second temporal difference while the camera creeps 0.03 degrees a frame, 8 to 11 % of a 1:1 jamb crop shimmered (with the sun's shadow on or off, so not shadow acne) and 0.1 % does after `FRAME_CLEAR` (frames 2 mm a side narrower). No hairline gap shows in straight-on close-ups from outside or inside.
4. The camera whips 4.9 m from the living room to the kitchen in 0.45 s. Pre-existing: the live page does the same (t - S = 55.63 to 56.08 s; 64.63 to 65.08 s on this branch, after the 9 s shift). Not changed here; logged as `ALM-BUG-NMI-00001`.
Nits: the comment on the rug slot in `sortElements` said "just above the openings" and was wrong; the new comment in `003b75a` replaces it. Two boot-code lines (`get T()` and `get g3()`) are joined on one line; it parses and works, and it is left alone so the page the checks ran on stays the page that ships.

**Checked (all headless, lowest priority; final page, `003b75a`).**
- Player 32, export 69, catalogue 14, responsive (390 and 768 px) pass for this page. The chapter chips went from 18 to 19 on purpose, and chip 16 is now sampled.
- Review fixes: 63 of 64 passed in the full run and 27 of 28 in a re-run of the player section. The one failure both times was "no part of the 3D half costs more than 120 ms the first time it is drawn", at different spots each time (S+82.0 at 1745 ms, then S+7.0 at 261 ms), on a box at 100 % CPU (141 Chrome and 33 node processes on it) with these tests at idle priority. A third run of the player section, with the box at 75 to 100 %, passed 28 of 28 (slowest first draw 41 ms, at S+67.0). The other review sections passed in the full run.

**Export.** The whole film, through the real Render button with a stand-in for the save dialog, on the final page (`003b75a`): 12,056 frames in 943 s (12.8 fps on a box at 100 % CPU), 238.7 MiB (10.0 Mbps on average, 16 Mbps target), H.264 High, 2560 x 1440, 60 fps, yuv420p, 200.933 s. `ffprobe` counts 12,056 frames and a full decode raises no error. Twelve spot frames decoded by number (not by time, see the lessons) match `renderFrame` at t = i/60 at 32.6 to 43.3 dB, and each beats its two neighbours: 30 of 30 checks. Aliasing against a 3x supersampled reference at t = 163, 166.9 and 196 is unchanged from `bb68322`: mean error 1.498, 1.072 and 1.067 against 1.500, 1.075 and 1.067; share of pixels over 12 levels 3.26, 2.06 and 2.19 % against 3.28, 2.06 and 2.19 %.

**Not verified.** Any GPU or browser other than headless Chrome 155 on ANGLE/D3D11 on this box (the GLSL uses constant array constructors, dynamic indexing of arrays and `inout` swizzles: legal in GLSL ES 3.00, not run elsewhere). The colours and the camera were judged by eye from rendered frames. The review's 120 ms first-draw check depends on how busy the box is: it failed twice at 100 % CPU and passed once at 75 to 100 % (see Checked).

**Gotchas.**
- The page keeps drawing other frames in idle slices after `__ready` (shader warm-up). A test that seeks with `at(t)` in one call and reads state in the next sees another frame's values; seek and read in one evaluated function. `png(t, w)` is safe.
- The session's check scripts (`export-full.mjs`, `verify-full.mjs`, `resp1.mjs`) hard-coded the first task's worktree path; they now read `SITE`.
- Paint is only legible when the camera frames one or two rooms (6.4 to 7.2 m visible height); at 12 m or more the whole plan fits and the colour bands vanish.

**The tour (not built).** Yes, it is possible, and the painted walls were its prerequisite. It is a feature with new lighting and a new camera mode, so it needs a design go first. Proposal, with defaults:
- Shape: about 35 to 45 s added after the HOME orbit. The camera dives to the front door, tours, and the end title comes up over a view out of the study window. The export grows by about a fifth (about 14,500 frames, about 290 MiB at the same rate).
- Camera: eye height 1.6 m, field of view 70 degrees or more, starting outside the entry door D01 (east wall, y = 4.65 m, opening into the corridor). The door swings open and the path runs down the corridor and into each room by its door: D02 living, D03 kitchen, D04 bed 1, D05 bath, D06 bed 2, D07 study. P01 is a pass opening between the living room and the kitchen.
- It needs: the section cut lifted (it already is from S+76.8), ceilings (there are none), interior lights (the sun's shadow map leaves rooms dark: about seven unshadowed point lights, which recompiles the materials), a first-person mode in the camera model (mapped onto the existing target / az / el / H / lp spline, or a new key type), a smaller near plane with a tighter far plane, a hand-authored path that clears the furniture, optional room captions with areas, a new chapter chip, and the end title moved after the tour.
- Risks: the furniture is crude at 1.6 m; interior lighting quality; depth precision at a small near plane (and the 2 mm clearance beside window frames would show as hairlines at eye height, so it may need trim); the front-door and reveal details.
