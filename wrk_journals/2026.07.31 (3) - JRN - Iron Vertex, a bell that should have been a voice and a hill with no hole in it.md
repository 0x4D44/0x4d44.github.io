# 2026.07.31 (3) — Iron Vertex: a bell that should have been a voice, and a hill with no hole in it

Two reports from Arthur, both accurate, both my fault, and both instructive
about the difference between "the code does what I wrote" and "the thing
works".

## "The screams sound more like bling, bling, bling"

They did. I had built a bell and called it a person.

The recipe was detuned triangle and sawtooth oscillators, a clean sine
vibrato, a resonant peaking filter, and a fast exponential attack and
decay. Every one of those is a *chime* ingredient. Clean periodic tones
plus a ringing filter plus a struck envelope is how you synthesise a
tubular bell on purpose.

A voice is not a tone with a filter on it. It is a buzzy source — the
vocal folds — resonated by fixed peaks that the shape of the mouth puts
in the spectrum. Those peaks are the **formants**, and they are the thing
the ear reads as "somebody shouting" rather than "something ringing". An
open /a/, which is the vowel of a scream, sits at roughly 850, 1350 and
2800 Hz, so the source now runs through three bandpass filters in
**parallel** at those frequencies rather than one in series.

The rest is what stops formant synthesis sounding like a machine reciting
a vowel:

- **jitter** — the pitch wanders at random, driven by filtered noise. A
  periodic vibrato is precisely what made the old one a theremin.
- **rasp** — a soft-clip waveshaper, for a voice under strain.
- **breath** — noise through the same mouth.
- **contour** — a fast scoop up and a long sag, and an attack measured in
  tens of milliseconds rather than the 4 ms strike of a bell.

Three narrow bandpasses throw most of the source away, so there is a
make-up gain after the mouth; without it the riders came out *quieter*
than they went in.

I cannot hear any of this. What I can do is measure it, and a Goertzel
probe over the rendered output says the spectrum now has peaks at the
formant frequencies with troughs between them — the formant-to-trough
ratio runs 8–24x, where a bell would put a couple of narrow partials
anywhere but there. That ratio is now a browser assertion.

**On using a real sample**, which was the actual question: I did not, and
I would not without being asked twice. A scream recording is somebody's
work, and I have no way to establish the provenance or licence of
anything I could pull off the web. Dropping in a file Arthur owns or has
clearly licensed is a ten-minute job whenever he wants it.

## "We crash through the grass going into the tunnel"

Exactly what was happening, and the description was better than my model
of it. My first guess was that the bore dipped below the terrain — the
site rule allowed track as little as 1 m up, and the bore has a 3.4 m
radius. Measured: only **one** site in a hundred was low enough for that.
Wrong cause.

The real one: **the hill had no hole in it.** I had drawn a tube through
a closed dome and called it a tunnel. The tube was hidden inside solid
geometry, and riding in put the camera straight through the hillside's
green skin, which is a very good description of crashing through grass.

There is no CSG here to subtract one solid from another, so the hole is
cut by hand: build the hill in world space, then throw away every triangle
whose centroid lies within the bore, and render what is left DoubleSide
so the inside face still draws. That went into `mesh.js` as `carveTube`,
which made it testable — and the test is the proof I could not get from
a screenshot: after carving, **zero** triangles remain inside the bore,
while more than half the solid survives, so it is a tunnel and not a
demolition.

Two things fell out of writing it:

- `IcosahedronGeometry` is **not indexed** — it comes out as loose
  triangles — so my first cut, which filtered the index buffer, threw
  `Cannot read properties of null`. The browser harness caught it because
  it fails on any page error; without that it would have been a silent
  black screen.
- three.js imports perfectly well under node. It only reaches for a
  document when you make a renderer or a canvas texture. So the whole
  geometry layer can now be unit-tested without a GPU, which is where
  `mesh.test.mjs` came from.

I also raised the minimum clearance for a tunnel site to 4.6 m so the
bore floor genuinely clears the grass, which costs three sites out of a
hundred and removes the one case where the first guess would have been
right after all.
