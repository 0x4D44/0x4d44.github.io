# 2026.08.02 — Iron Vertex: inside the car, and inside the hill

Five reports from Arthur, and four of them turned out to be two bugs
wearing different hats. Both bugs were the same shape: **two pieces of
code deriving the same geometry independently, and drifting.**

## "The side walls of the cars disappear and you can see inside them"

Looking up at the train from the chase or wing camera, the cars were
hollow — you could see the seats through the bodywork.

The cause was one line, and it had been wrong since the train was
written:

```js
scratch.lat.crossVectors(scratch.fwd, scratch.up);
matrix.makeBasis(scratch.lat, scratch.up, scratch.fwd);
```

`fwd × up` is a *left*-handed basis. Its determinant is −1, so a matrix
built on it is a reflection, and every triangle in the car came out wound
backwards. Back-face culling then drew the far inner wall of each box
instead of the near outer one — invisible from level with the train,
obvious from below.

The fix is `up × fwd`. The same mistake was in `sweptTube`, which builds
the rails, the spine and the tunnel bore on `tangent × up`: those were all
lit inside out too, and had been all along. There the fix is the winding
rather than the frame (`a-d-c / a-c-b`), because the frame is also what
positions the vertices.

Worth stating plainly: **a mirrored basis is not a rotation, and
`makeBasis` will not warn you.** The geometry lands in the right place,
which is why this survived so long. Only the normals give it away.

## "I'm sitting behind the coaster, not in a car"

Exactly right, and the code said so. The back-seat camera was placed
1.35m *behind* the last car's reference point — outside the bodywork —
and then the car it was nominally in was hidden so it would not fill the
lens. Hiding the car is what removed the last cue that you were in one.

Now the camera sits where a rider's eyes are, in car-local metres
(`EYES` in `coaster.js`), resolved on the same frame the car mesh is
built on, in the middle of a seat row between the two riders. Nothing is
hidden any more: the car is the point.

That change surfaced a second thing worth recording. At the front row it
looked right immediately; at the back row a black slab covered the bottom
third of the frame. It was the row's own over-the-shoulder bar — 1.6m
wide, 0.8m in front of your face and only 24cm below eye level, so it
subtended nearly the whole frame width. Raising the rear eye by 31cm
drops it to a rail across the nose, which is what it looks like from the
seat. **The geometry was already correct; the framing was not.** Those
need different fixes and it is easy to go looking for the wrong one.

## "The chase camera should follow the cars into tunnels and the station"

The trailing cameras hung at a fixed 5.4m above the rails and 7.6m out to
the side. That is right in the open and fatal under a roof: the chase
camera did not follow the train into the tunnel, it flew into the
hillside above the portal, and the station shed swallowed it whole.

So the track now carries an **enclosure profile** — one float per sample,
0 in the open and 1 where there is a roof close overhead — and the
cameras collapse towards the centreline against it. It is grown by 20m
before it is smoothed by 7m, deliberately: smooth a hard edge and the
value at the portal itself comes back down, so the camera arrives
half-ducked, which is a collision rather than a near miss.

The profile and the two camera rigs live in `track.js`, not in the
renderer, so they can be checked without a GPU. The new test walks 120
seeds and asserts the camera fits inside the bore (diagonally, for the
wing camera, which is out *and* up) and under the shed roof at every
enclosed sample — and that it comes back out again in the open, because a
chase camera that stays tucked in is not a chase camera. Worst case
across the sweep is 1.3m of headroom to spare.

## "On some coasters we can still go through grassy banks"

Seed 3102803771. This is the one I had already fixed once, badly.

The hill over a tunnel is an ellipsoid with a hole cut through it. The
site picker checked that no *other* track was buried inside that hill —
but it exempted a window either side of the span, on the theory that the
approach legitimately runs along the hillside. It does, right up until it
curves back into the hill. On that seed four samples were buried one to
four samples past the portal: inside the exemption, inside the rock.

Measured on the reported seed:

```
track samples buried INSIDE the hill: 4
their distance along-track from the span ends (samples): 1, 2, 3, 4
```

There is no exemption now. Track the hill would swallow is either
**absorbed into the tunnel** — the bore and the carved hole extend along
the whole absorbed run, so the portals end up where the track really does
leave the hill — or the site is rejected outright.

That still left the drift that caused it. Three places derived the hill:
the site test, the renderer, and the unit test, each from `mid`/`radius`/
`top` by its own arithmetic. Now `tunnelSite` returns the ellipsoid
itself and the other two consume it.

Including its lumps. The renderer displaces the hill's surface by up to
1.125× to stop it reading as a balloon, and the first version of the fix
padded the test ellipsoid by that factor to cover the bulges. That was
correct and useless: the margin swallowed the park, and usable tunnel
sites across 120 seeds fell from 65 to 20. So the displacement function
is exported (`hillBump`) and the test evaluates the surface *in the
direction of the query point* — the shape that actually gets built, not a
sphere around it. 52 of 120 seeds keep a tunnel, and nothing is inside
one.

## "It'd be nice to look around in wing, pov and chase"

Free look, in all three. `state.look` is a yaw/pitch pair applied after
`lookAt` as a rotation in the camera's own frame, so in POV it yaws about
the *car's* up and still works upside down in a loop. It recentres when
you change camera or seat, because carrying a 160° look into a new view
means arriving pointed at nothing.

## What to take from this

Both real bugs were two derivations of one shape, drifting. The tunnel
had three. The general rule is boring and keeps being right: **derive a
shape once and pass it around.** The corollary is the one I had to learn
twice here — if a test has to model something the renderer does, export
the function rather than approximating it, because an approximation that
is *safe* can be as bad as one that is wrong. A 1.125× fudge factor threw
away two thirds of the tunnels to catch a bulge that a shared function
locates exactly.
