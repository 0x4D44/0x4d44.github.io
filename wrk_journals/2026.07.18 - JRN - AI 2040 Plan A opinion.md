## Editorial judgement

Plan A's best contribution is procedural. It forces AI policy ideas to operate inside a concrete scenario and asks who an aligned system serves, not merely whether it is aligned. Its weakness is that a readable sequence can make a long chain of uncertain assumptions feel executable. The article therefore recommends treating the report as a requirements document and adversarial test rig, not a timetable.

The central tensions used in the piece are:

- verification coverage must improve while the scenario expands world AI compute 3,000-fold;
- research transparency distributes scrutiny but also helps covert projects;
- capital income replaces wages faster than the report explains how democratic leverage survives;
- a plan built around reversibility ends with an irreversible transfer beyond meaningful human review.
