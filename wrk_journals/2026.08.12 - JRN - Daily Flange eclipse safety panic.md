# Daily Flange eclipse safety panic

## Ask

More preposterous stories leaning into the current nonsense about eclipse
precautions: disposable cameras and phones exploding when pointed at the sun,
sea monsters rising during totality, and swimming pools draining to one end (or
onto the ceiling).

## Work

Eight stories appended to `news/articles.js` as one block, spread across the
paper rather than piled on a single desk:

- **Technology** — single-use cameras detonate at totality (thirty-eight of
  forty on the test rail); handsets "leave the hand", so an eclipse mode locks
  the camera and adds 340g of unexplained mass.
- **Maritime** — harbours told to expect an emergence from the depths and to
  look busy, because attention "is understood to be nourishing".
- **Health** — the alignment gradient empties a pool into the deep end; the
  hazard is not drowning but the walk down the exposed slope.
- **Science** — covered pools invert and hold their swimmers against the
  ceiling for the duration; foam matting taped to municipal roofs.
- **Weather** — a pool-gradient band added to the national outlook, with a
  fourth grey band for indoor pools labelled "up".
- **Voices** — a column arguing the precautions are timid, since nobody has
  addressed the kettle.
- **Letters** — the postbag on all of it.

Recurring names reused so the run sits inside existing continuity: Professor
Consuela Marsh and the Institute for Applied Darkness (from the dusk-shadows
story), Hester Fernsby on Weather, Gideon Marchbanks in Voices, P. Galloway of
Motherwell writing in again.

## Safety decision

The invented hazards are cameras and pools, not eyes — but two of these stories
mock "do not point a camera at the sun", which is advice that is genuinely
correct for a dull reason. Both camera stories and the postbag carry a real
notice: an unfiltered camera or phone aimed at the sun can be destroyed, lining
up the shot means looking at the sun, and eclipse glasses or a certified solar
filter are the actual answer. The pool and sea-monster stories mock a
non-existent hazard and take the standard satire notice.

## Artwork: vector, not generated

Arthur asked for heroes. No image generator was reachable from this session, so
the eight are **drawn vector art** rather than the generated painterly art the
rest of the paper uses: one Python file emits eight SVGs from a shared palette
(night sky, corona amber, pool blues, a common eclipse/head/star helper), each
rendered in headless Chromium at 2x and downsampled to a 1600x900 WebP.
Total 214 KB for all eight — the generated heroes run 60-140 KB each, so the set
is cheaper than one of them.

The source is committed at `news/images/src/eclipse-heroes.py`, which is a
departure: the generated art has no source file, and a future editor asked to
change one of these would otherwise be hand-editing a WebP. The file carries its
own re-export recipe.

Captions: these take the renderer's default "Artist's impression; file photo;
entirely made up.", which is accurate for a drawing. The validator asserts none
of them sets `imageCaption` — the reviews' real screenshots and the generated
art are captioned differently, and a drawing must not borrow either claim.

Two rendering notes worth keeping:

- **Render larger than the art and crop.** At exactly `--window-size=1600,900`
  the page clips the bottom ~90px of a 1600x900 image, which quietly ate the
  weather map's caption bar. Rendering at 1700x1080 and cropping to 3200x1800
  from the top-left fixes it. A default 8px body margin also shifts the image;
  the wrapper HTML zeroes it.
- **The renderer's category chip sits bottom-left of the hero.** The map's
  "POOL GRADIENT · TUESDAY" bar started there and collided with the WEATHER
  chip; the text is now right-aligned. Worth checking on any hero with content
  in that corner.

## Validation

- `node news/tests/validate-static.mjs` — passes at 1,133 articles (was 1,125).
  New coverage: exactly eight eclipse-tagged stories, the desk spread asserted
  exactly, six-paragraph minimum and a pull quote on each, the three
  safety-notice stories asserted to carry the eye-safety warning, and each story
  carrying its own `images/<id>.webp` (present on disk, alt text over 60 chars,
  no `imageCaption`).
- `npm run build` — all static validators pass.
- `node news/tests/browser.test.mjs` — passes.
- `node --check news/articles.js`, `node --check data.js`, `git diff --check`.
- Rendered three article pages at 1280px in headless Chromium: hero, body, pull
  quote, tags and the amber safety notice all correct. Reviewed a contact sheet
  of all eight heroes (three passes — the pool's water read as a level slab, the
  phones as blank white cards, and two scenes had props cropped at the frame).

## Notes

- Catalog copy in `data.js` moved 1,125 → 1,133 to stay in lockstep with the
  validator assertion.
- `assert.deepEqual` against an array built with `.map()` on the corpus fails
  even when the contents match — `articles` is loaded in a `vm` context, so the
  mapped array carries that realm's `Array.prototype`. Use
  `Array.from(list, fn)` to build the comparison array in the test's own realm.
