# 2026.07.31 (2) — Iron Vertex: launches, second lifts, tunnels, and lost property

A follow-up round on the back of the first. Arthur watched it and came
back with six things: the chase camera sometimes ends up under the track;
the passengers should wave; the odd easter egg where a wig or glasses fly
off; more screaming, at the right moments; some circuits with a double
lift and some with a magnetic launch; and some track that goes through
scenery, Disney-style.

## The chase camera was solving the wrong problem

It was placing itself by projecting **backwards along the train's
tangent** — `position - forward × 24m`. On a straight that is exactly
right, and everywhere else it is wrong, because by the time you have gone
24 m back the track has curved away from the tangent. On a climb the
tangent points up, so backwards points down, and the camera goes under the
ground; over the crest of a hill it goes under the rails.

The fix is to stop extrapolating and start *reading*: sample the
centreline itself at `s − trainLength − 10`, which is a real place the
train has just been, and lift it in **world** up (never the car's — on a
loop the car's up points at the ground and the camera would follow it
there). Then a floor at `groundHeight + 3.2` for anything left over, and a
spring so the sample-to-sample jitter does not reach the picture.

## Getting the energy in, two more ways

The generator had exactly one idea about where the energy comes from: a
chain lift at the start. That is now a list of `boosts` — spans where
something other than gravity is driving — and everything downstream
(roles, the design sweep, the live sim, the renderer) reads the list
rather than knowing about chains.

- **A second chain lift**, roughly halfway round, on circuits long enough
  to fit one. It re-tops the budget, so the hills after it are sized
  against *its* summit rather than a first drop that friction has been
  eating into ever since. ~20% of lifted circuits get one.
- **A magnetic launch**: no lift at all, a level launch track, and the
  train thrown out of the station at exactly the speed it needs to crest
  a top hat — `sqrt(2g·h·1.22 + 60)` — at a little over 1g, which is what
  a linear synchronous motor actually manages. About a third of circuits.

Both were straightforward once the boosts list existed. What was *not*
straightforward: the chain mesh collects every sample with the `lift`
role, and with two lifts that laced them together with a ribbon of chain
straight across the park. It now builds one strip per contiguous run.

The launched circuits also gave the tests something sharp to check: with
gravity giving back exactly what it takes, **the fastest the train ever
goes is the launch speed** — measured over a dozen launched seeds, the
peak anywhere on the circuit stays within 5% of it.

## Tunnels

The Disney trick is to put something solid in the way and run the track
through it. The site has to be found rather than chosen, so `tunnelSite()`
went into track.js beside `supportColumns` — a geometric decision, plain
data out, testable under node. It wants 46 m of free-running track that is
low, level and upright, and it wants the hill over it not to bury anything
else.

That last clause is where the first attempt found **zero sites out of
120**. The test was "no track sample within the hill's radius", and the
track leaving each portal is of course within the hill's radius — it is
the approach, running along the hillside exactly as it should. Measuring
the exclusion **along the track** instead, the way self-clearance does,
took it from 0 to 100 sites out of 120.

Visually the first pass put a 15 m grey sphere over each portal as "bare
rock" and it looked like a boulder had landed on the ride. A chunky stone
arch with a lintel, a scatter of small boulders, and a lumpy (rather than
spherical) hill reads far better.

## The people

Riders now wave — a roll of the shoulders rather than a raise, which is
what makes it read as waving rather than as more airtime — on the chain,
in the station, and any time the train is going slowly enough to be worth
waving from. Not everyone waves, and never quite together.

And once in a while, at the exact moment the floor drops away, somebody's
wig or somebody's glasses leave the train: thrown forward at the speed the
train was doing, tumbling, falling under gravity, and lying on the grass
for a while afterwards as evidence. The rider is visibly bare-headed until
the train gets back to the station. Rare on purpose — a 40-second lockout
and a low per-frame chance — so it is a thing you eventually notice rather
than a thing that happens every lap.

Verifying that one needed a trick: scale the item ×25, force the odds to
95%, take a burst of screenshots, and there it is, a ginger wig the size
of a car sitting on the grass beside the tunnel. Then put the numbers
back.

## Screaming

The old trigger was g-force only, which misses the single most screamed-at
moment on any coaster: the nose-down plunge at the top of a drop, where
the car is at a perfectly ordinary 1g. `rideMix()` now also takes the
pitch and whether the car is inverted, and the scream cooldown scales
down with how big the moment is, so a proper drop gets a wall of noise
rather than one polite shout every couple of seconds. A launch gets the
rising hum of the stators, and a shout of its own.
