# 2026.07.31 (4) — Iron Vertex: four things it was missing

Arthur said to add whatever I would like to see in it. So this is a
choice, not a spec, and the reasoning behind each pick is the interesting
part.

The temptation with an open brief is to add spectacle — night mode,
weather, a second coaster. I went the other way: the ride itself is
already the spectacle, and what it lacked were the things that make a
ride *legible* and *keepable*. Four gaps, in the order they annoy you.

## You could not keep a coaster you liked

The generator makes a fresh circuit from a random 32-bit seed and the
page has always accepted `?seed=`, but pressing NEW TRACK never wrote the
seed anywhere. So the best coaster you ever saw was gone the moment you
pressed the button again, and there was no way to send one to anybody.

Now every load pushes the seed into the address bar with
`history.replaceState`. It is three lines and it turns the thing from a
slot machine into something you can share. Of everything here this is the
smallest change and probably the most useful.

## You could not tell where you were

At 100 km/h from the front seat, one airtime hill looks like another and
you have no idea whether the next thing is the loop or the brake run.

So there is a plan of the circuit in the corner: a 2D canvas, drawn once
per track and re-blitted each frame with a dot on it. The line is drawn
in the circuit's own livery, thickened by height so the lift and the
hills read as the heavy strokes, and coloured by role — amber chain,
blue launch, orange brakes, white station. It is the cheapest possible
second view and it makes the ride readable.

## The back seat was the same as the front seat

It should not be. Over a crest the front car is already falling while the
back is still being dragged up over it, and the curvature under each is
different — which is precisely why enthusiasts queue longer for the back
row. The sim was computing the felt g at one reference point and using it
for everything.

`CoasterSim.feltAt(s)` now resolves the same accelerations at any arc
position, so the camera can sit in the back car and the g readout is
**that seat's** g. The test asserts the two seats disagree by more than
half a g somewhere, and — the part that would catch a sign error —
that each seat is the harder one *somewhere on the circuit*, because a
train where one row is always worse is not a train.

## A lap left no trace

Coasters are compared on numbers, and the number enthusiasts actually
argue about is airtime seconds — time spent light in the restraints. The
ride knew all of it frame by frame and threw it away.

`RideLog` keeps the current lap and hands back a summary when the train
crosses the station: time, airtime seconds, top speed, max and min g. It
lives in track.js rather than the page because it is arithmetic over the
ride state, which means node can check it — including that two laps of
the same circuit come out within two seconds of each other, which is a
nice incidental proof that the sim is stable.

## And one thing that is not a feature

`prefers-reduced-motion` is now honoured: no camera shake, no speed-FOV
kick. The ride still runs, it just stops moving the lens about. It costs
two conditionals and it is the difference between "a fun page" and "a fun
page I can look at".
