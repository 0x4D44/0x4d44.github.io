# AI 2027 review and Plan A comparison

## Aim

Read the complete AI Futures Project report *AI 2027*, review it in the established
Daily Flange long-form opinion style, then write a second article comparing it with
*AI 2040: Plan A*. Both pieces needed charts, explainers and real documentary
photographs rather than an all-synthetic visual package.

## Research

- Read all 71 pages of the official *AI 2027* PDF, including the race and slowdown
  endings and appendices A–W.
- Read the official compute, timelines, takeoff and security forecasts published
  with the scenario.
- Read the authors' 27 January 2026 clarification of modal years, medians and changed
  forecasts.
- Read the authors' 2 April 2026 Q1 timelines update. It moved Daniel Kokotajlo's
  automated-coder median to mid-2028 and Eli Lifland's to mid-2030, while describing
  events as moving at roughly 65% of the scenario's pace.
- Used the already researched and published Daily Flange treatment of *AI 2040:
  Plan A* as the comparison baseline.
- Consulted the independent *AI 2027 Tracker: One Year of Predictions vs Reality*
  for a clearly attributed external scorecard. Its categories are judgement calls,
  so the article treats them as a mixed indicator rather than ground truth.

The editorial conclusions were:

1. *AI 2027* works best as a fire drill about institutional latency, not as a
   calendar appointment.
2. Its strongest material concerns automated AI R&D, security lag, ambiguous
   alignment evidence, race dynamics and the internal–public capability gap.
3. Its weakest move is multiplying several uncertain exponential assumptions into
   one cinematic monthly sequence.
4. *AI 2027* and Plan A are not simply the same forecast shifted thirteen years.
   The first is a prediction/warning; the second is a recommendation.
5. The practical synthesis is to use *AI 2027* for observable tripwires and Plan A
   for pre-agreed institutional responses.
