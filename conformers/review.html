Notice how many of these are really the same number. The gauche interaction in butane, each 1,3-diaxial contact in methylcyclohexane, and half a methyl's A-value are all ≈3.8 kJ/mol, because they're all the same physical crowding seen from different angles.

2A method for any conformation question

Open chains

  1. Find the bond the question is about and draw a Newman projection along it.
  2. Put the biggest group on the front carbon at 12 o'clock.
  3. Turn the back carbon in 60° steps: staggered, eclipsed, staggered… For each pose, list every eclipsed pair and every gauche pair of large groups.
  4. Add up the costs from the table. Lowest total = most stable. Staggered poses are minima; eclipsed poses are maxima.

Cyclohexanes

  1. Draw a chair properly (parallel lines; axial vertical; equatorial parallel to the ring bonds once removed).
  2. Place the groups using up/down (wedge/dash) from the question. On each carbon, decide whether "up" is axial or equatorial — it alternates round the ring.
  3. Flip the ring. Every axial ↔ equatorial; up stays up. Draw the second chair.
  4. Cost each chair: sum the A-values of its axial groups (plus any gauche penalty between neighbouring groups). The cheaper chair is the major conformer.
  5. If asked for the ratio, use K = eΔG/RT.

3Glossary

ConformationAny 3D arrangement reachable by rotating about single bonds.
ConformerA conformation at an energy minimum (e.g. anti, gauche, chair).
ConfigurationAn arrangement that can only change by breaking bonds (cis/trans, R/S).
Dihedral (torsion) angleThe angle between two bonds on neighbouring atoms, seen along the bond joining them.
Staggered / eclipsedBack bonds between / behind the front bonds of a Newman projection.
Anti / gaucheTwo large groups 180° / 60° apart in a staggered conformation.
Torsional strainEnergy cost of eclipsed bonds.
Steric strainEnergy cost of groups crowding each other (van der Waals repulsion).
Angle (Baeyer) strainEnergy cost of bond angles bent away from ideal (109.5° for sp³ carbon).
Ring strainTotal extra energy of a ring compared with an unstrained chain; measured by heat of combustion.
Axial / equatorialBonds parallel to the ring's axis / pointing out round the ring's equator.
Ring flipChair-to-chair interconversion; swaps every axial and equatorial position.
1,3-Diaxial interactionSteric clash between an axial group and the axial groups two carbons away on the same face.
A-valueEnergy difference between axial and equatorial conformers for one substituent.

4Practice problems

Work each one on paper first — draw the Newman or the chair — then pick your answer.

Problem 1 · Newman projections

Looking along the C2–C3 bond of 2-methylbutane, (CH3)2CH–CH2CH3, what is the energy of the lowest staggered conformation, relative to a hypothetical strain-free one?

C2 carries two methyls and an H; C3 carries one methyl and two H's. However you stagger them, C3's methyl must sit between two groups on C2, and at least one of them is a methyl: one CH3/CH3 gauche interaction at best, 3.8 kJ/mol. Two of the three staggered poses manage that; the third wedges C3's methyl between both of C2's methyls (two gauche interactions, 7.6).

Problem 2 · Rotation barriers

Estimate the barrier to rotation about the C2–C3 bond of butane when going from anti to the eclipsed conformation at 120°.

At 120°, each methyl eclipses an H (2 × 6.0) and the remaining H's eclipse each other (4.0): 16 kJ/mol. 19 is the barrier at 0°, the fully eclipsed pose.

Problem 3 · Ring strain

Cyclopentane releases 3320 kJ/mol on combustion. Using 658.6 kJ/mol per unstrained CH2, its ring strain is about…

5 × 658.6 = 3293. 3320 − 3293 = 27 kJ/mol (the usual quoted figure is 26). "5" is the strain per CH2; "664" is the heat per CH2.

Problem 4 · Shapes of rings

Which statement about cyclobutane is correct?

Folding by ~25° pushes the angles down to ~88° (slightly worse) but staggers the C–H bonds a little (better). The trade is a small net win.

Problem 5 · The chair

On carbon 1 of a chair, the axial bond points up. On carbon 2, the axial bond points…

Axial directions alternate round the ring. So on carbon 2 the equatorial bond is the one on the top face.

Problem 6 · Equilibria

Isopropylcyclohexane has an A-value of 9.2 kJ/mol. The ratio of equatorial to axial conformers at 25 °C is closest to…

K = e9.2/2.48 = e3.7 ≈ 41.

Problem 7 · cis / trans

Which dimethylcyclohexane can place both methyl groups equatorial at the same time?

On C1 and C3, the "up" bonds are both axial or both equatorial (they're the same kind of carbon, two apart). So cis (both up) can be e,e. cis-1,2 and trans-1,3 are always a,e.

Problem 8 · Putting it together

In the major conformer of trans-1-tert-butyl-3-methylcyclohexane, the methyl group is…

trans-1,3 is always a,e — one group must be axial. Axial methyl costs 7.6 kJ/mol; axial t-butyl costs ~23. The ring sits with t-butyl equatorial and the methyl axial, with a preference of about 15 kJ/mol (over 99.7%).

Problem 9 · Conformation or configuration?

The two chair conformations of methylcyclohexane…

The ring-flip barrier is only ~45 kJ/mol. They're conformers of a single compound, in rapid equilibrium (about 95:5 in favour of equatorial methyl).

5Where next

Conformations come back again and again later in organic chemistry. A few places to look out for them:

  • E2 eliminations need the H and the leaving group anti-periplanar — a Newman projection question. In cyclohexanes that means both must be axial.
  • Sugars such as glucose are six-membered rings in chairs; β-glucose is the most common sugar in nature partly because every one of its large groups can be equatorial.
  • Steroids are fused cyclohexanes locked into rigid chairs, which is why their shapes, and their biology, are so precise.
  • NMR spectroscopy can measure dihedral angles directly: the coupling between two H's depends on the angle between them (the Karplus relationship).
