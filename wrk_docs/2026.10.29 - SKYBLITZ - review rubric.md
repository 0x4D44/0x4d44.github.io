# SKYBLITZ — review rubric

Used by the adversarial reviewers (and by the build agents to know how they will be judged).
Target: **every category ≥ 9.0 and the weighted total > 9.0.** Reviewers are hostile by design: their job is to find what is wrong, missing, ugly, broken or boring, and to prove it with evidence. A score above 9 must be *earned by evidence*, not granted for effort.

## How a review is run

1. Boot the game with the headless harness (`skyblitz/tests/tools/shot.mjs`, `chrome.mjs`, `window.__sky`; see the contract §8) on the lead's server. Play — do not just read code. Drive real key events, run full races (autopilot **and** manual input), look at screenshots *at full resolution with your own eyes* (open the PNG with the Read tool), measure numbers.
2. Cover at minimum: title → menu → aircraft select → course select → a full 3-lap race on **each of the 4 courses** (autopilot is acceptable for 2 of them; at least one must be a manual-input race), pause/resume, results, Grand Prix flow, Time Trial, settings, controls screen, 390×844 and 768×1024 and 1920×1080 layouts, `quality=low/medium/high`.
3. Every finding is a **ticket**: `severity` (blocker / major / minor / nit), `category`, `where` (file:line or screen/course/time), `evidence` (screenshot path, number, log line, repro steps), `fix hint`. No evidence → the ticket is discarded.
4. Score each category 0–10 using the anchors below. State the evidence for the score in two sentences. Do not average up; one blocker caps its category at 6.
5. Be calibrated: 5 = "a student project that works", 7 = "a good indie jam game", 8 = "a polished indie release", 9 = "a commercial arcade racer", 10 = "best in class". Do not give 9+ unless you tried hard to break it and could not.

## Categories, weights and anchors

### 1. Flight feel (weight 18)
* 0–4: floaty/car-like/spaceship-like, uncontrollable, jittery, or NaNs.
* 5–6: controllable but generic; turns feel like steering a car; no sense of energy or attitude.
* 7–8: banking turns, speed from dives, loops and barrel rolls work; camera decent; minor jitter or tuning issues.
* **9–10**: unmistakably a *plane*: bank-to-turn, pitch along the lift vector, energy exchange, believable stall/G, loops/rolls feel great and are readable, boost kick, the camera sells speed and attitude, input (keyboard/gamepad/touch) responsive and learnable in a minute; handling differs between craft.
* Must verify: manual 60 s test with key events; measured cruise/boost speeds; loop completes; barrel roll lateral displacement; no jitter in interpolated render (frame-to-frame position deltas smooth).

### 2. Visual quality and spectacle (weight 16)
* 0–4: placeholder/flat/untextured/unlit, obvious z-fighting or missing assets.
* 5–6: coherent but plain; programmer art.
* 7–8: pleasant lighting, decent models, particles; some courses weaker than others.
* **9–10**: each of the 4 courses looks like a different premium game (sky, lighting, fog, water/terrain, props), aircraft are hero-quality with livery, post-processing (bloom, grade, speed blur) tasteful, explosions/trails/boost FX spectacular and readable; ground shadows and altitude cues work; no visual glitches in any screenshot at any of 5 vantage points per course.
* Must verify: ≥ 5 screenshots per course incl. in-race chase view at full resolution; aircraft turntable; FX gallery; check low and high quality.

### 3. Course and level design (weight 12)
* 0–4: bland loop of rings, unreadable path, unfair corridors.
* 5–6: playable but repetitive; shortcuts trivial or broken.
* 7–8: varied sections, loop/arch, a shortcut, decent wayfinding.
* **9–10**: each course has identity and set pieces (loop, tunnel/arch, corkscrew, shortcut with risk/reward), rhythm (fast sweepers ↔ technical), item/boost placement that rewards skill, and wayfinding so clear a first-timer never wonders where to go at 400 km/h; shortcuts measurably faster but riskier; lap times 55–85 s; difficulty ramp across the 4 courses.
* Must verify: overview + fly-through screenshots, lap times from autopilot runs, shortcut time saving numbers, project() robustness.

### 4. Racing, AI and fairness (weight 14)
* 0–4: AI broken/idle/cheating visibly, races decided by the start, lap skipping, crashes.
* 5–6: AI follows a line; races are processions or rubber-band teleports.
* 7–8: AI competitive, uses items, some overtakes.
* **9–10**: *intelligent*: racing lines, corner management, item logic, barrel-roll evasion, shortcut choices by personality, mistakes by skill; close packs with real overtakes at every difficulty; rubber-banding invisible but effective; difficulty levels feel distinct; no exploits (shortcut lap-skip, wall-riding); position/lap logic flawless; rocket start; results/points correct.
* Must verify: ≥ 10 headless full-race sims (different seeds/courses/difficulties): finish times, overtake counts, DNFs, determinism; manual race as 8th-place start; exploit attempts.

### 5. Weapons, items and combat feel (weight 10)
* 0–4: items missing/not working, hits invisible.
* 5–6: items work but flat; hits unclear.
* 7–8: all items work with FX and sound; decent balance.
* **9–10**: all 10 items + cannon are fun, readable and fair; counterplay exists (barrel roll, shield, outrun, obstacles); lock-on/warning feedback is clear and escalating; hits are juicy but never frustrating (short tumble, invulnerability); item distribution comebacks; no stuck states.
* Must verify: `__sky.give(...)` every item and use it against AI; missile evasion; screenshot each effect.

### 6. Audio and music (weight 10)
* 0–4: silent, harsh, or broken; clipping.
* 5–6: some sounds; music loops badly or missing.
* 7–8: engine, SFX and music present and mixed OK.
* **9–10**: distinct original music per course (rendered with the ferrosintesis synth) with adaptive layers and seamless loops; procedural engine/wind/boost that respond to speed/G/boost and feel like aircraft; escalating warnings; positional AI engines; punchy UI sounds; clean mix with ducking, no clipping, volumes/mute work, silent under `?mute=1`, resumes on gesture, no leaks.
* Must verify: offline-render numeric checks (peaks/RMS/NaN), loop seams, decode every OGG, sizes in budget, event→sound coverage table, an AudioContext run in Chrome with no errors.

### 7. UI/UX and game flow (weight 12)
* 0–4: broken navigation, dead buttons, overflow, unreadable HUD.
* 5–6: functional but ugly or cluttered.
* 7–8: attractive menus and HUD, full flow works.
* **9–10**: cohesive "arcade broadcast" look; every screen finished and animated; HUD readable at a glance at 400 km/h; full mouse + keyboard + gamepad + touch; aircraft/course select with 3D turntable and track maps; GP flow with standings; settings persisted; pause/resume/retry/quit all clean; hints for first-timers; no control under the Almanac pill; no horizontal scroll at 390×844/768×1024; reduced-motion and reduce-flash respected.
* Must verify: walk every screen at 3 sizes; keyboard-only run; screenshot each; run `tests/responsive.test.mjs` for the slug.

### 8. Performance, robustness and polish (weight 8)
* 0–4: <30 fps, crashes, console errors, leaks.
* 5–6: 40–60 fps with hitches; occasional errors.
* 7–8: 60 fps on this machine at high; no errors.
* **9–10**: 60 fps at `medium` and `high` on the dev GPU with headroom (frame ms reported), auto quality governor works, load time < 6 s warm, no console errors/warnings across a 3-minute race on each course, no memory growth across 3 consecutive races (geometries/textures/listeners disposed), tab-hidden/resize/context handling, graceful asset-missing fallbacks.
* Must verify: `__sky.perf()` per course, `renderer.info` before/after repeated races, error list empty, resize storm.

## Weighted total
`total = Σ weight × score / 100` (weights sum to 100). The game ships when **total > 9.0 and no category < 8.5 and no open blocker/major ticket.** The lead runs reviewer rounds (3 reviewers, different lenses: *player/fun & flight*, *visual/audio/UI*, *engineering/robustness/AI/race logic*), triages tickets, assigns fixes to the owners, and re-reviews until it passes (max 6 rounds; if the cap is hit, the final report states the true scores).
