# King's Cross live model: scoring rubric and loop rules (DRAFT v1, not yet frozen)

Status: draft for Arthur's review. Once Arthur says "frozen", only Arthur changes it, and
every change is logged at the bottom with a date and a reason.

## How the loop works

1. The builder (lead) works through the design doc's phases.
2. After each chunk of work, an **independent scorer subagent** scores the project against
   this rubric. It gets the repo path, this file and an evidence pack. It does **not** get
   the builder's summary or opinion of how it went.
3. The builder fixes the lowest-scoring axes first and asks for a re-score.
4. The loop ends when **every axis scores above 9** (see "Scale"), or on a stop rule below.

## Scale

Each axis is 0-10 in half-points. "Above 9" is read as **9.5 or more** on every axis.
Arthur may relax this to 9.0 or more. A score is an integer or a .5.

- 10: nothing a careful reviewer could fault; every evidence item is met.
- 9: one minor flaw, or one evidence item is thin.
- 7-8: works, but a visible gap on at least one evidence item.
- 5-6: partly works; a core evidence item is missing.
- 0-4: not working, or unverified.

## Axes

Each axis lists what a 10 looks like and the evidence the scorer must check for itself.

### 1. Geometric fidelity
- 10: track layout, platforms 0-11 and their lengths, the throat and the tunnel mouths match
  OSM and survey references to within about 1 m on platforms and a few metres on the throat.
  Scale is true (a 26 m carriage is 26 m).
- Evidence: a script that prints platform lengths and track gauge from the model against
  OSM; top-down and side screenshots with a 10 m grid; the list of unresolved OSM issues.

### 2. Station look
- 10: the train shed arches, the western concourse, platform furniture and the surrounding
  buildings are recognisable as King's Cross from the standard viewpoints, with no obvious
  texture stretching, z-fighting or floating geometry.
- Evidence: screenshots from fixed cameras (listed in the evidence script), compared to
  reference descriptions.

### 3. Train models
- 10: Class 800/801 (and 802, 803 for Hull Trains and Lumo) built to published dimensions,
  correct car counts, procedural liveries, bogies, pantographs and windows; one coach looks
  right from three metres.
- Evidence: a dimension test (length, width, height per car) and close-up screenshots.

### 4. Live-data correctness
- 10: every berth seen in a recording is either anchored on the track graph or listed as
  unmapped with a reason; platform berth `1000+N` places a train at platform N; a replayed
  hour produces the same trains and positions as live did.
- Evidence: the replay report (anchored vs unmapped berths, step jumps, headcode churn).

### 5. Motion
- 10: trains never teleport, reverse or pass through each other; they accelerate and stop at
  the buffers, hold at platforms and leave in the right direction; a late or missing step is
  handled without a visible glitch.
- Evidence: a motion test over a recording (max jump, speed and acceleration limits) and a
  short capture sequence.

### 6. Signals
- 10: signal positions come from the reviewed overlay; aspects follow the live S-class bytes
  where they decode, and fall back to a clear "unknown" state where not.
- Evidence: the decoded-address table (decoded vs not), and a screenshot per state.

### 7. Robustness and security
- 10: the page works with no relay (replay), with a stopped relay, and with a relay that drops;
  the relay is loopback-only and no response, log or recording ever contains the login;
  the page credits OpenStreetMap; nothing in the repo holds a secret.
- Evidence: the relay test suite and its mutation run, a grep of the repo and of built
  output for the account name, the host-header refusal, and a with-and-without-relay run.

### 8. Performance and size
- 10: a steady 60 fps on Arthur's laptop (measure and report the machine), a bundle under an
  agreed budget (proposed: 2 MB gzipped, geometry included), and a first render within 3 s on
  a warm cache.
- Evidence: a headless Chrome measurement script with its numbers.

### 9. Engineering quality
- 10: `npm run test:kings-cross` green; every new test seen to fail once; typecheck clean;
  `tests/responsive.test.mjs` green for `kings-cross/` (no sideways scroll, nothing under
  the Almanac back pill); the catalog entry, tag, shelf, icon and `almanac-back.js` all done
  per CLAUDE.md; no new warnings.
- Evidence: the command outputs, which the scorer runs itself.

## Hard gates (cap every axis at 6 until fixed)

- The login, account name or any token appears in the repo, a response, a log or a recording.
- The relay answers non-loopback requests.
- A test was deleted, skipped or weakened to get green.

## Scorer rules

- Run the commands yourself. A claim in a document is not evidence.
- Be strict. Score what exists now, not what is planned.
- For each axis give: the score, the evidence you checked, the top three gaps, and what
  would raise it by a full point.
- Try to find a fault before you score. If you cannot find one, say what you tried.
- Do not negotiate. The builder may point at evidence but may not argue a score upward; a
  disagreement goes to Arthur.
- Use a fresh scorer for each round, so no round is anchored on the last. Ask for Opus-class
  judgement (per the fleet's model tiers).

## Stop rules

- **Success:** every axis above 9 in one round, then a second independent round confirms.
- **Stagnation:** two rounds with no axis improving by at least a point. Stop and report.
- **Cap:** 10 rounds. Stop and report.
- **Arthur's gates:** stop for Arthur after the first rendered station, after the first
  train, before any push or integrate, and before anything public (relay, licence).
