## 1. Verdict

1. **Not shippable yet.**
   - The weighted score is **7.91**, against a bar of 9.0.
   - Every category is below 8.5.
   - One major is open: **R2-E-01**. Auto quality, the default, does nothing below 10 fps (observed) and cannot see GPU load without the GPU timer. Inference: that covers Safari and Firefox.
2. **The round-1 fix wave landed.**
   - 30 of 32 round-1 tickets are FIXED-VERIFIED, including all 6 majors.
   - E-23 (pace and shortcut risk) is PARTIAL.
   - E-08 (missed-loop message) is NOT FIXED for the real cut. Its new banner only fires on a relock that a real cut never produces. My round-1 harness caused part of that: see R2-E-02.
   - **Race logic:** 24 headless three-lap races on the current balance had 0 projection glitches, 0 relocks and 0 respawns. Standings were ordered every time.
   - **Determinism:** hazards are now identical across render cadences on every course.
   - **Leaks:** GPU counters stay flat over 10 consecutive races.
   - **Frame cost:** 2.8–4.7 ms average and p95 ≤ 7.8 ms, at every preset on every course.
3. **The tests now bite where round 1 found them hollow.**
   - Every round-1 survivor except one is now killed (R1, R2, R3, I1–I4, C1, C2, F1, U1, V1–V6). The one that remains is the A1 comment-out, which is cosmetic.
   - New survivors cluster in four places:
     - Respawn bookkeeping: E01c, E01d.
     - Whole-run smoke tests posing as regression tests: R1-5 (K3), sim.test.
     - The keyboard guide: GD1, GD3, GD5.
     - The leak test's heap check: it misses typed-array memory entirely (L1, L2) and passes a 0.26 MB-per-race object leak (L4).
   - Also surviving: the missile intercept lead (X1).
4. **What the fixes cost.**
   - The shader warm-up adds 0–2.8 s to every race start, and it repeats every race.
   - Sunrise takes 5.6–9.9 s to start a race even without the warm-up.
   - On neon, five shader programs still compile after the warm-up. One of them, a depth program, costs a 127–132 ms frame 7 s into the race in 3 of 3 runs (R2-E-05).
   - The boot watchdog tells slow-network players "Skyblitz failed to load" while the game is still downloading normally.

## 5. The lead's questions

**(a) Tests resting on chaotic whole-run scenarios.** Four places.

1. **R1-5** (`race.test.mjs:641`) is the clearest case. With both roof-throw fixes removed (K3) it still passes, because redrock seed 2 no longer throws anyone on today's balance. The fixes are guarded by the unit tests R1-3 and R1-4 (K2 and K1 killed), not by R1-5.
2. **sim.test** killed none of the six whole-race-relevant mutants I gave it: E01a, E01c, E01d, X1, X2 and X3. Its tolerances are now tight (respawns ≤ 1, glitches ≤ 2), but no respawn happens in its races, so it cannot see respawn bugs. It is a smoke test.
3. **The E-08 test** is the opposite failure: a scripted, unrealistic scene. It stubs `course.project` to make a jump the real projection never makes (R2-E-02).
4. **The E-01 test** sets the good-mark by hand, so the automatic marking is unguarded (E01c and E01d, R2-E-07).

The well-built unit tests are E-01 (all branches at 20/50/80 %), E-02, E-07, E-09, R1-3, R1-4, the governor traces and the guide's mouth and loop tests. Mutating their targets fails them in 1–3 s.

**(b) Is leak.test's slope-based heap limit honest?**
- Mostly yes, as a backstop. The sharp checks are the GL counters (± 2 buffers, no texture growth) and the live three.js objects found by `queryObjects` (± max(25, 2 %)). Those caught both instanced-mesh leaks (E06a, E06c) in under a minute.
- The heap slope (< 0.3 MB per race over races 2–5, after two forced GCs) only matters for a leak that holds no three.js object.
  - L3 (0.64 MB of small objects kept per race): KILLED, "heap after GC grows 0.64 MB per race".
  - L4 (about 0.26 MB of small objects per race): SURVIVED, at a slope of 0.28 MB per race.
  - L1 and L2 (0.5 MB and 0.2 MB of `Float64Array` per race): SURVIVED, at slopes of about 0.06 MB per race. The heap number (`usedSize`) does not count ArrayBuffer backing stores, so a typed-array leak of any size is invisible to it.
- The limit sits between the measured warm-up drift and the round-1 leak (0.55 MB per race); the test comment documents that.
- Baseline slopes per course over races 2–5: redrock 0.043, sunrise 0.030, neon 0.074, frost 0.096 MB per race. The limit is 3× the worst of them.
- My neon ×10 probe shows the heap levels off after race 5 (20.5–22.7 MB for races 5–10, no trend). So a slope taken over races 2–5 measures the warm-up, which is where the 0.20 MB per race neon failure the world agent hit came from. Raising the limit to 0.3 was a widening, but not a dishonest one.
- What it does not cover: typed-array memory, object leaks under about 0.3 MB per race, and the High and Med presets (R2-E-12).

**(c) Is it acceptable that `X-old-lead` has no unit test?**
- No, but it is minor. Not even removing the lead entirely is caught: X1 survives race-items and the sims, while X2 (backwards) and X3 (5 s) are killed. The 7-point hit-rate difference (38.8 % against 31.6 %) is the lead's whole reason to exist, and only a 32-race harness outside the suite measures it.
- The items agent's argument holds: a constant-velocity target leaves the curving corridor, so outcome tests do not separate the two models.
- A white-box test does: assert the aim point equals target + velocity × `interceptT`, and check `interceptT` against the closed-form root (R2-E-09). It would kill X1, X2 and X3 in milliseconds.

**(d) The E-29 gate.** FIXED-VERIFIED (section 2).
- A plain player URL gets no `__sky`, no `console.error` wrapper and no error log. Its globals are only `__skyBoot` and `__THREE__`.
- Every documented test parameter turns the surface on. The log is capped at 200.
- E29 is killed by core E-29.
- Nits: `debug=0` and `quality=low` also turn it on (R2-E-13).
