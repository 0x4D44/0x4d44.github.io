## 1. Intent & scope

**One line:** A self-contained browser game for the almanac in which the player
is the Chief Engineer of a cruise ship — running the diesel-electric plant,
fuel & bunkering, emissions compliance, planned maintenance, and failures up to
full disasters — across six stepped levels from a 200-pax expedition ship to a
96 MW mega-liner, with in-game tutoring for new players.

**Goals:** see acceptance criteria (§10). **Non-goals (explicitly out):**
- No 3D, no canvas ship rendering — this is a panel/console game.
- No LNG/dual-fuel modelling, no CII/EEXI mechanics beyond a report-only CO₂
  stat on the finale, no dry-dock/class-survey gameplay, no crew personalities.
- No multiplayer, no server, no service worker / PWA / manifest (decision 2).
- No free-play sandbox in v1 (levels only; the finale is big enough).

## 2. Game design

### 2.1 Fiction

The player is the new Chief Engineer of the (fictional) **Boreal Line**,
promoted ship by ship. A mentor — **Staff Chief Engineer Maren Voss** — rides
along, speaking through the event log, mentor toasts, and the objectives
panel. Levels are voyages; completing one (arrive at final port, ship intact)
unlocks the next.

### 2.2 The six levels

| # | Ship | Pax | Plant | Hotel / prop @ svc / aux MW | Route (duration) | New systems taught |
|---|------|-----|-------|-----------------------------|------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | MV **Aurora** | 200 | 3× 2.2 MW DG, 1 board | 1.5 / 2.4 @ 13 kn / 0.2 | Norwegian fjords day cruise (~14 h) | Mimic, DG start/stop & breakers, load & spinning reserve (2 online = efficient but no reserve; 3 = N+1), alarm ACK, telegraph |
| 2 | MV **Baltica** | 700 | 3× 5.0 MW, 1 board | 3.0 / 6.5 @ 17 kn / 0.3 | Baltic overnight (~36 h) | Fuel tanks & burn, bunkering decision, hotel shed stages, maintenance tickets, **scripted blackout DRILL in port** |
| 3 | MS **Meridian** | 1,500 | 4× 8 MW, 1 board | 8 / 15 @ 20 kn / 0.5 | Med 3-port hop (~3 d) | Purifiers & cat fines, running-hours PMS, repair teams, ECA zone & MGO switchover (guided first), PMS auto-start toggle |
| 4 | MS **Atlantica** | 2,600 | 4× 12 MW, **2 boards + tie** | 9 / 25 @ 21.5 kn / 0.8 | Southampton→New York (~5 d, ECA both ends) | Weather & sea state, LO sumps & sloshing (Viking-Sky mechanic), N+1/board redundancy (guided tie ops), **scrubber**, schedule pressure |
| 5 | MS **Caribia** | 4,200 | 5× 14 MW, 2 boards | 16 / 30 @ 21 kn / 1.0 (+0.7 scrubber) | Caribbean loop (~5 d) | Hotel zoo (chillers, freshwater, sewage), full bunkering op w/ sampling, **engine-room fire procedure**, **PSC inspection (low-stakes first run)** |
| 6 | MS **Harmonia** | 6,800 | 4× 14.4 + 2× 19.2 MW (96 MW), 2 boards, 3 azipods | 20 / 54 @ 22 kn / 1.5 | Transatlantic repositioning (~6 d) | Everything + storm & rogue wave, SRtP fire (run on one board), PSC rerun; CO₂/voyage report stat |

Arithmetic closes by construction (X1): N−1 capacity ≥ hotel+prop+aux at
service speed on every level (checked: 4.4≥4.1, 10≥9.8, 24≥23.5, 36≥34.8,
56≥47.7, 76.8≥75.5 — L6 deliberately tight). Every level's content test
asserts (a) an N+1 lineup exists at service speed under nominal, weather
(+30% prop), scrubber, per-board, and post-SRtP configurations as applicable,
and (b) bunker capacity ≥ route burn × 1.2 (L6 ≈ 315 t/day at service speed —
pinned by unit test against the model's own constants). Voyage clocks are
compressed (§2.7); real session targets ≈ 8–20 min/level.

### 2.3 Core loop

Prepare (bunker/maintenance choices in port) → steady-state optimization at
time compression (lineup vs load, fuel economy, maintenance windows) → crisis
at 1× (alarm cascade, triage, procedure) → recover → debrief ("Chief's
Report": timeline, causal chains, stars). The design soul: **deferred
maintenance and thin margins author the player's own crises** — every casualty
traces to visible prior state, named in the debrief. Steady state has a live
needle: a **projected fuel margin at arrival** readout (burn vs plan), so
cruising at 8× is visibly earning/losing the Efficiency star.

### 2.4 Simulation model (engine)

Fixed-step simulation, **1 tick = 1 game-minute**, all state one serializable
plain object (including the PRNG cursor), all randomness through a seeded PRNG
(mulberry32), all inputs via `applyAction(state, action)`. `tick(state)`
advances one minute. **Quantization rule: nothing resolves in less than one
tick; every intervention window is ≥ 5 ticks.** Real-world sub-minute dynamics
(emergency DG 45 s, boiler purge) round to 1 tick.

- **Voyage & position (X2).** Route = legs `{fromPort, toPort, distanceNm,
  eca?, weatherBand, deadline?}` + port dwell ticks. Speeds are distinct:
  bridge-ordered (telegraph), player-commanded (`speed.set`, ≤ ordered), and
  **actual** = min(commanded, power-limited by available propulsion MW).
  `distanceIntoLegNm += actualKn / 60` per tick; leg completes at its
  distance; arrival vs deadline drives the schedule score;
  `bridge.requestSlowdown` lowers the ordered speed (schedule cost),
  `requestResume` restores it. Position triggers voyage-script rows and the
  drift-timer geometry (drifting near a scripted lee shore).
- **Electrical: board topology from day one.** `boards[]` (1 board L1–L3, 2
  from L4) each with member DGs, feeders, and a bus-tie breaker between.
  Feeder→board map in ship content. Per-board: demand, online capacity,
  reserve, overload/trip ladder, blackout. Tie closed = boards share as one
  bus; tie open = independent. **Protection semantics (X3):** a board FAULT
  (bus fault, ER fire consequence) first trips the tie open (1 tick), then
  kills the faulted board — the healthy board islands; a plain OVERLOAD
  collapse with the tie closed takes down the combined bus (the risk of
  running tied — a real tradeoff the manual states); breakers may close onto
  a dead bus; synchronization is abstracted (close = synced). The emergency
  DG feeds a separate **essential bus** (steering, lighting, controls, one
  bilge/fire pump) — never propulsion. Consumers: hotel feeders in **shed
  stages** (galley/laundry → HVAC → non-essential), propulsion feeders (split
  across boards), aux (constant per ship + thrusters while manoeuvring).
  `demand = hotel(t) + propulsion(v) + aux`; hotel varies by day/night +
  climate + events; propulsion `P = P_svc × (v/v_svc)³`.
- **Per-DG state:** board, state (stopped/starting/online/tripped/repair),
  load %, running hours, condition 0–100, LO sump %, fuel source (HFO/MGO).
  Online DGs on a bus share load proportionally to rating.
- **Spinning reserve.** Per board: reserve = online capacity − demand vs
  largest online unit (N+1 indicator on the mimic). PMS auto-start assist
  toggle from L3 (auto lineup is safe but fuel-inefficient; manual is the
  optimization game).
- **Blackout & recovery.** Bus collapse → emergency DG carries essential bus
  (1 tick) → guided black-start: start DG → close breaker → restore sections
  in order (cooling → fuel → propulsion → hotel). Drift timer near coast
  (L4/L6 scripted) gives Viking-Sky stakes. A consequence-free **drill**
  teaches the sequence on L2; organic blackouts are star-denying thereafter.
- **Fuel.** Tanks: HFO and MGO (tonnes). Burn per tick =
  `Σ(DG_kW × SFOC(load)) / 60e6` tonnes (SFOC ~174 g/kWh at 80% load, rising
  toward low load; sanity: L6 ≈ 250 t/day at service speed — pinned by unit
  test). Purifier throughput must cover HFO burn; purifier faults → cat-fines
  exposure meter (visible in FUEL panel) → accelerated DG wear. Bunkering in
  port: tonnes per grade at port prices within budget; capacity & reserve
  rules; on L5+ a topping-off **rate choice** (full rate to >90% risks
  overflow → spill fine).
- **ECA / emissions.** Route legs flagged ECA. Inside an ECA every online DG
  must burn MGO (or scrubber running, L4+). Switchover is an action with
  progress (~45 game-min); **compliance is deterministic**: violation-hours ×
  fine rate assessed at next port (no dice), plus debrief line. Scrubber lets
  HFO run in ECA, consumes ~1.5% plant power, can fault (forces switchover).
- **Maintenance.** Components (DGs, purifier, boiler, chillers, evaporator,
  sewage, stabilizers, steering, azipod bearings…) carry running hours vs
  service interval → due → overdue; overdue multiplies failure hazard and
  degrades efficiency/SFOC. Jobs need a free repair team + duration; some
  port-only. Teams: 1 (L1–2) → 2 (L3–4) → 3 (L5–6). **LO top-up is a team
  job** (raises sump %; the Viking-Sky mitigation).
- **Failures/events.** Scripted per level + hazard-weighted random pool
  (seeded; weights scale with condition/overdue/weather). Phase-machine
  contract in §2.5.
- **Canonical tick pipeline (X4).** Actions apply between ticks (queued from
  UI, applied in dispatch order). `tick()` then runs, in order: (1) time &
  position advance; (2) job progress/completions (maintenance effects land
  now — a job completed this tick counts before escalation checks); (3)
  demand recompute (hotel curve, weather, speed); (4) power balance per
  island → overload timers → preferential trips → blackout; (5) fuel burn,
  tanks, purifier/cat-fines; (6) event phase timers advance — an intervention
  recorded in any earlier step of this tick or before counts as "in time";
  (7) hazard rolls (≤ 1/game-hour/component); (8) comfort/schedule/derived;
  (9) alarm raise/clear → time-compression drop. One rule, serialized
  everywhere: pending interventions/jobs carry their remaining ticks and
  reserved team in state.
- **Weather.** Per-leg forecast (calm/moderate/rough/storm) on the route
  strip. Effects: propulsion demand +10–30%, comfort drain, stabilizers
  relevant, LO-sump sloshing hazard when sump < 50% (trips DGs in heavy roll),
  rogue-wave scripted beat (L6). Player options: request speed reduction
  (schedule cost), secure for heavy weather, stabilizers, top up sumps
  beforehand.
- **Comfort & schedule.** Comfort 0–100 drains on shed stages, chiller out
  (fast in tropics), water rationing, sewage down, heavy rolling without
  stabilizers, blackout; recovers slowly. Schedule: required arrival times;
  late costs score. Both live in the header.
- **Budget.** Fuel, parts, fines vs voyage allowance; projected fuel margin
  readout is derived state.

### 2.5 Events — phase-machine contract

An **event instance** = `{id, eventId, componentId?, phase, phaseTicksLeft,
causedBy?}` where `id` comes from a monotonic counter in state (serialized)
and `causedBy` links to the parent instance/decision for exact debrief chains
(X5). Actions target `instanceId`; the (eventId, componentId) tuple is only a
uniqueness guard so the same failure can run on DG2 and DG4 independently but
not twice on DG2. Each event declares:

- `phases[]`: ordered (e.g. `warning → alarm → escalation → casualty`), each
  with a **duration in ticks** (warnings are LONG — authored in game-hours so
  they're visible even at 32×), the annunciator tile + log line it raises, and
  its severity (none/amber/red).
- `interventions[]` per phase: id, label, cost (instant | team+duration),
  effect — **transition target** (e.g. back to `resolved`, or to a
  `degraded-stable` state with a lasting penalty) — so regression semantics
  are explicit. Earlier phases have cheaper interventions.
- `respond`: **per-phase ordered action list** — one dataset, two consumers
  (X7): it renders to the player as the event's procedure checklist in the
  Manual (and as Voss's advice), and the playthrough bot consumes exactly the
  same public data — the bot never knows anything a player can't read.
  Multi-step procedures (fire: quick-close valves → stop vents → close
  dampers → evacuate → release CO₂ → boundary cooling; black-start sequence)
  are ordered interventions with enforced prerequisites (CO₂ refuses before
  evacuation — releasing early is a casualty).
- Consequences as declarative data (component damage, board loss, comfort/
  budget deltas, follow-on event spawn) — no closures, snapshot-safe.

**Concurrency cap** on unscripted events: L1–3 max 1 active, L4 max 2, L5–6
max 3; scripted beats exempt. New-event hazard rolls happen at most once per
game-hour per component. **Commissioning rule (X10):** each level's random
pool arms only after its commissioning objectives complete — every newly
introduced coupled system gets one protected, guided first run (L3: first ECA
switchover; L4: tie open/close + sump top-ups; L5: bunkering op) before
random failures may fire.

**Cheap specs for the named set-pieces:**
- **PSC inspection (L5 low-stakes, L6 counts):** a checklist audit of existing
  state at the port call — emergency-gen test done this voyage? any overdue
  critical jobs? ECA violation-hours? oil-record (auto-kept) clean? Each
  deficiency = fine + debrief line; L6 detention only if ≥ 3 deficiencies.
  No new mechanics — it reads state the player already manages.
- **SRtP fire (L6):** scripted composition of existing pieces: fire event in
  ER2 → board 2 lost (its DGs + feeders) → run on board 1: shed to essential +
  partial hotel, reduced speed, arrive. No new engine mechanics beyond board
  topology (§2.4).
- **Rogue wave (L6, scripted during storm):** one tick of chaos — largest
  online DG trips, bilge-well alarm (minor ingress event spawns), comfort hit,
  several amber alarms; halved if `weather.secure` done and speed reduced.
- **Voyage script:** levels ARE a declarative phase machine too — an ordered
  list of `{at: time|position|condition, do: message|objective|event|gate}`
  rows; gates (L1–2 only) hold the voyage at "standby" until the objective
  completes. Same declarative shape as events; no second engine.

### 2.6 Scoring & progression

Completion = arrive at final port, ship intact → next level unlocks. Three
stars:
- **Safety** — no unscripted blackout, no **player-fault** casualty-phase
  reached (scripted set-piece damage — the L2 drill, the L6 SRtP board loss,
  the rogue wave's initial hit — is flagged `scripted` and never denies the
  star; what you do NEXT does, X8). ACK is never scored (A3).
- **Service** — voyage-average comfort ≥ threshold AND on-time arrival.
  (Average, not floor: an early trough on a 5-day voyage dilutes to a few
  points and stays recoverable — deliberate.)
- **Efficiency** — arrive with fuel + parts within budget and zero
  compliance fines (deterministic violation-hours accounting).

Failure (ship loss, grounding on drift timer, unrecovered blackout at voyage
end) → debrief with the full causal chain, then **Retry voyage** (whole level
only: levels are 8–20 min, and a crisis-start state is often already doomed by
earlier choices — the incident report → fresh run loop IS the pedagogy).
Campaign progress + best stars in localStorage.

### 2.7 Time & pacing

Speeds: **⏸ pause / 1× / 8× / 32×** (game-min per real second). Rules:
- The rAF accumulator **aborts its batch and drops to 1×** the tick ANY new
  alarm (amber or red) raises — warnings can't blink past at 32× (A1).
- Warning phases are authored in game-hours; intervention windows ≥ 5 ticks.
- All orders issuable while paused (pause-and-plan). L1's first event has Voss
  explicitly teach pause ("Take the time — stop the clock, read the panel").
- Render once per rAF frame regardless of ticks elapsed.

### 2.8 Tutorial & help

- **Objectives**: per-level phase checklist with live checkmarks; on phone a
  **persistent current-objective banner above every tab** (A4); tapping it
  opens the relevant tab; L1–2 auto-switch to the tab an objective targets.
- **Mentor**: Voss's messages arrive in the event log AND as a transient toast
  above the active tab; each teaches one action; L1–2 gates pace the voyage.
- **Engineering Manual**: overlay, ~12 one-screen pages (Power & the bus,
  Generators, Spinning reserve, Blackout recovery, Fuel & bunkering, Purifiers
  & cat fines, ECA & switchover, Maintenance, Alarms, Hotel systems, Heavy
  weather, Fire & emergencies). Alarm entries and panel headers link to their
  page ("?").
- Tooltips on all controls; "What's this?" on annunciator tiles.

### 2.9 UI

ISA-101 high-performance HMI: calm dark grays; color reserved for meaning
(amber warning, red critical, blue action-pending, green running). Single dark
theme. Seven-seg-style readouts for hero numbers (CSS/SVG digits or
`tabular-nums` — **no external font**), bar meters with normal bands
elsewhere.

Layout (desktop): header (ship/clock/speed/route strip with ECA + weather +
ports/schedule/budget/comfort/fuel margin) → annunciator strip → mimic center
+ right rail (objectives, event log) → slide-over panels FUEL / MAINT / SYS /
MANUAL. Phone (≤ 720 px): condensed header, objective banner, tab bar POWER /
ALARMS / FUEL / MAINT / SYS / LOG, persistent one-line alarm ticker.

**Mimic density rules (fixed now, for L6 @ 390 px):** DG **mini-tiles**
(state LED + load% + fuel grade) in a grid above their board's bus bar; tap →
detail sheet (breaker, hours, condition, sump, temps, actions). Two boards
stack vertically with the tie breaker drawn between. Consumers render as
**shed-stage group chips** (not individual feeders) + propulsion feeders.
Touch targets ≥ 44 px. Interaction stays ON the diagram.

Telegraph: bridge orders arrive as an order chit + required speed; player
acknowledges and sets the plant to match. "Request reduction" available
(bridge grumbles, schedule cost).

### 2.10 Failure UX

Blackout is not game over — it's the set-piece (drilled on L2, §2.4). Ship
loss only via unrecovered catastrophes. Debrief always names the chain:
"Blackout 14:32 ← DG3 trip ← fuel starvation ← purifier deferred Day 2."

### 2.11 Accessibility

- Annunciator flash ≤ 2 Hz (under WCAG 2.3.1's 3/s); under
  `prefers-reduced-motion` flashing is replaced by a steady high-contrast
  border + icon.
- All controls are real `<button>`s with visible focus; slide-overs/overlays
  close on Esc; logical focus order; the game is keyboard-playable.
- New alarms announce via a rate-limited `aria-live` region (assertive for
  red, polite for amber; coalesced to ≤ 1 announcement/2 s).
- Any element hidden via the `hidden` attribute but styled with `display:`
  gets an explicit `[hidden]{display:none}` override (lessons_learnt).

## 4. Testability design

- Engine pure & deterministic: `(level, seed)` + action script fully
  determines a run; snapshot-resume proven equivalent by test.
- Events declare per-phase `respond` action lists → bot coverage extends
  automatically with content; emergent procedures live in one bot policy
  module.
- Content validated structurally; level arithmetic (N+1, bunker margin)
  validated by content tests, so tuning can't silently break winnability.
- Property checks inside playthrough: no NaN, fuel ≥ 0, alarms raised ⇔
  thresholds crossed, bot never acts on information a player can't see.

## 6. Risks & mitigations

- **Scope creep** — aux zoo capped (decision 7); set-pieces specced cheaply
  (decision 16); the gating table is the whip.
- **Pacing/tuning** — constants in one `TUNING` block; content tests assert
  winnability arithmetic; manual playtest before ship.
- **Mobile mimic density** — density rules fixed in §2.9; browser test runs
  at 390×844 and 360×640.
- **Alarm fatigue vs drama** — baseline alarms rare; floods only in scripted
  cascades (L5+); concurrency caps (§2.5).
- **First-contact overwhelm** — L1 shows 3 DGs and ~8 tiles; progressive
  reveal (nothing not-yet-live renders at all).
