# Review rubric — Planes, Plans & Pennants (higher-graphic-communication)

Scoring rubric for adversarial review of the Higher Graphic Communication revision site.
Each dimension is scored 1–10 by independent reviewers (the dimension score is the **lowest** score any
reviewer gave it). The release bar is **at least 9 on every dimension**. A reviewer must list every defect
found, ranked by severity, with file, location and a concrete fix; a score of 9+ is only allowed when no
blocking defect remains in that dimension.

Sources of truth: Higher Graphic Communication course specification v2.0 (Sept 2018, C835 76) including
Appendix 2 (standards and conventions), the 2025 Higher course report, the 2025 assignment and its marking
instructions, the 2024 question-paper marking instructions, and BS 8888 / BS 1192-style drawing practice as
used in the course appendix.

| # | Dimension | What a 9–10 looks like | Automatic cap |
|---|-----------|------------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Technical correctness** | Every statement, definition, drawing convention, projection, development, CAD procedure, colour/typography fact and quiz answer is correct; diagrams obey the conventions they teach (third-angle layout, line types, hatching, dimensioning rules); every widget computes/draws correct geometry. | Any wrong answer or false statement: max 6. Any widget that draws or computes something wrong: max 7. |
| 2 | **Syllabus coverage and fidelity** | Every item in the "Skills, knowledge and understanding for the course assessment" table of the course specification is taught (both the question-paper and assignment columns); assessment facts (90 + 50 = 140 marks, 2 h 30, 8 h assignment, A3 pages, mark ranges, 2025 boundaries and task structure, report quotes) are accurate and sourced; nothing out of scope is presented as examinable. | A missing spec item: max 7. A wrong assessment fact or invented/misquoted report claim: max 7. |
| 3 | **Intelligibility and pedagogy** | A typical S5/S6 student who passed National 5 Graphic Communication can follow every chapter unaided: concepts before procedures, short plain-English sentences, defined terms, worked examples that model the form of an exam answer, misconceptions addressed, good progression. | Unexplained jump or undefined term a student needs: max 8. |
| 4 | **Diagrams and visual clarity** | Static diagrams are accurate, correctly drawn to the conventions taught, labelled, legible at phone width and in dark mode; the site practises what it preaches (good layout, contrast, hierarchy). | Mislabelled or misleading diagram: max 7. |
| 5 | **Interactivity and animation** | Widgets work without errors, are correct, teach something the text cannot, respond smoothly, and include genuinely clever animation (e.g. unfolding a glass box, rotating a model, sweeping a profile); controls are clear; state stays consistent with the text. | Broken widget or console error: max 6. |
| 6 | **Exam alignment** | Worked examples and checks use exam-style wording (describe / explain / compare), sensible mark allocations and the terminology the course report says candidates must use; assignment advice matches the published task and marking instructions; no claim to reproduce official marking instructions we do not have. | Advice that would mislead a student about how marks are earned: max 7. |
| 7 | **Theme integration and identity** | The Minecraft × RAF Air Cadets × Napoleonic-navy mashup is present across decks, consistent with the Computing/Physics/Maths/Engineering siblings, never obscures the graphics content, and there is a clear but subtle visual cue (aubergine masthead, CMYK colour bar, isometric-grid paper, magenta accent) that this is the Graphic Communication site. | Theme that confuses the content: max 7. |
| 8 | **Accessibility, robustness and responsiveness** | No horizontal scroll at 390px or 768px; nothing under the "← Almanac" pill; keyboard operable; aria labels on canvases and SVGs; reduced-motion respected; no console errors; light and dark themes legible; contrast adequate. | Horizontal overflow or an untappable control: max 6. |

## Process

1. Reviewers work independently and adversarially: their job is to find faults.
2. Each returns a score per assigned dimension and a ranked defect list.
3. All defects are fixed (or rebutted with evidence), then the affected dimensions are re-reviewed.
4. Repeat until every dimension scores ≥ 9.
