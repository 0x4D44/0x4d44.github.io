## 2. The central question: can a person on a keyboard enjoy Normal?

**Not yet.**

**Steering does not pay.** On Normal, a key player who steers like a person (the tap bot) is last in 6 of 8 Chrome races and 7 of 8 Node races. The same craft with no keys pressed is 1st or 2nd in Chrome. Whatever a keyboard player does with the keys makes the result worse, so the game teaches them to let go.

**Easy is not kind:**
- The tap bot averages 5.75 (Chrome) and 5.88 (Node), with no podium in Node.
- Easy and Normal converge for a trailing player. With the player far behind, the rubber band holds the leading AI at `RACE.aheadFloor` 0.65 on both, and Easy's aiSpeed 0.67 is only 3% above that floor.
- The AI winners' times prove it: Easy 245.9 / 301.0 / 266.3 / 285.2 s against Normal 253.2 / 297.6 / 238.5 / 263.2 s (same courses and seed, Chrome).

**The rubber band is invisible but cannot help.** It is smooth: no jumps in any trace, and the AIs within 250 m ahead fly at 87-98% of the player's speed. It holds the field back by 20-40%, but the tap bot loses on control, not on speed. In Node the player averages 84-92 m/s and the leading AI 65-78 m/s, and the player still finishes last.

Where the tap bot's time goes (Node, Normal, 8 races, per race):
- 15.1 tumbles: 11.5 ground, 2.3 hazards, 1.8 items.
- 30 s below 15 m/s of course progress.

The guide flies better than the tap bot does, so every key press costs time.

The bots, all Normal unless marked, seed 7 in Chrome:
- **tap:** real key events, a decision every 0.1 s, 0.12 s lag, taps whose length is the strength.
- **track:** the ideal player, keys chosen every 1/60 s, no lag.
- **hands-off:** no key ever pressed.
- **autopilot:** the AI brain on the analog path.

Compared with round 1, the gap has closed a lot:
- The ideal bot now wins most Normal races (round 1: 4th-8th).
- The tap bot flies a lap in 94-112 s (round 1: 132-192 s).
- The tap bot is 1.05-1.40x the winner over 3 laps (round 1: 2.4-3.4x per lap). Much of that comes from the rubber band slowing the field.

**The guide:** it helps an idle player and fights a held bank key (R2-P-01). The fix wave already accepts the shared fade, and R2-P-02 carries the rest.

**What is wrong, and the rule I propose** (measured on the Node replica: real race code, real guide, the key ramps of `input.js`):
1. **The ladder alone cannot fix it.**
   - Normal aiSpeed 0.82 and 0.86 leave hands-off at 2.63 and 4.25: the rubber band keeps it in touch at any pace.
   - Help from place 2 with playerHelp 0.15 moves the tap bot only to 6.50. The race handoff already found that such help pushes neon AI contact over its limit.
2. **The guide is the lever.** Mean place of the tap bot on Normal, against 7.88 for the shipped guide:
   - one shared fade plus a risk safety net that stays on under held keys: 6.75 (tumbles 15.1 -> 9.0);
   - adding a 25% residual guide share under held keys: 4.88 (tumbles 6.9, 4 podiums in 8), while the ideal track bot improves too (3.63 -> 2.25);
   - details and values in R2-P-02.
3. **Easy:** aiSpeed 0.67 -> 0.60 takes the tap bot from 5.88 to 1.63 (7 podiums in 8) and puts Easy under the 0.65 floor, so Easy stops converging with Normal.
4. **Hands-off is a design call for the lead.**
   - With the candidate guide, hands-off gets better too: 3.00 in Node, with 3 wins. Letting go then still beats a tapping player, 3.00 against 4.88.
   - Weakening the guide for idle players does not separate the two: it traps hands-off craft and hurts the tap bot (R2-P-02).
   - The options are a hands-off speed rule after 10 s without a key (untested) or accepting hands-off as a disclosed assist mode.
5. **The gate I propose:** the keybot tap mode, falcon, 4 courses x seeds 7 and 1.
   - Normal: mean place <= 5, and better than hands-off on the same seeds.
   - Easy: mean place <= 3.
   - The track bot must stay at or under 3 on Normal.

**Limit of the method.** The tap bot knows the ideal line (it copies the autopilot) and reacts in 0.12 s, faster than a person (0.2-0.25 s), but it cannot anticipate. A person who reads the corridor ahead does better; a first-timer who does not know the line does worse. The race handoff assumes the median human sits between tap and track. That is untested, and it does not answer the hands-off result.

## 8. Known limitations, weighed

- **Neon Expert contact 1.17-1.19% (target 1.0%):** not re-measured. It is an AI-contact metric that a player sees only as AIs scraping the walls on Expert. It counts in Course 7.0 and Racing 6.5 only as a residue of P-10, and it does not decide either score.
- **Gamepad and touch tested only synthetically:** I could not hold a real pad. This caps Flight and UI below 8.5 by itself, because the rubric asks for all inputs to be "responsive and learnable". R2-P-05 (a connected pad turns the guide off) comes from code and a surviving mutation, not from a device.
- **Audio never heard:** every run was `?mute=1`. Audio 7.0 is Lens B's number carried forward; only the event side (pings, cue) was verified here.
- **Missile intercept lead has no unit test:** its behaviour is covered by the 32-race hit rate (40.2%) and the browser trials, not by a test. It costs Items nothing in score, but a regression would go unnoticed (as in R2-P-07).
- **The key bots are models:** see the limit of the method in section 2. The ordering hands-off > track > tap is robust across Chrome and Node. The absolute places are not.
