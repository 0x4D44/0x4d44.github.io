# OxAlphaRally — AAA build plan

Date: 2026.08.22 · Owner: lead session (goal `OxAlphaRally`) · Status: active

## Mission

A new rally game in `/Users/md/language/0x4d44.github.io/OxAlphaRally/` at the level of
the best modern rally games, with the feel of the original Colin McRae Rally era: fast,
scary, readable. Every domain at AAA quality — handling, stages, damage, weather, audio,
pace notes, graphics. Original assets and branding only (no real drivers, manufacturers,
trademarks, copied layouts or sounds). A harsh independent critic gates every slice and
must be *wowed* — blind side-by-side against the best modern games and real rally footage
— before a slice closes.

## Baseline decision

Seeded from `opus-rally/` @ trunk commit `0832ef2` (copy taken 2026.08.22). Rationale:
strongest local foundation — true 4-wheel model (Pacejka-style tyres, per-wheel struts,
centre-diff drivetrain, turbo/anti-lag), Euler-spiral corner-grammar stage generator,
HDR post pipeline (radial blur → chroma → bloom → ACES → grade → vignette → dither),
100% procedural WebAudio engine synthesis (firing-impulse additive stack), TTS co-driver,
damage model (~28 components), touch controls, replay, career; 434 unit assertions plus
a drivability oracle that autopilots all 12 book stages. Its published defect list
(`opus-rally/OUTSTANDING.md`) is adopted verbatim as the opening backlog.
`cairn-run-rally` stays untouched (mid-gauntlet, separate IP); its matrix-oracle idea is
ported later.

## Law of the codebase

`CONTRACTS.md` (copied from opus-rally) remains binding: plain ES modules, SI units,
+Y-up +Z-forward, no hot-path allocation, seeded RNG only (`rng.js`), headless-testable
pure paths, one owner per module with pinned data shapes. Rebrand renames product-facing
identity only (title, visible strings, car/stage/event names as content work touches
them); internal module seams keep their names unless renamed coherently in one pass.
Keep `THREE-LICENSE.txt` beside the vendored three.js bundle forever.

## Opening backlog (= opus-rally OUTSTANDING.md, verify each before fixing)

P0 — player-blocking / critic-cited:
1. Car reads as a slab: closed-hull deck at belt-line kills cockpit view, hides dash and
   wheel, no glasshouse depth, no cabin trim. Rebuild body loft + interior.
2. Restart on a low-grip climb strands the player — auto-clutch must slip from rest.
3. Headlights blow out to pure white; night reads as dusk (moon intensity/exposure).
4. Snow renders as hard white squares.
5. Terrain skin interpenetrates road ribbon up to 0.81 m — conform lattice vertices near
   the centreline (`meshes.js`).

P1 — correctness:
6. `surfaceAt.onRoad` keys off |lateral| alone — grip reported 50 m past the finish.
7. `speedProfile` lacks a vertical-curvature term and models the engine as P/(m·v) —
   crest launches the car at speeds the profile authorised, mud-climb speeds are false.
8. Scenery blows its triangle budget (272k/314k vs declared 240k); nothing thins it.
9. Verify touch controls actually fixed (`touch.js` postdates OUTSTANDING) — else fix.

P2 — polish: FOV/speed-feel audit (vertical-vs-horizontal lesson), camera rigs, particles.

## Critic protocol (the gate)

After each slice the lead runs evidence capture, then spawns an independent harsh-critic
sub-agent (fresh context, briefed to be brutal):

- Evidence via `tests/shoot.mjs` at pinned quality (the autoscaler would photograph
  SwiftShader at lowest preset); manifest.json proves car state — **a frame whose surface
  reads GRASS is not evidence**. Cockpit AND chase cams; day, night, wet, snow states.
- Critic judges against a written AAA rubric (Colin McRae Rally-era feel plus modern
  rally-game presentation), ranks our frames blind against reference descriptions of
  best-in-class footage where feasible, and returns a verdict per lens: visuals /
  handling telemetry / audio / stage character.
- Verdict scale: WOWED or NOT-THERE with specific defects. NOT-THERE loops the owning
  worker with the defect list. No slice closes on anything less than WOWED.
- Gates always green first: `npm test`, `npm run validate`, browser gate.

## Methodology lessons carried forward (opus-rally "things that turned out to be lies")

1. Measure output pixels, not input radiance — mirror exposure/ACES/sRGB in weather
   assertions so tests measure what the eye sees.
2. Never anchor source scans on newline sequences — CRLF checkouts silently blind them.
3. A browser gate must actually START a stage, not just generate one (11 of 12 could not
   start once).
4. Correct units composed wrong still break — camera fov is vertical; author it as such.
