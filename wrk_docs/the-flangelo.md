# The Flangelo

*A story about flanging sprockets, after Julia Donaldson's "The Gruffalo".*
*(Draft — not wired into the almanac catalog.)*

A sprocket took a roll through the deep dark wood.
A fox saw the sprocket, and the sprocket looked good.

"Where are you rolling to, little steel sprocket?
Come down to my den — I've a file in my pocket.
I'll flange you this afternoon for free."
"It's terribly kind of you, Fox, but you see,
I'm taking my tea with a Flangelo."

"A Flangelo? What's a Flangelo?"
"A Flangelo! Why, didn't you know?

He has terrible torque and terrible claws,
And terrible teeth in his gear-grinding jaws."

"Where are you meeting him?"
"Here, by these rocks,
And his favourite food is flange-grilled fox."

"Flange-grilled fox! I'm off!" Fox said.
"Goodbye, little sprocket," and away he sped.

"Silly old Fox! Doesn't he know?
There's no such thing as a Flangelo!"

On rolled the sprocket through the deep dark wood.
An owl saw the sprocket, and the sprocket looked good.

"Where are you rolling to, little steel sun?
Come up to my treetop — I'll flange you for fun."
"It's frightfully kind of you, Owl, but no,
I'm taking my tea with a Flangelo."

"A Flangelo? What's a Flangelo?"
"A Flangelo! Why, didn't you know?

He has knurled steel knuckles and pistons for toes,
And a red-hot rivet on the end of his nose."

"Where are you meeting him?"
"Here, by this stream,
And his favourite food is flanged owl cream."

"Flanged owl cream! Toowhit-toowhoo!
Goodbye, little sprocket," and away Owl flew.

"Silly old Owl! Doesn't he know?
There's no such thing as a Flangelo!"

On rolled the sprocket through the deep dark wood.
A snake saw the sprocket, and the sprocket looked good.

"Where are you rolling to, little bright moon?
Come flange in my log-pile this fine afternoon."
"It's wonderfully kind of you, Snake, but no,
I'm taking my tea with a Flangelo."

"A Flangelo? What's a Flangelo?"
"A Flangelo! Why, didn't you know?

His eyes are ball bearings, his tongue's a steel spline,
And he's spanner-sharp spikes all the way down his spine."

"Where are you meeting him?"
"Here, by this lake,
And his favourite food is flange-rolled snake."

"Flange-rolled snake! It's time I hid!
Goodbye, little sprocket," and away Snake slid.

"Silly old Snake! Doesn't he know?
There's no such thing as a Flangel…

…OH!

But who is this creature with terrible claws
And terrible teeth in his gear-grinding jaws?
He has knurled steel knuckles and pistons for toes,
And a red-hot rivet on the end of his nose.
His eyes are ball bearings, his tongue's a steel spline,
And he's spanner-sharp spikes all the way down his spine.

Oh help! Oh no!
It's a Flangelo!"

"My favourite tea!" the Flangelo said.
"You'll flange up a treat on a slice of lead."

"Flange *me*?" said the sprocket. "Why, don't be absurd!
I'm the scariest sprocket this wood ever heard.
Just roll on behind me and soon you will see,
Everyone is afraid of me."

"All right," said the Flangelo, bursting with laughter.
"You roll on ahead and I'll trundle on after."

They rolled and they trundled till the Flangelo said,
"I hear a hiss in the leaves ahead."

"It's Snake," said the sprocket. "Why, Snake, hello!"
Snake took one look at the Flangelo.
"Oh crumbs!" he said. "Goodbye, little sprocket!"
And off he slid, quick as a rocket.

"You see?" said the sprocket. "I told you so."
"Amazing!" clanked the Flangelo.

They rolled some more till the Flangelo said,
"I hear a hoot in the trees ahead."

"It's Owl," said the sprocket. "Why, Owl, hello!"
Owl took one look at the Flangelo.
"Oh dear!" he said. "Goodbye, little sprocket!"
And he shot from his branch like a bolt from a socket.

"You see?" said the sprocket. "I told you so."
"Astounding!" clanked the Flangelo.

They rolled some more till the Flangelo said,
"I hear some paws on the path ahead."

"It's Fox," said the sprocket. "Why, Fox, hello!"
Fox took one look at the Flangelo.
"Oh help!" he said. "Goodbye, little sprocket!"
And he dived down his hole and bolted to lock it.

"Well, Flangelo," said the sprocket. "You see?
Everyone is afraid of me.
But now my chain is starting to grumble,
And my favourite food is Flangelo crumble."

"Flangelo crumble!" the Flangelo said,
And quick as a flywheel he turned and fled.

All was quiet in the deep dark wood.
The sprocket found an oilcan, and the oil was good.
