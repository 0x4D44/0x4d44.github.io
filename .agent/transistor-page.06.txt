        <p>The silicon die is only part of the story. The package has to make electrical contact, survive assembly, shed heat and sometimes bolt itself to several kilograms of aluminium.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="story-grid">
        <article class="story-card"><span class="story-no">01 · seal</span><h3>Metal cans kept the world out.</h3><p>TO-18 and TO-39 packages enclosed the die in a welded metal shell. They were expensive but robust, easy to identify and well suited to early low-volume electronics.</p></article>
        <article class="story-card"><span class="story-no">02 · mould</span><h3>Plastic made transistors ordinary.</h3><p>The TO-92 and SOT-23 use moulded epoxy. Once automatic assembly took over, the package became cheap enough to vanish inside almost everything.</p></article>
        <article class="story-card"><span class="story-no">03 · heat</span><h3>Power puts the metal back.</h3><p>TO-220, TO-247 and TO-3 expose a tab or case to a heatsink. Their apparent bulk is not wasted space: it is a low-resistance path from a hot die to the outside world.</p></article>
      </div>
    </div>
