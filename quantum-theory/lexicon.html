Chapter 8

The Lexicon

The working vocabulary of the field, defined the way physicists actually use it; the constants of nature to the precision the SI now fixes them; the century-and-a-half timeline in one table; and an honest reading list for going further.

8.1Vocabulary, A–Z

amplitude §2.1
The complex number nature assigns to each way an event can happen. Add for indistinguishable alternatives, then square the magnitude for probability.
Auger recombination §7.5
Non-radiative decay in which an exciton hands its energy to a third carrier instead of a photon. The culprit behind quantum-dot blinking.
band gap §7.1
The forbidden energy range between a semiconductor's full valence band and empty conduction band; the minimum price of making an electron–hole pair.
Bell inequality §5.4
A bound (|S| ≤ 2 in CHSH form) obeyed by every local hidden-variable theory and violated by experiment at 2√2. The theorem that made metaphysics measurable.
blackbody §1.1
An ideal perfect absorber; its thermal spectrum depends on temperature alone and is described exactly by Planck's law — the problem that started everything.
Born rule §2.1
P = |ψ|². The only bridge between the theory's amplitudes and observed frequencies.
boson §4.3
A particle of integer spin. Many may share one state, and prefer to — hence lasers and condensates.
bra / ket §2.2
Dirac's notation: |ψ⟩ a state vector, ⟨φ| its dual, ⟨φ|ψ⟩ their inner product — the amplitude to find |ψ⟩ in condition φ.
Brus equation §7.2
Eg(R) = Egbulk + (ħ²π²/2R²)(1/me* + 1/mh*) − 1.786e²/4πε₀εR: how a quantum dot's radius sets its colour.
coherence §5.2
The definite phase relationships within a superposition (the density matrix's off-diagonals); what interference spends and decoherence destroys.
collapse §2.4
The discontinuous update of the state on measurement. Whether it is a physical process, an inference update, or an artefact of incomplete description is the measurement problem.
commutator §2.5
[Â,B̂] = ÂB̂ − B̂Â. Zero: compatible observables. Non-zero: an exact uncertainty trade-off, ΔA·ΔB ≥ ½|⟨[Â,B̂]⟩|.
conduction band §7.1
The first empty band of a semiconductor; home of mobile electrons once promoted across the gap.
decoherence §5.6
The environmentally-enforced decay of coherence as scattering records which-path information; why big things look classical, at exponential speed.
degeneracy §3.5
Several distinct states sharing one energy (hydrogen level n holds n² of them). Also: the Pauli-pressure state of matter in white dwarfs.
density matrix ρ §5.2
The state description that handles mixtures and subsystems; pure states have ρ² = ρ, and entangled subsystems are mixed even when the whole is pure.
eigenstate / eigenvalue §2.3
Â|a⟩ = a|a⟩: a state in which observable A has the definite value a — the measurement outcomes and their after-states.
effective mass §7.1
The inertia a carrier displays inside a crystal's periodic potential, set by band curvature; 0.13 me for electrons in CdSe, which is why nanometre crystals show quantum effects.
entanglement §5.3
A joint state not expressible as a product of single-particle states; the parts have no individual descriptions, only correlations — stronger than any local classical account allows.
exciton §7.1
A bound electron–hole pair — a hydrogen atom rescaled by a crystal, several nanometres across; a quantum dot is a box smaller than one.
expectation value §2.3
⟨A⟩ = ⟨ψ|Â|ψ⟩: the mean of many measurements of A on identically prepared systems. Not necessarily any single possible outcome.
fermion §4.3
A particle of half-integer spin. Antisymmetric under exchange, hence at most one per state (exclusion) — the reason matter has structure and volume.
fine-structure constant α §6.4
e²/4πε₀ħc ≈ 1/137.036 — the dimensionless strength of electromagnetism and the expansion parameter of QED.
Hamiltonian Ĥ §2.4
The energy observable, and by Postulate V the generator of time evolution: to know Ĥ is to know everything the system can do.
Hermitian operator §2.3
Â = Â†. Real eigenvalues, orthogonal eigenvectors, complete basis: exactly the credentials an observable needs.
Hilbert space §2.2
The complete complex inner-product vector space where states live; ℂ² for a qubit, square-integrable functions for a particle on a line.
hole §7.1
The absence of an electron in an otherwise full valence band, behaving as a positive carrier with its own effective mass.
hot injection §7.4
The 1993 synthesis that made monodisperse quantum dots: burst nucleation from injected precursors, then diffusion-limited size-focusing growth, quenched at the target colour.
interference §2.1
The enhancement or cancellation of combined amplitudes; visible wherever no record distinguishes the alternatives, gone the moment one does.
ladder operators â, â† §3.3
Operators that step a harmonic oscillator down and up its evenly spaced levels; in field theory they annihilate and create particles.
Lamb shift §6.2
The 1,058 MHz splitting of hydrogen levels Dirac's theory calls degenerate, caused by vacuum fluctuations; the measurement that launched modern QED.
measurement problem §5.1
The unresolved tension between smooth unitary evolution and the single definite outcomes we observe; narrowed but not closed by decoherence.
no-cloning theorem §5.5
Linearity forbids copying an unknown quantum state — the foundation of quantum cryptography and the reason quantum information is unbackupable.
observable §2.3
Any measurable quantity; formally, the Hermitian operator whose eigenvalues are its possible values.
Pauli exclusion principle §4.3
No two identical fermions in one quantum state. Builds the periodic table, stiffens matter, holds up dead stars.
phonon §3.3
A quantum of lattice vibration — the harmonic oscillator ladder applied to a crystal's normal modes.
photon §1.3
The quantum of the electromagnetic field: energy hν, momentum h/λ, spin 1, bosonic — and one whole photon per detection click, always.
Planck's constant h §1.2
The grain of action, 6.626 070 15 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s exactly (it now defines the kilogram). ħ = h/2π appears wherever frequency becomes angular.
probability current §3.1
The flow field that keeps |ψ|² locally conserved; what “motion” means for a probability cloud.
qubit §2.7
Any two-level quantum system; its states fill the Bloch sphere, its phases carry the interference that quantum computers spend.
quantum dot §7
A semiconductor nanocrystal smaller than its exciton — a manufactured particle-in-a-box whose size chooses its colour.
renormalisation §6.4
Expressing predictions in measured (dressed) masses and charges, whereupon the loop divergences cancel; understood today as the insulation of low-energy physics from unknown high-energy detail.
Schrödinger equation §3.1
iħ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ: the deterministic, linear law of motion for amplitudes — everything except the outcomes.
spin §4.1
Intrinsic angular momentum with no rotating parts; two-valued along any axis for the electron, and the property that sorts matter into fermions and bosons.
spin–statistics theorem §4.3
Half-integer spin ⇒ fermion, integer ⇒ boson; provable only in relativistic quantum field theory (Pauli, 1940).
stationary state §2.6
An energy eigenstate: only its global phase evolves, so nothing observable changes — why atoms in definite levels do not radiate.
Stern–Gerlach experiment §4.1
The 1922 splitting of a silver beam into exactly two spots: space quantisation, and the prototype of every quantum measurement since.
superposition §2.2
A weighted sum of states, itself a state — with phase relationships that no “secretly one or the other” story reproduces.
tunnelling §3.4
Transmission through a classically forbidden barrier via the wavefunction's evanescent tail, exponentially sensitive to width and height; powers the Sun, alpha decay and the STM.
uncertainty principle §2.5
Δx·Δp ≥ ħ/2 — a theorem about states, not instruments: conjugate quantities are not jointly defined, and the bound sets the size of atoms.
unitary evolution §2.6
The rigid rotation of the state vector by e^{−iĤt/ħ}: probability conserved, information never destroyed — between measurements, at least.
vacuum §6.2
The field state with zero particles and non-zero fluctuation: it shifts levels (Lamb), presses plates together (Casimir) and tickles atoms into spontaneous emission.
valence band §7.1
A semiconductor's topmost filled band; leave a vacancy in it and the vacancy is a hole.
wavefunction ψ §3.1
The position-basis amplitudes ⟨x|ψ⟩ of a state: complex-valued, spread over configuration space, and the single most argued-about object in physics.
work function §1.3
The exit toll W a metal charges an escaping electron; the photoelectric threshold frequency is W/h.
zero-point energy §3.3
The ½ħω a confined system keeps at absolute zero because stillness would violate the uncertainty principle; why helium never freezes unpressed.

8.4Where to go next

  • Feynman, QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter — the arrow-summing picture of Chapters 2 and 6, done by its inventor for a general audience, with zero loss of honesty.
  • Griffiths & Schroeter, Introduction to Quantum Mechanics — the standard undergraduate route through Chapters 2–4 with full mathematics.
  • Sakurai & Napolitano, Modern Quantum Mechanics — the graduate step: symmetry, angular momentum and Bell done properly.
  • Bell, Speakable and Unspeakable in Quantum Mechanics — the measurement problem and non-locality from the sharpest writer the subject has had.
  • Zee, Quantum Field Theory in a Nutshell — Chapter 6 at full strength, with jokes.
  • Norris's thesis and the Bawendi group's reviews for quantum-dot spectroscopy, and the 2023 Nobel scientific background (“Quantum Dots — Seeds of Nanoscience”, nobelprize.org) for a superbly written free survey of Chapter 7.
  • On this site: The Interference Engine — quantum computing, the engineering of Chapter 2's phases; and The Star-Forged Ring and The Death of Stars, where Chapter 4's degeneracy pressure decides the fate of stars.
