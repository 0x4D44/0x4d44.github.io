Chapter 6

Fields & Light

Marry quantum mechanics to special relativity and the particle itself dissolves: what remains fundamental are fields filling all of space, with particles as their countable ripples. The resulting theory — quantum field theory, and its flagship, quantum electrodynamics — is the most accurate physical theory ever constructed, and it comes with the best story in physics: Feynman's sum over every history at once.

6.1Why particles had to go

The deepest layer of the theory has a startling punchline: particles aren't the fundamental things. Fields are — and a particle is just a ripple in one.

The big idea, up front

Picture space filled everywhere with invisible fields — one for electrons, one for the electromagnetic force, one per kind of particle. Normally each field lies calm. Poke energy into it and it ripples, and a single ripple of the smallest allowed size is a particle: a ripple in the electromagnetic field is a photon, a ripple in the electron field is an electron. That's why every electron is identical to every other (they're ripples on the same pond), why particles can be made and destroyed (ripples come and go), and why light and matter turn out to be the same kind of thing.

Why go to all this trouble? Because relativity forces it. Non-relativistic quantum mechanics quietly assumes you keep the particles you start with. Relativity revokes the assumption. Once E = mc² prices matter in energy, a collision energetic enough simply makes new particles — photograph any modern accelerator event and count the debris: two protons in, dozens of hadrons out. A one-particle Schrödinger equation cannot describe a world where “how many particles?” has a time-dependent answer. Worse, forcing relativity onto a single-particle wavefunction breaks causality: amplitudes leak outside the light cone unless something profound is added.

The something is the quantum field. Promote the field itself — the electromagnetic field, an “electron field”, one field per species — to the fundamental object, a quantum operator at every point of spacetime. Fourier-analyse a field into its normal modes and each mode is exactly the harmonic oscillator of §3.3; quantising it hands every mode the even ladder E = (n + ½)ħω, and the integer n acquires a magnificent reinterpretation: it counts particles. A photon is one quantum of excitation of a mode of the electromagnetic field; an electron, one quantum of the electron field. The ladder operators â†, â become creation and annihilation operators, and processes classical physics could never write — an atom creating a photon that did not exist, a photon becoming an electron–positron pair — are ordinary matrix elements. Identical particles are identical (Chapter 4) for the deepest possible reason: every electron is a ripple in the same sea, as interchangeable as two ripples on one pond. And spin–statistics stops being a postulate: only the boson pairing of integer-spin fields, and the fermion pairing of half-integer ones, yields a theory that is local, causal and stable — Pauli's 1940 theorem, the “why” behind the minus sign that builds the periodic table.

6.2The busy vacuum

Sum the zero-point halves over the field's infinite modes and the vacuum — the state with no particles — seethes with fluctuation. This is not rhetoric; the vacuum's activity is measured:

  • Spontaneous emission. Why does an excited atom, alone in the dark, radiate at all? Because “the dark” is the fluctuating vacuum field, tickling the atom down its ladder. Dirac's 1927 quantisation of radiation derived Einstein's A coefficient from first principles — the founding calculation of QED. (Suppress the modes with a reflective cavity and the atom demonstrably cannot decay: cavity QED does exactly this.)
  • The Casimir effect. Two neutral conducting plates exclude long-wavelength vacuum modes between them; the imbalance in zero-point pressure pushes them together with a force per unit area of π²ħc/240d⁴ — about one atmosphere at a 10 nm gap — measured to percent precision, and a genuine engineering consideration in nano-mechanical devices.
  • The Lamb shift. In 1947 Willis Lamb measured a 1,058 MHz sliver between two hydrogen levels (2s₁/₂ and 2p₁/₂) that Dirac's equation calls exactly degenerate. The culprit: the electron, jostled by vacuum fluctuations, smears its position and samples the Coulomb well slightly differently in the two states. The race to compute it (Bethe's first estimate was done on the train home from the Shelter Island conference) forged modern QED.

6.3The sum over every history at once

Feynman's picture of how a particle gets from A to B is gloriously mad, and exactly right: it takes every possible path at once — and the sensible one wins by a vote.

Here's the recipe. To find the amplitude for a particle to travel from A to B, take every path connecting them — the straight one, the wiggly ones, the absurd detour to the Moon and back — and give each an arrow (§2.1) of the same length but pointing in a direction set by that path. Add all the arrows. Most of them, from wild paths, point every which way and cancel; only the arrows from paths near the sensible, straight one line up and survive. The classical path isn't selected — it's what's left standing after everything else cancels:

ψ(A→B) = ∑all paths ei S[path]/ħ FEYNMAN, 1948

Nothing selects the classical path; it emerges. Where the action varies rapidly from path to path (anything far from the classical route, or any situation with S ≫ ħ), neighbouring arrows point every which way and annihilate. Near the path of stationary action — exactly the path classical mechanics decrees — the arrows align and reinforce. Classical physics is the stationary-phase approximation to a democracy of histories; turn ħ down in the apparatus below and watch Newton condense out of the spiral. The formulation is fully equivalent to Schrödinger's and Heisenberg's, and it is the working language of modern field theory, where the “paths” are entire field configurations.

Crank up the “action scale” slider. At low values (deep quantum), the particle genuinely tries every wandering path and the arrows on the right spiral all over. Turn it up (heavier, more everyday) and the wild paths cancel each other out — only the straight, sensible path survives. Watch Newton's single trajectory condense out of a democracy of every path at once. That's where classical physics comes from.

6.4QED: diagrams, ghosts and twelve decimal places

Interacting field theories are solved perturbatively: expand in powers of the coupling — for electromagnetism the dimensionless fine-structure constant α = e²/4πε₀ħc ≈ 1/137.036 — and Feynman's diagrams are the bookkeeping that keeps the expansion honest: every way the process can happen, drawn. Each line is a propagating quantum, each vertex a creation/annihilation event carrying a factor √α; integrate over everything unobserved. Two electrons repel by exchanging a photon; that photon is virtual — an internal line, never observed, permitted to be “off-shell” because it lives inside an energy–time uncertainty window. Virtual pairs dress every charge: the vacuum near an electron polarises like a dielectric, so the charge you measure depends on how closely you probe — coupling “constants” run with energy, a measured fact.

The loops carry a famous curse: integrated naïvely they diverge. The cure, renormalisation (Tomonaga, Schwinger, Feynman, Dyson, 1946–49), is not a swindle but a recognition: the “bare” mass and charge in the equations are not observable — only the dressed, measured values are. Express predictions in terms of measured mass and charge and every infinity cancels, order after order, in any renormalisable theory. Modern effective-field-theory language makes it fully respectable: low-energy physics is insulated from unknown high-energy detail, which is why physics is possible before knowing the final theory.

The payoff is the most precise confrontation between thought and measurement in science: the electron's anomalous magnetic moment. Dirac says g = 2 exactly; the vacuum's dressing adds Schwinger's α/2π and thousands of loop diagrams more. As of the 2023 measurements,

g/2 = 1.001 159 652 180 59 (13) EXPERIMENT — AND THEORY AGREES TO ~10⁻¹²

— agreement to about one part in a trillion, the equivalent of predicting the distance from Edinburgh to Sydney to the width of a bacterium. Whatever quantum field theory is, it is not approximately right.

6.5The Standard Model, on one page

Apply the same machinery — fields, gauge symmetry, renormalisation — to the weak and strong forces and you get the Standard Model: three generations of matter fermions, four force carriers, and one scalar. Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) binds quarks with gluons that carry colour charge themselves, so the force strengthens with distance — quarks are confined in hadrons forever, while at close range they rattle around nearly free (“asymptotic freedom”, Nobel 2004). The electroweak sector unifies electromagnetism with the weak force above ~100 GeV; the Higgs field's condensate breaks the symmetry and gives the W, Z and the fermions their masses — confirmed by the 125 GeV boson found at CERN in 2012.

The rest appears in accelerators, cosmic rays and the early universe. And the table is confessedly unfinished: it omits gravity (no accepted quantum theory of it yet), says nothing about dark matter or dark energy, does not explain why three generations or why these masses, and leaves the measurement problem of Chapter 5 exactly where it found it. The most accurate theory in history is also, by its own lights, an effective theory — a low-energy shadow of something not yet seen. Physics remains open at the top.

Down from the summit

From here the almanac descends from the highest theory to the most touchable application. Take the electron field, put it inside a semiconductor crystal a few nanometres wide, and Chapter 3's particle-in-a-box comes back — as a manufactured object you can hold up to the light. That is a quantum dot.
