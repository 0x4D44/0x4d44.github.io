# The Modern Frontier

After ChatGPT, AI became an industry, a geopolitical contest, and a daily tool for hundreds of millions. This final chapter maps the players, shows what the technology can actually do right now — with live demos — and sits, honestly, with the questions we don't yet have answers to.

who is building it

¶ The frontier labs

A handful of organisations train the largest models. They differ less in architecture — almost all are scaled Transformers — than in philosophy: how open to be, how fast to move, how much to weigh capability against safety.

OpenAI
2015 · San Francisco

Founded as a non-profit to ensure AI "benefits all of humanity," now capped-profit. Bet earliest and hardest on scale; built GPT and ChatGPT. Tightly partnered with Microsoft.

flagship · GPT-5, o-series reasoning
Google DeepMind
2010 / merged 2023 · London

The RL pioneers behind AlphaGo and AlphaFold, merged with Google Brain. Deep research bench; strengths in science and multimodality.

flagship · Gemini family
Anthropic
2021 · San Francisco

Founded by ex-OpenAI researchers focused on AI safety. Pioneered Constitutional AI and interpretability research. Builds the Claude models.

flagship · Claude family
Meta AI (FAIR)
2013 · Menlo Park

Champion of open-weight models. The Llama series put capable models in everyone's hands and seeded a vast open ecosystem.

flagship · Llama (open weights)
Mistral AI
2023 · Paris

Europe's frontier contender. Famous for small, efficient, openly-licensed models that punch far above their size.

flagship · Mistral / Mixtral
xAI
2023 · Bay Area

Elon Musk's lab, integrated with X. Scaled to a very large GPU cluster unusually fast; builds the Grok models.

flagship · Grok
DeepSeek
2023 · Hangzhou

Chinese lab that stunned the field with efficient open reasoning models trained at a fraction of expected cost — proof the frontier is not a Western monopoly.

flagship · DeepSeek-R1 (open)

2024–now · the next turn

¶ Reasoning & agents

Two shifts define the current frontier. First, reasoning models (OpenAI's o-series, DeepSeek-R1, and others) are trained — using reinforcement learning, echoing Chapter 04 — to produce a long internal "chain of thought" before answering. Spending more compute at inference time, not just training time, sharply improves maths, code and logic.

Second, agents: models wrapped in loops that let them use tools, call other software, browse, write and run code, and pursue multi-step goals with minimal supervision. The bet is that the next leap comes not from a bigger brain alone, but from giving the brain hands.

the open questions

¶ What we still don't know

Capability has outrun understanding. Honest open problems, held without panic or hype:

  • Alignment. We train models to satisfy a reward proxy, not to share our values. Ensuring powerful systems reliably do what we mean is unsolved.
  • Interpretability. These are billion-parameter black boxes. We are only beginning to read what happens inside them.
  • Truth & hallucination. A next-token predictor optimises for plausibility, not accuracy, and will state falsehoods fluently.
  • Impact. Jobs, education, misinformation, concentration of power, energy use, and the data labour and consent behind training sets.
the honest summary

We have built systems that are astonishingly capable and that we do not fully understand. The history in this almanac is, ultimately, a story of scale producing surprises. The defining question of the next chapter — the one not yet written — is whether we can make those surprises ones we're glad to have.

the throughline

From Turing's tape to a trillion-parameter agent, one idea recurs: don't hand-craft intelligence — build a system that learns, then feed it data and compute. Every winter came from forgetting it; every spring from rediscovering it at a larger scale. You've now seen the whole arc — and driven the machinery behind each turn.
