Why now, and not in 1970

The enabling technology

Most of the core ideas are surprisingly old. What changed wasn't cleverness but scale — three curves bending upward at once. The single most important chart in modern AI is the amount of computation poured into training a model. It has grown by roughly a hundred-billion-fold since the perceptron.

fig.0 — training compute over time

Compute

Moore's law, then GPUs repurposed from gaming, then custom TPUs and AI accelerators. Parallel matrix multiply is the whole game.

Data

The internet became a training set. ImageNet, Common Crawl, code, audio and video gave models trillions of examples to imitate.

Algorithms

Backprop, ReLU, attention, normalisation, Adam, and RLHF — small tricks that, stacked, let very deep networks actually train.

The bitter lesson

In 2019 Rich Sutton argued that the methods which win, in the long run, are the ones that simply scale with compute — general search and learning — not the ones where we hand-craft human knowledge. The history below is, in large part, that lesson being learned the hard way, twice.
