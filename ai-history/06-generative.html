# Generative AI
& Diffusion

The same Transformer that predicts text could be pointed at pixels, audio and instructions. In three years AI stopped merely classifying the world and started generating it — and one training trick turned a clever autocomplete into a helpful assistant the whole world would use.

4 milestones · 1 model DDPM · CLIP · DALL·E · Stable Diffusion · RLHF · ChatGPT live
2020–2021 · text meets image

¶ CLIP and DALL·E

How do you teach a model to draw "an avocado armchair"? First it must know how words and pictures relate. OpenAI's CLIP (2021) trained on 400 million image–caption pairs from the web to place images and text in a shared embedding space (the vector idea from Chapter 03, now spanning two senses). A photo of a dog and the words "a dog" land in the same neighbourhood.

With that shared space as a compass, DALL·E (2021) could generate images from text. Suddenly anyone could conjure pictures of things that had never been photographed. The question was no longer "can it?" but "how good, how fast, and for whom?"

2020 · the method that won

¶ Diffusion

The technique that came to dominate image generation is beautifully counter-intuitive. Take a training image and gradually add random noise until it's pure static — an easy, well-defined process. Now train a network to undo one step of that: given a noisy image, predict the noise to remove. Do this for every noise level.

To generate, start from pure noise and run the network in reverse, peeling away a little noise at each step. Out of the static, an image gradually condenses — guided, if you like, by a text prompt via CLIP. DDPM (2020) made this work; Stable Diffusion (2022) made it efficient and open, putting image generation on ordinary computers and detonating a creative explosion (and a fierce debate about training data and consent).

Lower the reverse steps to ~8 and the result is rougher; more steps give a cleaner image but cost more compute. That speed-vs-quality dial is exactly what real samplers (DDIM, etc.) optimise.
2022 · the alignment trick

¶ RLHF — teaching a model to be helpful

GPT-3 was brilliant but unruly: a pure next-token predictor will happily continue a prompt in unhelpful, false, or toxic ways, because it's imitating the whole internet, not trying to assist you. The fix was RLHF — Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (InstructGPT, 2022):

  • Have humans rank different model responses to the same prompt.
  • Train a reward model to predict those human preferences.
  • Use reinforcement learning (the spirit of Chapter 04) to optimise the language model against that reward — nudging it toward answers people prefer.

This is the bridge between the games chapter and the language chapter: learning from a reward signal, now applied to helpfulness instead of winning Go. It didn't make models smarter so much as aimable — and that made them usable.

30 November 2022

¶ ChatGPT

OpenAI wrapped an RLHF-tuned GPT-3.5 in a chat box and released it as a "low-key research preview." It reached one million users in five days and 100 million in two months — the fastest-adopted consumer product in history. Nothing about the underlying model was radically new; the leap was access. For the first time, anyone could talk to a frontier AI in plain language.

The technology had existed for two years. The interface had existed for two months. The difference was the difference.

The modern era — the gold rush, the lab race, the boardrooms and the anxieties — begins the moment that chat box went live.

Carry-forward

Generation + alignment turned AI from a back-end classifier into a front-and-centre product. The final chapter is about what happened next: who's building it, what it can actually do today, and the questions we now have to answer.
