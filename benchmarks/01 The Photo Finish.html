Seven contestants — every Fable 5 setting above low, three generations of Opus, and Sonnet 4.6 — hit a perfect 100.0 across both tracks. Haiku 4.5 and Fable-low missed a single task each. Even GPT-5.5's best configurations landed at 97. The arena was built to be hard: zebra puzzles with provably unique solutions, programs you must trace by hand, constraint bundles with up to eight simultaneous rules. It did not matter. The frontier ate it.

When the top half of the field is indistinguishable from perfect, your scoreboard is no longer measuring intelligence. It's measuring the test.

ACT II // THE CHAMPIONSHIP — BREAKING THE TIE

A benchmark that everyone aces is a broken instrument. So the arena was rebuilt overnight, knobs turned to championship spec: six-house puzzles with a fifth attribute category, programs with mutual recursion and aliasing traps, ~13 simultaneous format constraints, math chains of twenty operations. Same machinery, new seeds, adversarially reviewed by 34 fresh agents before any model saw a task.

* codex output-token counts exclude some reasoning tokens; cross-vendor token comparisons are indicative only. Single-run cells: differences under ~2 points are noise.

The tie cracked — from the bottom. Opus 4.5, flawless at base tier, dropped two program-tracing tasks while burning 3.8× Fable-medium's tokens trying. Sonnet 4.6 held 99.8 but needed 3× the wall-clock. And the three contestants still standing at 100.0 are now separated by exactly one thing: fable-medium delivers the same perfect score at half the reasoning budget of Opus 4.7 and 4.8.

Difficulty scaled the spend, not the ranking. Every model paid 2–4× more tokens; the order never moved.
