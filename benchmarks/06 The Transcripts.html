The same model family, one notch of reasoning effort apart, on the same 11-step warehouse problem. One carries the right intermediate through the final swap. One doesn't.

Both did the hard arithmetic correctly. The cheaper run grabbed the wrong number at the last step — a slip a few hundred extra tokens of checking erased.
HEAT 3 // OUTPUT-04 — THE MEMO TRAP, CAUGHT IN THE ACT

The most-missed task family in the whole arena. Both models below computed every printed value correctly. They disagree on one number: how many times the function was called.

The function increments its counter on every invocation — including the ones that hit the memo cache and return immediately. Count those and you get 25. GPT-5.5 counted only the calls that did fresh work and reported 22. It made this exact undercount at every effort level; see the live step-through in Chapter 03.

TAKEAWAY

Three heats, three lessons. Heat 1: when the answer is free, the price is the thinking — and it varies 3–5× between minds that score identically. Heat 2: the cheapest correct run and the cheapest wrong run are separated by a single careless step, which is why self-verification is the real frontier. Heat 3: the surviving errors are tiny, specific, and shared across families. That's the whole thesis of Chapter 04, written in transcripts instead of charts.
