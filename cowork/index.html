The new part · Cowork

Handing off, not hovering

The chat window keeps you present for every line. Cowork is built for the work you don't want to sit through — the long, patient reading and first-drafting that never quite reaches the top of your day.

Its own private room

Each task runs in a sealed, temporary workspace, created for the job and wiped when it's done. One matter's papers aren't mixed with another's, and nothing is left lying around afterwards.

Several matters at once

Hand it a stack of jobs and they run side by side, each in its own room. Summarising the bundle, pulling the clauses and drafting the letter don't have to queue behind each other — or behind you.

On a standing schedule

Set it to do something every morning — scan a court list, watch the news on a client, produce a Monday summary of the week ahead — and the work is sitting there finished when you arrive.

Works from a brief

You don't chat it through step by step. You describe the task once — the papers, the question, the format — and it gets on with it, coming back with a draft rather than a conversation.

Shows its working

You get the reasoning and the sources behind every point, not a black box — so you can check each quote and citation against the original before anything is relied on or leaves your hands.

Meets you where you are

Start a task from your laptop or your phone, close the tab and get on with your day. It keeps working without you, and gives you a nudge when there's something to look at.

The handoff

One question, three moves

You don't pick one gear for good. A single piece of work slides along the dial — you think it through, hand off the grind, then take back the last word. The judgement stays yours.

The dial isn't a menu you choose from once. It's a gearbox — you shift up as the task gets clearer and you need your hands on it less.

One thing to keep in mind

Claude drafts and researches; it doesn't practise law, and it can be confidently wrong. Treat everything it produces as a first draft for a qualified professional to review — read it, and check every quote, citation and figure against the source before you rely on it or send it out. The value is in the hours of legwork it saves you, not in taking your judgement off the hook.
