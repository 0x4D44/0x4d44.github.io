Review log

How The Locked Room is reviewed, and how each round scored.

Method

An independent AI reviewer, given no knowledge of earlier rounds, reads the whole page and its code, verifies a sample of at least 30 factual claims (some by running code, some from documentation), tests the interactive widgets, and scores eight criteria from 0 to 10. Each verified error deducts from technical accuracy (critical 2, major 1, minor 0.3). The bar: weighted score above 9.0 and no criterion below 8. Reviews are by an AI model and sometimes had no web access, so they are a filter, not an audit.

Rubric

#CriterionWeightA 10 looks like
1Technical accuracy25%Every claim is correct, version numbers and semantics included; hedged where uncertain; nothing misleading.
2Coverage and depth20%All requested topics (Landlock, seccomp, resource limits, no-fork, sandboxes, unikernels, eBPF, Firecracker, cut-down Linux, language choice and dependency reduction) covered with mechanism, limits and trade-offs, not name-drops.
3Actionability15%A reader can apply it: correct commands, configs and code, ordering, and pitfalls.
4Security judgement15%States limits and bypasses plainly, defence-in-depth framing, correct threat-model reasoning, no false assurance.
5Plain English and structure10%Follows the Plain English Campaign guide: sentences average 15 to 20 words, active verbs, "you" and "we", everyday words, jargon explained at first use, lists for runs of items. Logical flow and easy to scan. Free of stock AI phrasing: "not X but Y" contrasts, "honest"/"truth" framing, vivid filler metaphors, em dashes.
6Interactive and visual aids5%Widgets and diagrams are correct and teach something that prose cannot.
7Engineering quality5%Works on phone and desktop, light and dark; accessible; no console errors; code samples compile.
8Sourcing and verifiability5%Claims can be traced to documentation, CVE records or papers; examples are tested rather than merely asserted.
