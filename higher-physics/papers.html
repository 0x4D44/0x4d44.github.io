The wardroom · past-paper practice

Past papers: sit the questions

The Exam drill taught you how the paper is built and marked. This deck lets you sit questions and be marked. Pick a tab, write or choose your answers, press Mark my answer, and the page scores you step by step against a marking scheme (relationship, substitution, answer with unit for a calculation; the key ideas for a written answer), shows the model answer and keeps your running totals. When you are ready, draw a half-length mock paper from every tab and run the clock.

Read this first: original questions, not the real papers

Every question here was written for this site, in the style of the 2022 to 2025 Higher Physics question papers: the same three areas in roughly the same proportions, the same command words (state, describe, explain, calculate, show that, suggest), the same habit of setting a context and asking several short parts, and the same mix of multiple-choice, calculation, graph, experiment and open-ended questions. None is copied from a past paper, and none predicts what will be asked. All of them use only the data sheet and relationships sheet printed on the Exam drill page. The real papers and their marking instructions are on the Qualifications Scotland website, and you should work through those as well.

The automatic marker looks for the relationship, your numbers, the final number with its unit, and the key ideas and key words of a written answer, the way a marker scans for them. It is a practice aid, not an examiner: it can miss good working laid out in an unusual way, so after every written answer or calculation you can tick the steps you did make.

How to use the marks

Work each question on paper first, then type in your answer. Mark with the model answer beside you. A calculation earns its marks for the relationship, the substitution and the final answer with the correct unit; a wrong or missing unit loses the final mark. For one-number answers the unit is printed beside the box, so type just the number (or add a unit with its prefix, such as mA, and it must be right). Be strict: if you made the idea but not the key word, ask whether a marker reading only your words would give the mark.
