Briefing room · read before your first watch

Welcome aboard, Cadet.

This is a complete, interactive guide to Higher Physics, the course awarded by Qualifications Scotland. It covers every topic in the current course specification, explained from National 5 upwards, with diagrams, live simulations you can play with, worked examples marked the way examiners mark them, and a pre-flight check at the end of every chapter.

You will learn it with 1805 (Blockhaven) Squadron, a flight of RAF Air Cadets posted for sea training aboard HMS Redstone, a 104-gun ship of the line from Nelson's navy built entirely from Minecraft blocks. Their sister crew already wrote up Higher Computing Science. This time the squadron has the ship's guns, its signal lanterns, its telescope and its redstone, and every one of them turns out to be a physics lesson. A cannonball is a projectile. A minecart crash is a collision. The light from a distant galaxy is stretched like the note of a passing ship's bell.

Every topic in all three areas can appear anywhere in the question paper, so there is no "safe" topic to skip. The skills chapter matters everywhere: uncertainties, significant figures and graph work turn up in calculations across the paper and earn a large share of the assignment marks.

How you are assessed

  • Question paper (2 hours 30 minutes, 130 marks), set and marked by Qualifications Scotland. Section 1 has 20 multiple-choice questions, each with five options A to E. Section 2 has 110 marks of structured questions: calculations, explanations, graphs, experimental design, and two open-ended "use your knowledge of physics to comment on…" questions. You are given a data sheet and a relationships sheet. You do not need to memorise the equations. You do need to know which one to choose and what every symbol means.
  • Assignment (20 marks, scaled to 33). You plan and carry out an experiment, find data from a second source, and write a report under controlled conditions. The report is marked by Qualifications Scotland. See the assignment chapter for exactly where the 20 marks come from.
⚠ New for 2026–27

Until 2025–26, Higher Physics had two question papers: a 45-minute multiple-choice paper (25 marks) and a 2 hour 15 minute paper (130 marks, scaled to 95). The assignment was scaled to 30. From session 2026–27 there is one 2 hour 30 minute paper with 20 multiple-choice marks and 110 other marks. The course content has not changed. Past papers are still excellent practice, but expect fewer multiple-choice questions.

How to use this site

  1. Work through each page in order. Each chapter matches a topic in the course specification (look for the small ODU · … tags) and builds on the one before.
  2. Play with every simulation. Fire the cannon, crash the minecarts, push the electron, bend the light. Change one thing at a time and predict what will happen before you look. That is exactly the skill the exam tests.
  3. Cover the worked examples. Try each one yourself first, then open the solution. Solutions are written the way markers want them: relationship, substitution, answer with unit.
  4. Learn the definitions in the boxed cards word for word. The 2025 course report says candidates "continued to find questions testing recall of facts and definitions challenging".
  5. Pass the ✈ pre-flight check at the end of each chapter. Multiple-choice questions mark themselves. For open questions you write an answer, reveal the marking guidance and mark yourself honestly.
  6. Finish with Skills and the Exam drill: uncertainties, graphs, the assignment, command words, timing and mixed exam-style questions.

Your rank

Every pre-flight check you pass counts towards promotion through the Air Cadet ranks, from Cadet to Cadet Warrant Officer. Progress is saved in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Ship's glossary

None of these words will be in your exam. They are the period flavour, explained so they never get in the way.

Ship of the line / first-rate
The biggest warships of Nelson's era, big enough to fight in the main battle line. A first-rate carried 100 or more guns on three gun decks.
Shot / round shot
A solid iron cannonball. A "32-pounder" fired shot weighing 32 pounds, about 14.5 kg.
Broadside
All the guns on one side of a ship fired together.
Bow chaser
A gun at the front (bow) of the ship that fires forwards, used when chasing another ship.
Recoil / breeching rope
A gun leaps backwards when it fires. The thick breeching rope stops it, and the time it takes to stop sets the force on the rope.
Quarterdeck
The raised deck at the stern from which the captain commands.
Orlop deck
The lowest deck, below the waterline, where stores were kept and the surgeon worked.
Magazine / cartridge
The magazine is the protected store room for gunpowder, deep in the ship. A cartridge is a cloth bag holding one charge of powder for a gun.
Capstan
A huge upright drum turned by sailors pushing on bars, used to haul up the anchor.
Purser / quartermaster
The purser kept the ship's accounts and stores; the quartermaster helped steer and kept stores of equipment.
Slipway
The sloping ramp on which a ship is built and launched.
Beat to quarters
The drum signal for every sailor to go to their battle station.
Crow's nest / masthead
The lookout's position high on the mainmast.
Leyden jar
A glass jar coated inside and out with metal foil, invented in 1745: the first capacitor.
Signal lantern
A lamp used to send messages between ships at night.
Air Cadets, squadron, flight
The RAF Air Cadets is a youth organisation for 12 to 20 year olds. Cadets belong to a local numbered squadron, which is split into smaller groups called flights.
Grob Tutor
The small two-seat training aircraft in which Air Cadets get flying experience.
Parade ground
The open square where cadets drill and march.
Redstone, comparator, minecart
Minecraft items: a dust that carries signals like wire, a block that compares two signal strengths and switches by the result, and a cart that runs on rails.
Light room
A small cabin beside a warship's magazine. A lantern shone into the magazine through a glass window, so the gunners could see to work in the magazine without any naked flame near the powder.
Watch, night watch
A sailor's spell of duty (usually four hours). "Night watch" is also this site's dark-mode button.
Blockhaven, 1805 Squadron
The made-up Minecraft town and Air Cadet squadron of this site. 1805 is the year of Trafalgar.
Courier craft
A thought-experiment spacecraft used in the relativity section. Nothing in 1805 flies near light speed, but the physics is real.
