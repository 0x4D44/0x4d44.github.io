About this map

The Peopling of the Earth is a model, not a dataset. Global population totals and the share of each macro-region are anchored to published estimates; those aggregates are then spread across fixed geographic settlement nodes and overlaid with the rise and fall of history's great cities, so the heat-map shows roughly where people lived, not just how many.

Sources

  • Deep history — McEvedy & Jones, Atlas of World Population History; the HYDE 3.2/3.3 database.
  • Regional shares — Maddison Project; Our World in Data.
  • Great cities — Chandler, Four Thousand Years of Urban Growth, with Modelski/Morris.
  • Modern era & projections — UN World Population Prospects 2024 (medium variant), peaking near 10.3 billion around 2084.

Caveats

Figures before 1 CE carry wide uncertainty (often ±30%). Numbers after 2100 are a single stabilise-then-decline scenario; after 2200 they are openly speculative, not a forecast. Most mortality events were modest checks on the global total — pick an affected region (e.g. Europe for the Black Death, Latin America for the post-Columbian collapse) to see them as real dips. Event markers are sized by estimated death toll; hover one for detail.
