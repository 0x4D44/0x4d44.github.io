## 2. Cab and what the driver sees
- Metro-Cammell front (photo): a **flat-ish front with THREE panes: driver's window (left), a central door with window, and a second window (right)**; the driver sits **left of the centre door**, close behind the glass (eyes ~0.8-1.0 m back, ESTIMATE). Desk height ~0.95-1.0 m, windscreen ~0.7 x 0.55 m (ESTIMATE from the TSC frame).
- Stadler (thumbnail of Down The Line Rail + Commons front views): **large black-framed glazing in three bays (left, centre door, right) with a yellow/amber LED destination display above (e.g. "Buchanan Street")**, a black lower nose panel with the roundel; white body above. The cab behind it is a **temporary driver compartment formed by a partition wall** (Rail Engineer https://www.railengineer.co.uk/subway-revival-glasgow-to-introduce-uto/); the glass is **blacked out for driver privacy** (https://busandtrainuser.com/2024/01/18/a-ride-on-glasgows-new-subway-trains/). Only one end has a full cab; the other end has a fold-out auxiliary cab (Commons J33 notes). Layout inside is not published: ESTIMATE compact desk with 2-3 small screens, master controller left, door buttons right, "ATO start" button (drivers "close the doors and press start" - Wikipedia).
- View: at normal height the driver sees a black circular tunnel ~3.35 m across with **rails converging, pale headlight pool on the invert and walls ~30-60 m ahead (ESTIMATE)**, brick/cast-iron ring joints; stations appear as a **bright end wall with the platform edge running alongside the cab on one side**. Driver's position is **left of the centre line**, so the platform for a given loop appears on the **left** (left-hand running; island platform between the two tubes - for the clockwise Outer loop the island is on the left).

## 3. Tunnel lighting
- **User is right: the running tunnels are essentially unlit** - dark except train headlights. Evidence: Scottish Daily Express evacuation account (https://www.scottishdailyexpress.co.uk/news/scottish-news/drama-glasgow-subway-breaks-down-30422989): passengers walked 5-10 min in the dark tunnel "emergency lights were on and staff had torches"; "slabs missing, rough underfoot"; water audible in storm drains under the walkway; an Inner Circle train passed in the neighbouring tube. So: **emergency lighting exists but is sparse/dim and only on in an incident**; a **cess walkway of paving slabs** beside the track; drains below.
- Other fittings (Rail Engineer https://www.railengineer.co.uk/subway-revival-an-update-on-glasgows-modernisation/): 21 pumping stations (tunnel low points), ~120 km of redundant cable removed, **new chainage markers with RFID datum plates** (small plates on the wall - CBTC balises), drip sheets, grouted/resin-sealed lining. Cross-passages/refuge niches are **not documented** (ESTIMATE: few or none; two separate single tubes ~6 m apart, evacuation is along the tube to the next station).
- Platform ends: bright station lighting stops at the end wall; the bore beyond is black.

## 4. Station/tunnel transition geometry
- Running tunnels: circular 3.35 m, lining brick/concrete/cast iron (see train-dimensions.md). At each station each tube enters a **circular opening in a flat end wall, ringed by 2-3 brick courses (St Enoch, Partick, Govan, Hillhead photos)**, or a plain arched mouth (SGX/Kelvinbridge twin tubes visible at the far end). No flared portal; the station vault is a separate larger barrel (island stations: twin ribbed vaults + central flat soffit; box stations: flat ceilings and columns). Rails run continuously, platform edge ~0.1 m from the train and 0.695 m above rail.
- **Name signs**: (a) a repeated **name band along the wall** (orange outer / charcoal inner) every ~10-15 m at ~2.2-2.6 m above platform; (b) **hanging black boards** with white bilingual text (e.g. "Buchanan Street / Sraid a' Bhuchain") and the roundel, suspended from the ceiling over the platform (Down The Line Rail thumbnail), plus hanging "Inner | Outer" blocks (charcoal | orange) at platform entrances; (c) line-diagram panels on the vault/wall (see stations-look.md).

## 5. Signalling
- Before 1977: station-section block signalling with **two-aspect red/green exit signals** at each station and an extra pair at the Clyde tunnel (railforums post - anecdotal). 1996: Vital Processor Interlocking + new supervisory system (Wikipedia). Modern (new fleet, being commissioned): **CBTC (Hitachi/Ansaldo)** - on-board controller, tachometer + balises (RFID datum plates), zone controller issues movement authority, enforces speed limits, ATO and door control (Rail Engineer UTO article). In CBTC no lineside signals are needed; **whether small legacy green/red lamps remain at tunnel mouths is unverified** (the TSC rendering has a green lamp; use it as a harmless cue). Trip arms/train stops: not documented. Speed boards: none documented (ESTIMATE: ATO enforces limits).
- Headway normally 4 min, 90 s under UTO; max speed 58 km/h; old cable ran at a constant ~19 km/h.

## 6. Ride feel / sound (mostly ESTIMATE + comparisons)
- Stadler: "much quieter than the Metro-Cammell, smoother, faster acceleration" (https://scottishtrains.zenfolio.com/blog/2024/2/glasgow-subway-new-stadler-trains-27-12-23); air suspension and resilient wheels (Railway Technology). Pre-1977 cars "shoogled" (loose bodies). Interior is air-conditioned, bright, no audio-visual next-station display at launch; door-side white lights flash and a ~4 s delay follows the door chime (busandtrainuser).
- Suggested sim treatment (ESTIMATE): steady 55-65 dB rumble rising with speed in the narrow bore (strong tunnel reverberation, close walls), light low-frequency sway (<0.1 g) and small pitch on gradient changes, flange squeal on the tightest curves (~200 m radius) and a distinct echo when passing station mouths; warm, stale air; headlight glow on the rings; hiss from air systems; door chime then pause then doors.

## 7. People
- From the POV thumbnails (Passing Stranger, real Stadler interiors): everyday winter wear - padded/puffer jackets, parkas, jeans, hoodies, backpacks and shoulder bags, headphones, phones in hand; mixed ages incl. students and commuters, tartan scarf/ zebra-print coat etc. **Seated passengers face each other across a very narrow aisle (about 1.2 m; knees nearly meet)**; standing riders hold orange poles with their heads close to the curved ceiling; busy trains have people standing in the aisle. On platforms people stand behind the yellow/tactile strip, wait quietly in singles/pairs, board after alighting passengers (PSD signs "allow passengers to exit before boarding"); football-match days at Ibrox bring large crowds in club colours.
- Crowd/people models can therefore be: mostly dark/neutral jackets, 10% bright colours, phones, bags.
