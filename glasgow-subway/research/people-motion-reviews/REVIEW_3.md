- **Cause** (`py_resamp.py`). ### 1c. Trying to break `atAnalysisRate` (`js/edge3.mjs`)
- Non-integer ratios (1/100, 1/90, 1/75, 1/61, 1/60.5): resampled, and low as above. The resampler is exact on a ramp at 1/61, 1/90, 1/100, 1/120, 1/144 and 1/1000 for lengths 100, 101, 1020, 1021 (the last sample is where it should be). 1/59.94 is left alone by design. A dt within 1e-9 of 1/60 is left alone; 1/60 - 2e-9 is resampled. So the item is a step function of dt at 60 Hz; it is steep rather than vertical because (by arithmetic) a 1020-frame window drifts a whole frame only when the rate is at least 0.06 Hz off, and so sweeps every phase; measured on the as-if device, 60.1 Hz already reads 0.74.
- Short or odd input: fewer than 3 samples returns the input. 3-5 samples at 100-144 Hz resample to 1-3 samples; with fewer than 3 the new p99 is NaN, which `fall` scores 0, where the old read 0 and scored 10. dt = 0 gives NaN (old Infinity; both score 0); a negative dt throws a RangeError (old a number); dt = NaN or Infinity returns the input. None is a real recorder input.
- NaN is unguarded in both versions (the sort comparator): in a toy 144 Hz series one NaN sample moved the new p99 from 2.16 to 1.44 and left the old at 5.18. The recorder emits no NaN. A stalled game (20 repeated frames) changed nothing.
- Below 60 Hz the series is left alone and reads low (0.89 at 50 Hz, 0.51 at 30), as the author says.
- A rate-free version would need a quantity the grid cannot see (for example the largest change of vertical velocity over a fixed 50 ms, or the p99 after a fixed zero-phase low-pass) and a band recalibrated on it. Not tried.

Before the note is quoted, four sentences should change: "stable from 60 to 144 Hz", "could not raise the figures further", "I have not been able to make that better honestly", "whatever the recording rate".
