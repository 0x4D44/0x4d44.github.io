A rounding error · winter 1961

The forecast that fell apart

Edward Lorenz, a meteorologist at MIT, was running a stripped-down weather model on a Royal McBee LGP-30 — a desk-sized vacuum-tube computer. To pick up a run from the middle, he retyped a number from the printout: 0.506, where the machine had quietly been carrying 0.506127. Same equations, same machine, a difference of one part in a thousand.

The two forecasts held hands for a while — then peeled apart into completely different weather. Nothing was broken. The world was simply that sensitive.

Deterministic nonperiodic flow · 1963

Bounded, and still unknowable

Two years later Lorenz boiled convection down to three equations and three numbers — σ=10, ρ=28, β=8⁄3 — and published Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow. The trajectory never settles onto a point and never closes into a loop, yet it never leaves a bounded region: it winds forever around two wings, crossing between them in an order that never repeats. Stable as an object; unpredictable in its detail.

Two states differing by imperceptible amounts may eventually evolve into two considerably different states. Edward N. Lorenz, 1963

The metaphor · 1972

The butterfly

The name came later. For a 1972 talk Lorenz needed a title and — as the story goes — the session's convener supplied one: Does the flap of a butterfly's wings in Brazil set off a tornado in Texas? The claim was never that butterflies push weather around. It was that in a system like this, the smallest unmeasured detail sets a hard horizon on how far ahead anyone can ever see. You can watch that horizon arrive.

What this is

A self-contained instrument. Every trajectory is integrated live in your browser with fourth-order Runge–Kutta — nothing is pre-rendered and nothing phones home. Seven canonical systems, each drawn in its own phosphor colour. The glow is just density: overlapping passes of the pen add up into light.

The equations and parameter sets are the standard published values; the history is drawn from Lorenz's 1963 paper and the usual retellings. Where a detail is folklore — the butterfly title, the exact keystrokes — it is flagged as reported rather than certain.
