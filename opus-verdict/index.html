How the verdict is reached — and how much to trust it

Opus 4.8 Verdict runs two detectors, both entirely on your own device. The default is an instant, transparent heuristic that measures the writing-style fingerprints large language models tend to leave and adds them up — a few milliseconds of JavaScript, and it shows exactly why. The optional ⚡ Neural model is a genuinely trained classifier for a stronger, second opinion.

The heuristic — the signals it weighs

Each signal returns a 0–100 "how machine-like" score; the overall verdict is their weighted average.

Burstiness — humans mix very short sentences with long ones; models pace themselves evenly. Low variance in sentence length is the single strongest tell. Machine vocabulary — the density of the words and phrases LLMs over-reach for (delve, tapestry, testament to, navigate the complexities, it's important to note…). Transition density — over-signposting with moreover, furthermore, however. Em-dash habit — the tell-tale over-use of the em-dash. Formulaic openers — many sentences starting the same way (The…, This…, It is…). Rule-of-three — the model's love of tidy three-item lists. Hedging and lexical repetition round it out.

The neural model — a real classifier, in your browser

The ⚡ button loads a fine-tuned MiniLM transformer (a 22 M-parameter encoder) and runs it locally through transformers.js + ONNX Runtime (WebAssembly). The library, the runtime and the ~22 MB int8-quantised weights are all served from this page — nothing is uploaded and no external service is called; the first run fetches ~43 MB once, then it's cached and inference takes ~100 ms. It was trained on the public HC3 corpus — tens of thousands of matched human answers and ChatGPT answers to the same questions, so it learns style rather than topic.

On held-out test data it separates human from machine text with ROC-AUC ≈ 0.999, and that holds on a domain it never saw in training (medical Q&A) — so it's learning genuine style, not one topic. It even fixes the heuristic's blind spot: human "corporate-speak" that the heuristic frets over, the model confidently reads as human.

Both are a signal, not proof. The heuristic can over-flag polished human prose; the neural model is trained on ChatGPT-era text, so it's strongest on that family and its confidence doesn't perfectly transfer to every new writing domain or newer model. Use either to raise a question, never to settle one — no detector should be the sole basis for an accusation.
