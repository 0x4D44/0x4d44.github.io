The model lab: build one and run it.

Everything from the first two chapters, assembled into a working three-dimensional engine you can rotate, zoom and drive. Change the cylinder count, the layout, the cycle and the fuel, and watch the crankshaft, rods, pistons, valves and sparks move exactly as the kinematics dictate.

Orientation

One crank angle, the whole machine.

The model is driven by a single rotating value: crankshaft angle. From it, every other motion is derived, not animated by hand. Each cylinder is assigned a crank throw and a firing phase exactly as a real engine's firing order demands; the piston position is the closed-form slider-crank solution you met in Ch. 01; the connecting rod simply spans from the wandering crank pin up to the piston pin, swinging as it must.

Because the geometry is real, the model shows you things diagrams hide: how the rods of a V-engine crowd onto shared crank pins, how a flat-six's pistons box outward in perfect opposition, and why a long crankshaft with many throws is a torsional spring waiting to resonate (Ch. 04).

Experiments

Things worth trying.

  • Drop to a single cylinder, 120 rpm. Watch how long the crank coasts between power strokes — the job the flywheel does (Ch. 04).
  • Inline-4 vs flat-4. Same count, very different motion. The boxer's opposed pistons cancel primary shaking forces the inline-four cannot.
  • V configuration, 6 and 8 cylinders. See opposing rods share a crank pin, and how the bank angle sets the rhythm of firing.
  • Switch to two-stroke. The valves shift to a uniflow timing near BDC, and every revolution now fires. Note how much "busier" the engine looks at the same rpm.
  • Petrol → Diesel. The plug in the head changes to an injector, and the flash colour and timing shift from a sharp yellow spark to the orange of compression ignition.
  • Wind the speed to 6,000 rpm. The reciprocating parts blur, the engine glows red-hot — and then it grenades. The inertia loads (∝ rpm²) of Ch. 04 are what ultimately limit engine speed; here you get to find the limit the fun way.
Glossary in motion

The parts, named.

Crankshaft & throws

The output shaft. Each throw (offset pin + webs + counterweight) converts a rod's push into torque. Counterweights balance the rotating mass of the throw and big-end.

Connecting rod

Joins piston (small end) to crank pin (big end). It both pushes and swings, so it carries bending as well as compression — and its mass is split into "reciprocating" and "rotating" shares in Ch. 04.

Piston & bore

The sliding seal that catches the gas pressure. The translucent ghost cylinder is the bore; toggle it off to see the internals clearly.

Valves & head

In four-stroke mode, intake (blue) and exhaust (purple) poppet valves open and close on cam timing. The head carries them and the spark/injector.

Flywheel

The heavy disc on the end of the crank. It stores the energy of each power stroke and releases it through the idle strokes — the smoothing element of Ch. 04.

Spark / injector

The yellow flash (petrol) or orange flash (diesel) marks ignition, fired just before TDC on the power stroke of each cylinder in turn.
