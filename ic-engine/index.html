How the internal combustion engine turns a measured fire into smooth rotating power — the cycle, the thermodynamics, the forces, and a century of engineering them better.

An engine must take a fuel that burns in milliseconds and deliver torque so steady you can sip coffee while it does. Between those two facts lies everything: the flywheel, the firing order, the balance shaft, the turbo. This study takes the machine apart, thermodynamically and mechanically — and lets you drive a working model of it.

How to read this study

One question, asked eight ways.

Every chapter circles the same engineering problem: how do you turn the violent, intermittent release of chemical energy into smooth, controllable, durable rotating power?

The cycle answers it in principle; thermodynamics sets the ceiling; the forces of reciprocating mass fight you the whole way; configuration, balance and the flywheel tame them; forced induction pushes past the limits; and a hundred years of wear, failure and refinement decide what actually survives in service.

Diagrams are interactive where you see the orange signal colour — drag, toggle, and drive the models. Chapter 03 is a fully rotatable 3D engine you can run. All figures are schematic.
