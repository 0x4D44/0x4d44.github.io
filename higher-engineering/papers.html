The Admiralty Board · past-paper practice

Past papers: the gunroom drill

The Exam deck taught you how the question paper is marked. This deck lets you sit questions and be marked. Pick an area, enter or choose your answers, press Mark my answer, and the page scores you against a marking scheme point by point, shows the worked or model answer, and keeps your running totals. When you are ready, draw a half-length mock paper from all five areas and run the clock, with the Data Booklet open beside you.

Read this first: original questions, not the real papers

Every question here was written for this site, in the style of the recent Higher Engineering Science question papers (2022 to 2026): the same five areas in roughly the same proportions, the same command words (state, describe, explain, calculate, determine, compare), the same habit of setting an engineering context and asking several short parts that build on each other. None is copied from a past paper, and none predicts what will be asked. The real papers and their marking instructions are on the Qualifications Scotland subject page (the SQA past-paper archive keeps up to five years), and you should work through those as well.

Numerical answers are marked by value, so you may type working, a unit or a power of ten (the last number counts). Written answers are marked by a program that looks for the key ideas and key words in each marking point, the way a marker scans for them. It is a practice aid, not an examiner: it can miss a good answer put in unusual words, so after every written answer you can tick the points you did make.

How to use the marks

Mark each question with the worked answer beside you, not from memory. A part of 2 marks wants two separate, developed points. For explain, give the cause and the effect. For a calculation, write the formula, the substitution and the answer with its unit. Round only at the end, and give 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
