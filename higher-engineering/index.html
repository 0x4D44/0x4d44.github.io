Briefing room · read before your first watch

Welcome aboard, Cadet.

This is a complete, interactive guide to Higher Engineering Science, the Scottish course that treats engineering as technology, science and mathematics working together. It is for anyone in S5 or S6 who has enjoyed National 5 Engineering Science, wants to design and understand real systems (circuits, drives, frames, programs) and would like to see how a mechanism, a transistor and a truss all come down to a few clear ideas. It covers every skill in the course specification, one deck at a time, with diagrams, simulations you can drive, worked examples set out the way markers like, and a short check at the end of each chapter.

You will learn it with 1805 (Blockhaven) Squadron, the Air Cadets who already wrote up Higher Maths, Higher Physics and Higher Computing Science. This time they are posted to HMS Ratchet, a 74-gun ship of the line built entirely from Minecraft blocks: iron-block fittings, redstone wiring and pistons. A ship of the line is a floating engineering project. Its capstan is a gear train, its steering is a feedback loop, its signal lamps are a binary code, its masts are a structure under load, and its pumps and guns run on pistons and valves. Every one of those turns up in this course.

Study timeAbout 160 hours of learning, including time to prepare for assessment
Usually takenS5 or S6. The recommended entry is National 5 Engineering Science (or equivalent). Higher leads on to HNC and HND courses, apprenticeships and engineering degrees.

Three course themes, six decks

The course specification groups everything under three themes: engineering contexts and challenges (the systems approach, energy and efficiency, the roles of engineers, and the impacts of engineering), electronics and control (analogue, digital and programmable) and mechanisms and structures. The question paper then samples six areas. This site gives each area a deck, and adds a deck for the exam and the assignment. The mark figures on each card are the question-paper ranges printed in the specification.

Exam & assignment

The Data Booklet, rounding and units, descriptive-answer technique, a mock paper, and how to earn the 50 assignment marks.

The ranges are the specification's, not a mark scheme. They overlap and do not add up to a single figure: the smallest ends sum to 78 and the largest to 136, and the paper is exactly 110 marks. In any one year the real split falls somewhere inside the ranges, so no area is safe to skip. A single question often mixes areas too: a power-and-efficiency calculation can sit inside a drive-system question.

How you are assessed

  • Question paper (2 hours 30 minutes, 110 marks), set and marked by Qualifications Scotland. Section 1 has 20 marks of short-answer questions. Section 2 has 90 marks of structured questions. Expect calculations, short or limited responses and extended responses. The Data Booklet is supplied, and the rounding instructions are printed on the front cover of the paper. You do not have to write code in a programmable-control question, but a flowchart must have enough detail: input and output pin numbers and a delay unit.
  • Assignment (50 marks), set each year by Qualifications Scotland and marked externally. You work on your own, for 8 hours, in a supervised classroom, with no teacher guidance. It is closed-book apart from the Data Booklet. It is a problem-solving activity made of several tasks, which may be linked by a theme. Your evidence goes in on paper: completed solutions, simulation printouts, photographs of your built models, and records of your testing and evaluation. There is no word count.

The 2026 course report explains that Qualifications Scotland aims to set assessments so that a competent candidate scores at least 50% of the marks (the notional grade C boundary) and a well-prepared, very competent candidate at least 70% (the notional grade A boundary), then holds a boundary meeting to make the final decision. How to use this site

  1. Work through the decks in order if you can. Start with Systems & energy: feedback, energy and efficiency come back everywhere. Then do Analogue before Digital & programmable, because motor control and PWM use transistors. Drives & pneumatics and Structures & materials are largely independent of the electronics, so you can take them in either order. Finish with Exam & assignment.
  2. Keep the Data Booklet open beside you. The exam gives you the booklet, so you do not need to memorise the formulae in it. You do need to know which one to use, what each symbol means, and which units go in. The decks use the booklet's symbols, so you get used to the ones the exam uses. Notice what is not in the booklet (for example the base–emitter voltage, 0·7 V): the question will give it to you, and the worked examples say so.
  3. Play with every widget. Drag the sliders, press the buttons, drive the circuit or the mechanism. Before you change anything, predict what will happen, then check. That habit is what handles an unfamiliar exam question.
  4. Try the worked examples yourself first. Cover the solution, write your own working, then open it. The marks are shown as •1, •2 … in the order a marker would look for them, with the total beside “Show solution”. These are typical mark allocations, written to show what earns marks. They are not reproductions of official marking instructions.
  5. Follow the calculation rules every time. Never round part-way through: carry the full calculator value and round only the final answer (to the number of significant figures asked for, or three if none is stated). Use the π key. Write the unit on every final answer, and convert (rev/min to rev/s, mm² to m², kN to N) before you substitute.
  6. Pass the ✈ pre-flight check at the end of each chapter. Multiple-choice questions mark themselves. For open questions you write your answer, reveal the marking list and mark yourself honestly.
  7. Practise writing, not just calculating. Descriptions and evaluations are where marks go missing. In pneumatics, for instance, say that a 3/2 valve actuates, that a 5/2 valve changes state, and that a piston instrokes or outstrokes. The Exam & assignment deck covers this too.
✎ A good habit for the assignment

Photographs and screenshots must be clear, and every claim in your evaluation should point to evidence: a test result, an amendment you made, or a specification point you can show was met. “It all worked” earns nothing without that evidence.

Your rank

Every pre-flight check you pass counts towards promotion through the Air Cadet ranks, from Cadet to Cadet Warrant Officer. Progress is saved in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Decoding the theme

The squadron, the ship and the blocks are scenery. They are there to give you something to picture, and every one of them maps to a real idea in the course. Here is the decoder.

You'll see……which stands for
🔴 Redstone dust, strength 0 to 15An analogy for an analogue signal. Redstone has only 16 steps, whereas a real analogue signal can take any value in a continuous range, such as a voltage from a sensor (Analogue electronics).
🔦 Redstone torches and repeatersDigital on/off signals. A redstone torch inverts its input, so it is a NOT gate; wire several and you build the other gates (Digital & programmable).
🧮 The redstone comparator blockThe comparator: a circuit that compares one signal with another and says which is bigger. In the course it is an op-amp (Analogue electronics).
🚩 Signal flags and signal lamps (Popham's code, Trafalgar)Digital codes. A row of lit and unlit lamps is a binary number, which is what a microcontroller sends and reads (Digital & programmable).
🟫 PistonsPneumatic cylinders: a piston pushed by air, controlled by valves and switches (Drives & pneumatics).
⚓ Capstan, windlass, chain pump, gun tackles, steering gearDrive systems: gears, belts, chains, worm drives, torque and power (Drives & pneumatics).
⛏ Hoppers and minecartsSystems and flows: inputs, processes, outputs and feedback, and the energy that is carried or lost along the way (Systems & energy).
🪵 Masts, shrouds, stays and the dock bridgeStructures: members in tension and compression, supports, reactions and loads (Structures & materials).
🧱 Oak, iron blocks and copper sheathingMaterials: strength, stiffness, ductility, brittleness and the factor of safety (Structures & materials).
✈ The Air Cadets and the Grob Tutor: propeller pitch, glider and wing sparsA drive system with a rotating shaft (power and torque), and beams carrying loads. The Tutor also tows the formula banners across the top of this page.

The premise. HMS Ratchet is a 74-gun ship of the line, built block by block, with iron-block fittings, redstone wiring and pistons in her rigging, steering gear and gun deck. The squadron has been posted aboard to learn how the ship works, and her crew know that a ship of the line is only as good as its engineering. There is no safe place to guess: if the steering feedback fails, if a shroud is overloaded or if a signal is misread, the whole ship pays.

The crew you will meet. Flight Lieutenant Alba Murray is the squadron's officer in charge. Captain Callum Reid commands HMS Ratchet. Master Shipwright Morag Kerr is the ship's chief engineer, and your teacher in these decks. Cadet Sergeant Jamie Rahman asks the questions you are probably thinking: look for the violet 🎖 Cadet Sergeant Rahman asks… boxes, each of which tackles a common misconception.

The banner at the top of this page is a small engineering demonstration of its own. Watch the gears on the foredeck turn (a 12-tooth driver turning an 8-tooth driven gear, so a velocity ratio of 8 ÷ 12, about 0·67: the output turns faster than the input), the wheel correct a gust (error, then correction), the redstone pulse lose one level of signal strength per block until the repeater restores it, the lamps count pulses in binary, and the dock bridge flex as the minecart crosses. Overhead, the Tutor towing each banner obeys its formula literally. Pause it with the button if you want to study a frame.

Ship's glossary

Plain-English meanings of the terms used across the course, with the deck where each is taught. Type in the box to filter, choose a deck, or click a column heading to sort.

Meaning in plain English
3/2 valve, 5/2 valvePneumatic valves named by ports and positions. A 3/2 valve has 3 ports (supply, outlet, exhaust) and 2 positions; a 5/2 valve has 5 ports and 2 positions. Say that a 3/2 valve actuates and a 5/2 valve changes state.Drives & pneumatics
ActuatorAn output device that makes something move or change: a motor, a solenoid, a pneumatic cylinder. It is the sub-system at the output end of a control system.Systems & energy
ADC (analogue-to-digital converter)Turns a changing voltage into a whole number the microcontroller can work with. More bits mean finer steps. In a program you compare or scale that number to make a decision.Digital & programmable
Amplifier gain, AvOutput voltage divided by input voltage for an op-amp circuit: Av = Vo ÷ Vi. A negative gain means the output is upside down compared with the input.Analogue electronics
AnnulusThe ring-shaped area left when you cut a small circle out of a bigger one. On the instroke of a double-acting cylinder, air pushes on the annulus: the piston area minus the piston-rod area.Drives & pneumatics
AssignmentThe 50-mark practical part of the course: eight hours, on your own, in a supervised classroom, marked by Qualifications Scotland. You analyse, design, build, test and evaluate.Exam & assignment
Base, collector, emitterThe three legs of a bipolar transistor. A small current into the base controls a much larger current from collector to emitter (when the transistor is in its active region).Analogue electronics
BearingEngineering sense: a part that supports a rotating shaft and lets it turn with little friction. Radial bearings carry loads at right angles to the shaft; thrust bearings carry loads along it. Plain, journal, ball and roller bearings are the types in the course. Navigation sense: a compass direction, such as 045°. (Both appear aboard.)Drives & pneumatics
BJT (bipolar junction transistor)A current-operated transistor. In the course it is the npn type, used as a current amplifier or a switch. It needs a base resistor to set the base current.Analogue electronics
BrakeUses friction to slow a moving part. The kinetic energy does not vanish: it becomes heat (Eh = cmΔT).Drives & pneumatics
BrittlenessBreaking suddenly with almost no stretching first. Cast iron and glass are brittle. A brittle material has no yield point.Structures & materials
Closed-loop systemA system that measures its own output and feeds that back to the input, so it can correct itself. A system with no such measurement is open-loop.Systems & energy
ClutchUses friction to join two shafts so that one can drive the other, and to let them separate again.Drives & pneumatics
ComparatorAn op-amp circuit with no feedback resistor that compares the input voltage with a reference. As printed in the Data Booklet: if Vi is less than Vref the output saturates positively; if it is more, negatively.Analogue electronics
Compound gear trainA train in which two gears sit on one shaft. The overall velocity ratio is the product of the ratios of each pair.Drives & pneumatics
CompressionA force that squashes or pushes a member. In a frame, a member in compression pushes on its joint (the arrow points towards the joint).Structures & materials
Concurrent forcesForces whose lines of action all pass through one point. Their moments about that point are zero, so only the forces themselves need balancing.Structures & materials
CouplingJoins two shafts so that torque passes from one to the other. A rigid coupling needs the shafts perfectly in line; a flexible coupling allows a little misalignment.Drives & pneumatics
Data BookletThe booklet of formulae and data that Qualifications Scotland gives you in the exam (and you may use in the assignment). Learn where things are in it before the day.Exam & assignment
Drain, sourceTwo terminals of a MOSFET. When the MOSFET is switched on, current flows from the drain to the source (for an n-channel device).Analogue electronics
DuctilityAble to be stretched a long way, for example drawn into a wire, before it breaks. Copper and mild steel are ductile.Structures & materials
Duty cycleThe fraction of each PWM period for which the output is high, as a percentage. A higher duty cycle gives a higher average voltage and a faster motor.Digital & programmable
Efficiency, ηUseful output divided by input, for energy or for power: η = Eout ÷ Ein = Pout ÷ Pin. It is never more than 1 (100%). The difference is lost, usually as heat.Systems & energy
ElasticityA material returns to its original shape when the load is removed. Beyond the elastic limit it does not.Structures & materials
EOR (exclusive-OR)A gate whose output is 1 when its two inputs are different, and 0 when they are the same.Digital & programmable
EquilibrantThe one force that exactly balances a set of forces. It is equal in size to the resultant and opposite in direction.Structures & materials
EquilibriumNothing is accelerating or turning: the forces balance in both directions and the moments balance. In the Data Booklet: ΣFh = 0, ΣFv = 0, ΣM = 0.Structures & materials
ErrorIn a feedback system, the difference between the value you want (the set point) and the value you measure. Control acts to reduce it to zero.Systems & energy
Factor of safetyUltimate load ÷ safe working load, or equivalently ultimate stress ÷ safe working stress. It has no unit. A factor of 3 means the structure is designed to carry a third of what would break it.Structures & materials
FeedbackSending some of a system's output back to its input. It can oppose a change (negative feedback) or reinforce it (positive feedback).Systems & energy
FlowchartA diagram of the steps of a program using standard symbols (terminator, process, decision, input/output). For a programmable-control question give pin numbers and the unit of every delay.Digital & programmable
Flyback (protection) diodeA diode connected across a relay coil, solenoid or motor. When the current is switched off, the coil makes a high-voltage spike; the diode gives it somewhere to go so the transistor survives.Analogue electronics
Free-body diagramA sketch of one object, on its own, with every force acting on it drawn as an arrow. It is the first step in every statics problem.Structures & materials
GateDigital sense: a logic gate, such as AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR or EOR. Analogue sense: the control terminal of a MOSFET, which draws practically no current. Same word, two different devices.Digital & programmable · Analogue electronics
hFE (current gain)The current gain of a bipolar transistor: hFE = Ic ÷ Ib. It applies in the active region, not when the load limits the collector current.Analogue electronics
Hinge (pin) supportHolds a beam in place but lets it turn. It can push in any direction, so it gives two reaction components: one horizontal and one vertical.Structures & materials
Idler gearA gear placed between the driver and the driven gear. It reverses the direction of rotation but does not change the velocity ratio, because its teeth count cancels out.Drives & pneumatics
Infinite loop, finite loopAn infinite loop repeats for ever (the program jumps back to a label such as main). A finite loop repeats a set number of times, controlled by a counter or a for/next loop.Digital & programmable
Integrated circuit (IC)A chip holding several gates. A quad 2-input NAND chip holds four NAND gates. When you minimise a logic design, count the chips, not just the gates.Digital & programmable
Limit switchA switch or valve operated by a moving part when it reaches the end of its travel. It tells a sequence control that the cylinder has finished its stroke.Drives & pneumatics
LuxThe unit of illuminance (how much light falls on a surface). A light-dependent resistor falls in resistance as the lux level rises, which is how a circuit can detect dusk.Analogue electronics
MalleabilityAble to be hammered or rolled into thin sheets without cracking. Gold and copper are malleable.Structures & materials
MomentThe turning effect of a force about a point: M = Fx, where x is the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of the force. Unit: N m.Structures & materials
MOSFETA voltage-operated transistor. In the course it is the n-channel enhancement-mode type, used as a switch. It conducts when the gate–source voltage is above the threshold voltage.Analogue electronics
NAND equivalentA circuit that does the job of another logic function using only NAND gates. A NAND with its inputs tied is a NOT; a NAND followed by a NOT is an AND.Digital & programmable
Negative feedbackFeedback that opposes change: the output is subtracted from the target. It makes a system steady and accurate. It is the heart of a closed-loop control system.Systems & energy
Nodal analysis (method of joints)Finding the force in every member of a frame by balancing the forces at each joint, one joint at a time. You give the size and whether each member is in tension (T) or compression (C).Structures & materials
Op-amp (operational amplifier)A very high-gain voltage amplifier with an inverting (−) and a non-inverting (+) input. Add resistors and it becomes an inverting, non-inverting, difference or summing amplifier, a voltage follower, or (with none) a comparator.Analogue electronics
PlasticityA material stays permanently deformed after the load is removed. It happens once the load goes beyond the elastic limit.Structures & materials
Pneumatic cylinderA piston in a tube, moved by compressed air. A single-acting cylinder is pushed out by air and returned by a spring; a double-acting cylinder is pushed both ways by air.Drives & pneumatics
Positive feedbackFeedback that reinforces change: the output adds to the input. Left alone it runs away to an extreme, so it is used for latches and alarms, not for steady control.Systems & energy
PowerThe rate of transferring energy: P = E ÷ t. Electrical: P = VI. Mechanical: P = Fv, and for a rotating shaft P = 2πnT with n in revolutions per second.Systems & energy
Proportional controlA closed-loop method in which the size of the correction is in proportion to the size of the error. Compare two-state (on/off) control.Systems & energy
PWM (pulse-width modulation)Switching an output on and off very fast at a fixed frequency and varying how long it stays on in each cycle. The average voltage, and so a motor's speed, follows the duty cycle.Digital & programmable
Rack and pinionA pinion (small gear) turning against a straight toothed bar (the rack). It turns rotation into straight-line motion. Rack speed = π × pinion diameter × pinion speed.Drives & pneumatics
ReactionThe force with which a support pushes back on a structure. For a structure at rest, the reactions balance the loads.Structures & materials
ResultantThe single force that has the same effect as a set of forces acting together.Structures & materials
Roller supportSupports a structure but can roll along a surface. It can push only at right angles to that surface, so it gives one reaction component.Structures & materials
SaturationWhen an output cannot go any further. An op-amp output sits close to its positive or negative supply rail. A transistor is saturated when the load, not the base current, limits the collector current.Analogue electronics
SCQFThe Scottish Credit and Qualifications Framework. Higher courses sit at level 6; Higher Engineering Science carries 24 SCQF credit points.Exam & assignment
SolenoidA coil of wire that becomes an electromagnet when current flows, pulling in an iron core. It is an electrical actuator, used to operate pneumatic valves, latches and locks.Drives & pneumatics
Strain energy, EsThe energy stored in a stretched material: Es = ½Fx. It is the area under the load–extension graph. Use newtons and metres to get joules.Structures & materials
Strain gaugeA thin metal foil grid whose resistance changes when it is stretched. Put it in a voltage divider or bridge to turn strain into a voltage. A dummy gauge cancels the effect of temperature.Structures & materials
Strain, εExtension divided by original length: ε = Δl ÷ l. It has no unit.Structures & materials
Stress, σForce per unit cross-section area: σ = F ÷ A. One newton per square millimetre (N mm⁻²) is one megapascal (MPa).Structures & materials
Sub-systemA smaller part of a bigger system that does one job and has its own input, process and output. Control diagrams are drawn as sub-systems joined by arrows.Systems & energy
SustainabilityMeeting today's needs without stopping future generations from meeting theirs. Judge an engineering solution by its materials, energy use, lifetime and what happens to it at the end.Systems & energy
TensionA force that pulls or stretches. In a frame, a member in tension pulls on its joint (the arrow points away from the joint).Structures & materials
Threshold voltageThe gate–source voltage at which a MOSFET starts to conduct between drain and source. Below it the MOSFET is off; the gate voltage must exceed it to switch the load on.Analogue electronics
TorqueThe turning effect of a force about the axis of a shaft: T = Fr, where r is the radius. Unit: N m. Use the radius, not the diameter.Drives & pneumatics
ToughnessThe energy a material absorbs, per unit volume, before it fractures. A tough material (such as mild steel) bends and stretches before breaking; a brittle one (such as glass) does not.Structures & materials
Transconductance, gmFor a MOSFET: how much the drain current changes for each volt change in gate–source voltage, gm = ΔId ÷ ΔVgs. It is the slope of the transfer characteristic.Analogue electronics
TransducerA device that changes energy from one form to another. Input transducers (sensors) such as an LDR or a thermistor detect the world; output transducers such as a motor, lamp or buzzer act on it.Analogue electronics
Truth tableA table listing the output for every possible combination of inputs, written in binary order (000, 001, 010 …).Digital & programmable
UDL (uniformly distributed load)A load spread evenly along a length, in N per metre. Treat it as one force, w × length, acting at the centre of the loaded length.Structures & materials
Velocity ratio (VR)Input speed ÷ output speed. For gears it equals teeth on the driven gear ÷ teeth on the driver. VR greater than 1 slows the output down and raises its torque.Drives & pneumatics
Voltage dividerTwo resistors in series across a supply, with the output taken from between them. In the Data Booklet: V1 ÷ V2 = R1 ÷ R2. Replace one resistor with an LDR or thermistor to make a sensor.Analogue electronics
Worm and wheel, worm and nutA worm is a screw thread. Against a wheel, one turn of the worm moves the wheel on by its number of starts, so VR = teeth on wheel ÷ starts, and it usually cannot be driven backwards. Against a nut, one turn moves the nut on by one pitch (lead).Drives & pneumatics
Yield point, ultimate tensile strengthOn a mild steel stress–strain graph, yield is where it begins to stretch a lot for little extra stress; the ultimate tensile strength is the highest stress reached, after which the specimen necks and breaks.Structures & materials
Young's modulus, EHow stiff a material is: E = σ ÷ ε. It is the slope of the straight (elastic) part of the stress–strain graph. The Data Booklet table gives E in kN mm⁻², so convert carefully.Structures & materials

Ship and Minecraft words

None of these is part of the course. They are period and game flavour, explained so they never get in the way.

Ship of the line
A big warship of Nelson's era with several gun decks, built to fight in a line of battle. A 74-gun ship carried about that many cannon, most of them on two gun decks.
Capstan, windlass
A capstan is an upright drum turned with bars to haul in the anchor cable; a windlass does the same job on a horizontal drum. Both are drives: a small force on a long bar becomes a big force on the cable.
Gun tackle
Ropes and pulleys (blocks) used to run a heavy gun in and out.
Chain pump
A pump worked by a chain of discs hauling water up a pipe from the bilge.
Shroud, stay
Ropes that hold a mast steady. Shrouds run from the mast down to the ship's sides; stays run fore and aft. They are always in tension.
Yard, yardarm
The horizontal pole across a mast from which a sail hangs.
Sheathing
A covering of thin copper sheet over the underwater part of a hull, to keep out shipworm and weed.
Popham's code
The system of coloured signal flags used by the Royal Navy in Nelson's time, in which each flag stood for a number, so a hoist of flags spelled a message in code.
Redstone
Minecraft's wiring material. Redstone dust carries a signal with a strength from 0 to 15, falling by one for each block it travels; a repeater restores it.
Piston
In Minecraft, a block that pushes other blocks when powered. In this course, think of a pneumatic cylinder.
Air Cadets, squadron, flight
The RAF Air Cadets is a youth organisation for 12 to 20 year olds. Cadets belong to a numbered squadron, divided into flights.
Grob Tutor
The small two-seat aircraft in which Air Cadets get flying experience.
Watch, night watch
A sailor's spell of duty. “Night watch” is also this site's dark-mode button.
Blockhaven, 1805 Squadron
The made-up Minecraft town and Air Cadet squadron of this site. 1805 is the year of Trafalgar.

The companion sites Chart, Compass & Calculus (Higher Maths), Shot, Spark & Starlight (Higher Physics) and Blocks, Wings & Broadsides (Higher Computing Science) cover the other three Highers.
