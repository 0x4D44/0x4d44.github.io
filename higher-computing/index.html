Briefing room · read before your first watch

Welcome aboard, Cadet.

This is a complete, interactive guide to SQA Higher Computing Science, the course that Qualifications Scotland now awards. It covers every topic in the course specification, explained from the ground up, with diagrams, animations, short narrated videos, a step-by-step debugger, a live SQL database and exam-style questions with marking guidance.

You will learn it alongside 1805 (Blockhaven) Squadron, a crew of RAF Air Cadets posted for sea training aboard HMS Redstone. She is a 104-gun ship of the line from Nelson's navy, built entirely out of Minecraft blocks. It is a ridiculous premise, but every analogy on the site is chosen because it explains something. The gun crew's drill is the fetch-execute cycle. The redstone lamps are bits. The captain's wax seal is a digital signature.

Usually takenS5 or S6, after National 5 Computing Science (or equivalent)

GradesA to D, decided by your total mark out of 120

Four areas of study

Mandatory · QP ≈ 41–45 marks · Assignment task 1: 25 marks

Software Design & Development

Methodologies, analysis, design, data structures, sub-programs, files, standard algorithms, testing, debugging, evaluation.

 Mandatory · QP 10–14 marks

Computer Systems

Two's complement, floating point, Unicode, graphics, fetch-execute, performance, environment, law and security.

 Option · QP 25 marks · Assignment task 2: 15 marks

Database Design & Development

Requirements, ERDs, compound keys, data dictionaries, query design and SQL, with a live database.

 Option · QP 25 marks · Assignment task 3: 15 marks

Web Design & Development

Site structure, wireframes, usability testing, HTML, CSS positioning, forms, JavaScript events.

Everyone studies Software and Computer Systems. Your school picks one of the two options, Database or Web. You can read both on this site, but in the exam you answer only one option section.

How you are assessed

  • Question paper (2 hours, 80 marks). Each section starts with short questions, mostly "C" marks, then moves on to longer questions set in a context. Code is shown in SQA reference language, and you may answer in any language.
  • Assignment (6 hours, 40 marks), done in class over several sessions under supervision. It is open book: notes, manuals and your own programs are allowed. It is set and marked by the awarding body. You submit program listings and screenshots.
  • About 30% of marks are "A" marks (they need deeper understanding) and about 50% are "C" marks.

What the marks reward

Nearly three-quarters of the marks are for designing and writing solutions, so practise by building things, not by reading. Every deck has interactive tools for exactly that.

How to use this site

  1. Work through a deck in order. Each chapter is mapped to the course specification (look for the small SDD · … tags) and builds on the one before.
  2. Play with every interactive. Flip bits, step through the debugger, break the form, run SQL. Learning sticks when you do something with it.
  3. Read the ⚠ Exam lookout boxes. The ones headed "From the 2025 course report" quote what the examiners saw candidates get wrong. The others are common slips worth avoiding.
  4. Pass the ✈ pre-flight check at the end of each chapter. Multiple-choice questions mark themselves. Open questions show the marking guidance so you can mark yourself honestly.
  5. Finish with the Exam drill: command words, timing, the reference language and exam-style mock questions.

Your rank

Every pre-flight check you pass counts towards promotion through the Air Cadet ranks, from Cadet to Cadet Warrant Officer. Only one option counts, Database or Web, whichever you have done more of, because that is how the exam works. Progress is saved in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Video explainers

Six short narrated films, each one to two minutes long, on the topics candidates find hardest. They are narrated by Flight Lieutenant Alba Murray and Captain Hamish Blackwood (computer voices, Scottish accents) and every one has captions. They also appear in the matching chapters.

Ship's glossary

None of these words will be in your exam. They are the period flavour, explained so they never get in the way.

Ship of the line / first-rate
The biggest warships of Nelson's era, big enough to fight in the main battle line. A first-rate carried 100 or more guns over three gun decks.
Broadside
All the guns on one side of a ship fired together. "Weight of metal" is the total weight of shot a broadside throws.
Beat to quarters
The drum signal that sends every sailor to their battle station.
Gun deck / orlop
The decks where the cannon stand / the lowest deck, below the waterline, where stores and the magazine are kept.
Magazine
The protected store room for gunpowder cartridges, deep in the ship. On this site it stands for main memory.
Powder monkey
A boy who carried cartridges from the magazine up to the guns. On this site, the data bus.
Quarterdeck
The raised deck at the stern where the captain commands. On this site, the main program's global variables.
Bosun (boatswain)
The officer in charge of the crew's work, who gave orders with a whistle called a bosun's call.
Purser
The officer in charge of stores, pay and records: the ship's database keeper.
Victualling
Supplying a ship with food and drink.
Rigging / ratlines
The ropes that hold up the masts and work the sails / the rope ladders sailors climbed to go aloft.
Admiralty commission
The official document appointing an officer, signed by the Admiralty. On this site, a digital certificate.
Air Cadets, squadron, flight
The RAF Air Cadets is a youth organisation for 12–20 year olds. Cadets belong to a local numbered squadron, which is split into smaller groups called flights (A, B, C).
Grob Tutor
The small two-seat training aircraft in which Air Cadets get flying experience.
