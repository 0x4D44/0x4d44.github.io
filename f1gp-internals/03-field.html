Inside Formula One Grand Prix Part I — The original

Chapter 03

Twenty-six cars

Every car in the game, yours included, is one 192-byte record in a single table. This chapter follows that table from the static data it is built from, through the cut from 34 entrants to 26, to the physics tick that moves every car, the AI that steers them, the walls and pit crews they meet, and the keys that drive yours.

One table, built at run time

The game keeps its whole field in one array in the data segment at 25B3:0D1B. It holds 26 records of 0xC0 (192) bytes each, 4,992 bytes in all, running up to 0x209A. The code that wraps round the table tests for 0x209B, one byte past the end.

26records, a count hard-coded at 22 separate sites
192bytes per record (0xC0)
4,992bytes, all zero in the load image
34entrants in the seed data, cut to 26

None of those numbers was guessed. The stride comes from a block copy that moves 0xC0 bytes per record over 26 iterations. The count comes from mov cx,0x1a sitting beside mov si,0xd1b at 22 separate places in the code. Nothing in the game shortens the table. Every loop over it runs 26 times, and the order table that indexes it is always 52 bytes long.

The table is empty on disk. In the executable's load image all 4,992 bytes are zero, and the game zeroes each record again before it fills it, so nothing is inherited from a template. Everything that ends up in the table is computed at run time from seed data that is in the executable: car numbers, driver names, team strengths and driver rankings. That matters for the port. It can rebuild the field from the player's own copy of the game without shipping a single byte of it.

How it works

A car that drops out does not leave the table. The game cannot express a field of 25. Instead it sets bit 0x80 of the record's flag byte at +0x96 and leaves the car where it is. Every reader steps over a flagged record. The AI's search for the car ahead, for example, tests that bit and keeps walking back through the order until it finds a runner.

What a record holds

The port has decoded 88 of the 192 bytes with confidence, and has listed the widths of most of the rest. Width and signedness were settled by reading every instruction that touches a field, not by what looked reasonable. Get one wrong and nothing errors: a speed read as unsigned turns a reversing car into one doing 65,000 km/h.

Several words, +98 and +A8 among them, start life at 0x4000. That is 1.0 in the game's fixed-point maths, and the same unit turns up throughout this chapter. The busiest byte in the whole record is +B3, with 138 accesses. Its bits 0x40 and 0x08 are set together on both cars in what looks like the collision path. The port records that as a reading, not a fact.

The trap

Two fields look like a word and are not. +1E is a signed word, but the game also reads +1F on its own as a signed byte, so the two overlap. And +3C is one flag byte even though a single instruction clears it as a word. That instruction also clears +3D, which is a separate set of flags.

Where the cars come from

The seed data lives in the stack segment of the running game. It is static in the executable file, which is how the port can read it from the player's copy.

SeedAddressShape
Car numbersSS:1A2240 bytes: 20 teams × 2 slots
Driver namesSS:1A4A40 records of 0x18 bytes
Team performanceSS:28EC18 words, indexed by team − 1; larger is faster
Driver rankSS:2914 or SS:293COne byte per car number: a global rank where 1 is the best driver
Team countSS:28D218 in the shipped game

A slot's team is (slot >> 1) + 1, so the 40 slots cover 20 teams. The season runs 18 teams, so the last two slot pairs are never scanned. A zero car number is an empty slot. The shipped table leaves some teams on a single car and leaves out car 13 altogether. That is how 40 slots come to hold 34 real entrants. The second rank table, at SS:293C, is used when bit 7 of the session-type byte is set.

The trap

The sources do not fully agree on the field. The seed data and this cut describe 34 entrants. A later full Championship run found the offline game racing a fixed field of thirteen teams and 26 drivers, all starting every round (Chapter 4). How the two fit together is not settled.

Thirty-four into twenty-six

Only 26 cars fit on the grid, so eight entrants have to go. The game decides who by running a real session. Pre-qualifying is not a lookup table. The routine at 0752:506B flattens the 40-slot seed into a dense working list at DS:0AB1, with its count at DS:0AAD. That list is copied to SS:17D8 and kept bubble-sorted by each car's best lap. The lap times are real, measured 32-bit values stored four bytes per car at SS:1800. Every time a car completes a lap, 0752:20C4 stores the time and re-sorts the list on the spot.

When the session ends, 0752:510E forces any human player into the top 26, 0752:4D91 takes a snapshot of the list, and 0752:5175 copies the first 26 entries back over DS:0AB1 and sets the count to 26. There is no per-team cap. It really is the fastest 26, plus a guaranteed place for the player. In the working list each entry is one byte: the car number in the low six bits, bit 7 for a human player's car, and bit 6 (only alongside bit 7) for the second player.

When nobody ran pre-qualifying

If SS:1234 is clear, no pre-qualifying took place. Then 0752:5093 builds the same ordering from arithmetic. This is the path the port reaches, because it has no pre-qualifying session of its own, and it is the path the original takes when the player skips pre-qualifying. It works like this:

  1. Circuit scale. 0752:4B52 starts from a per-circuit spread word at DS:0222, supplied by the track file. In wet conditions (SS:122E set) it first folds the word through a factor of 0x3700. It then blends towards a flat 0x4000 by difficulty: ((word − 0x4000) × (4 − difficulty) >> 2) + 0x4000. At the lowest difficulty the circuit's own word decides how far the field spreads. At the highest, every circuit is the same.
  2. Team base. 0752:4AF0 gives each team scale × performance / 716. The divisor, 716, is also the largest performance value in the shipped table. If the performance mode at SS:1224 is not exactly 1, every team gets the fastest team's performance, so the machinery is equal.
  3. Driver term. 0752:4BFA subtracts a weighted rank and a noise term from 1.0, then multiplies the base by what is left. The rank weights by difficulty are 0x80, 0x20, 0x08, 0x04, 0 and the noise weights 0x400, 0x100, 0x40, 0x20, 0. At the top difficulty both are zero, so the driver stops mattering.
  4. Jitter and floor. Every score loses twice a random byte, and no AI score falls below 0x1000.

A player's car is not scored on pace at all. A named player gets a flat 0x4000 less the same jitter. The ranking step then subtracts 0x4000 again, so the player sorts to the bottom of the list, and the promotion step puts them back in. That is how the original starts a player at the back without dropping them from the field.

The trap

The routine that maps a list entry back to its team, 0752:4717, compares the whole entry byte, flags included. A player's entry has bit 7 set, so it never matches a seed slot, and the routine falls back to team 1. That is a quirk of the original. The port keeps it, because "fixing" it would change which field appears.

How we know

There is no live capture of the cut. The port transcribed the fallback from the instructions and checked it step by step. Before writing any code, the author worked out two things the arithmetic must imply and wrote them as tests. With no circuit loaded at difficulty 0, the scale collapses to zero, so every car sits on the floor. At difficulty 4, the driver's rank cannot reach the score at all. Both held on the first run. A third test holds the random generator still, varies only the rank, and requires the score to fall steadily. A sign error cannot survive that.

Two orders: the road and the results

The game keeps two separate orderings of the same 26 cars, and they answer different questions.

The track order at 25B3:0C65 says who is where on the road. It holds one word per car: the car's byte offset from the start of the entrant table (0, 0xC0, 0x180 and so on). A 0xFFFF sentinel sits at each end. The spatial sort at 0752:44C5 keeps it in order of +0x26, the car's progress round the lap, taken modulo the track length at DS:0172. The sort is a plain bubble sort: each pass that makes a swap is followed by another, until a pass is clean. Each swap rewrites both cars' +66 bytes, so every car always knows its own slot.

The race classification says who is winning. It lives in each record's +AA byte and in a list of car-number bytes at SS:1940. The two orders agree only when the cars are first placed on the grid. They part company as soon as anyone is lapped: a backmarker just ahead of the leader on the road is a lap behind in the results.

The classification pass at 0752:4546 runs only in a race, and only when the spatial sort actually swapped something. It first works out the finishing distance: the race's lap count at SS:123C times the track length. For each car it computes race progress as (lap − 1) × track length + track-record index and stores it at +A4. A car that has passed the finishing distance is frozen at finish + (1000 − its old place byte), so cars that have finished keep the order they finished in. Then it bubble-sorts the 26 cars on that value, comparing the high word as signed and the low word as unsigned, and breaks ties on +1E. Finally it writes each car's +AA as twice its place and copies its car-number byte into SS:1940. When a car's place changes, the +04 sentinel is reset. If the player's cockpit is showing, the same pass prints the new position.

Why the renderer cares

The renderer needs the track order too. Every frame, 15F2:AFA3 walks the track order backwards from the viewing car's own place. It draws nothing itself. For each car it accepts, it stamps "a car is here" onto one track record: a few flag bits, plus a class byte made from 0x80 | record[+66]. The road passes then draw those records as cars. Afterwards, 15F2:B1A7 clears the stamps by masking bits off, not by restoring saved bytes, and it clears one bit the stamp never set.

The class byte carries twice the car's place on the road, not its index in the table. The consumer at 15F2:5C6B treats anything below 0xB4 as a car, and 0xB4 is exactly 0x80 | (2 × 26). It masks off the top bit, uses the result as a byte offset into the track order, follows the word it finds there to the entrant record, and reads +84. That byte is a small bias that decides which of two overlapping objects is drawn on top.

From the port

An early survey read +84 as a level-of-detail setting. A research pass that chased every writer of the byte found it had exactly one: the car-list routine sets it to 1 (or 2 when a scene word is large) and adds one for each busy scene slot it steps over. So it is a draw-order bias. The same approach showed that 0C65 is spatial, not the race order. Both corrections came from asking who writes a thing rather than reading the thing itself.

One physics tick

The routine that moves the whole field once is 0752:5C2F. For a long time the project carried a different address, 19ED:6F90, as "the physics". That turned out to be track-building code. The real tick runs in this order:

The tick begins by setting DS:0B99 to 0x80 and calling 0752:586A. That runs the spatial sort and the neighbour pass. The car loop then walks the freshly sorted track order. For each car it resolves one order word, runs a short setup prefix at 0752:5C42, and takes one of two branches. A car that is still racing (a "runner") goes through the traffic check, the section gates, the driving update and track association, then the long continuation. A retired car takes a shorter path.

After all 26 cars, a set of services runs once per frame. The parameter service at 0752:5B27 updates just one car per frame: its mass, tyre grip and reliability, using the tyre-wear curve at 0752:77EB and one random draw. A cursor at DS:2925 steps on by 0xC0 each frame and wraps after the 26th car, so each car is refreshed every 26 frames. The spray service at 0752:3A81 clears its level in the dry. In the wet it scans the cars ahead in track order, keeps the nearest eligible one, and works out how much spray it throws, using the cosine table.

How we know

The port was checked against two complete ticks captured from the original running in an emulator, one on the grid and one with cars moving. Each captured snapshot covers the whole of memory at the start and end of the tick and at every boundary inside it. The port's version of 5C2F..6AB8 reproduces both ticks byte for byte, across the full 26-car loop and at every single-car boundary. That proves those two ticks, not every possible race state.

Driving a car: the dynamics at 995C

The per-car driving update is 0752:995C. It runs for the player's car and for every AI car alike. Only the inputs differ: keys and joystick for the player, the AI's own steering and pedal decisions for everyone else. It breaks into four stages.

  1. The head (995C..9B1D) prepares the car's controls. It includes the recovery steering for a spinning car and the special handling for a retired car.
  2. The producers (9B1D..9FEA) work out wheel speed, aerodynamics, gear, engine and brakes. They fill a force workspace at DS:226F..2306, which holds two 48-byte force records at DS:2277 and DS:22A7.
  3. Force resolution (A02D..A48E, with traction limits at 96E1..97E8) turns those forces into two acceleration words. If one force record hits its grip limit, the other is recoupled. This stage changes no entrant byte except the traction flags at +7E.
  4. Integration (A48F..A554) updates velocity, world position and yaw over the selected timestep. It is skipped when DS:3195 is non-zero.

Everything is integer maths in the game's own units, with 1.0 written as 0x4000. The port keeps those units. It never converts to metres or seconds, because every rounding step has to match.

Why your throttle is not your throttle

The throttle producer at 0752:93EA..9534 shows the style. The player's pedal decides whether the throttle is on. The car record then holds, at +98, what the physics actually allows. When the car is moving above a threshold speed, the traction flag at +7E bit 0x40 is set and the surface is not type 3, the producer subtracts a step from the previous value, never going below 0x1000. Otherwise it adds the step back. Either way the result is capped at 1.0. The step is a surface-dependent rate multiplied by the timestep, scaled down by 214.

From the port

An acceptance test held the throttle key down and expected full throttle, 16,384, every frame. On frame 445 it read 15,074 and failed. The runtime was right. With a rate of 8,192 and a timestep of 2,621, the step works out at 1,310, and 16,384 − 1,310 = 15,074. The code at 0752:94F5 had simply cut the throttle as the car began to slide. The test had treated a physics output as a keyboard input. The fix was to the test, which now expects 15,074 exactly, then 13,764, then recovery.

Gears and the engine

Gear changes run at 0752:9535..95F0, and the automatic-shift decisions at A621, A6B4 and A6E6. The gear byte is signed, and the gearbox ratio lookup at 7B18/7B5F uses it to pick a setup byte before an unsigned divide. After the car loop, 0752:92E0..93E9 updates engine speed and the automatic gearbox for the car being heard. It works out the rpm before any shift, clamps the gear as a signed value, alternates the downshift gates and smooths the result. The same routine publishes a word for the sound driver, which is how the engine note follows the car.

Keeping to the road

A circuit is a chain of 46-byte track records (see Chapter 2). Each car holds a far pointer at +12 to the record it occupies. After the dynamics move the car in world coordinates, 0752:1717..179D works out which record it is now over and updates that pointer, and only that pointer. It copes with the wrap at the end of the lap and with the jump between the main track and alternative routes such as the pit lane.

Next, 0752:6AB9..6C16 refreshes the car's track-relative coordinates. It reads the world position at +28/+2C, five words from the new track record, and the cosine table, and it updates exactly six words in the car record: lateral offset, distance along the segment and their relatives. Before the dynamics run, 0752:1ACD..1BCD applies the section gates: corridor checks on the main track and association with an alternative route.

How it works

World position and track position are kept side by side. The physics works in world space, where forces and velocities make sense. The AI, the timing and the renderer work in track space: which record, how far along it, how far from the centre. The association and refresh steps keep the two in step once per car per frame.

How the AI drives

The computer cars drive with the same dynamics as yours. What makes them AI is where their inputs come from. Four pieces of code supply them.

Following the line

Each track record carries racing-line words, filled in when the track loads (Chapter 2). The AI steers towards a lateral target held at +0E. At 0752:6C3F the game reads the racing-line word in the current record and the same word in the next record, 46 bytes on. It interpolates between them by the car's fraction along the segment:

target = current + ((next − current) × fraction) >> 14

where a fraction of 0x4000 means the car is at the end of the segment. If flag +18 bit 0x10 is set, the target is simply zero, the centre of the road. The steering logic then compares the car's lateral offset at +0A with that target.

A fault in that steering logic produced visible spinning in the port. At 0752:5FC8 the original clears a steering demand of the opposite sign, then jumps to a shared clamp-and-add. The port had put the shared clamp and add inside only one branch. A six-case regression test showed the old code giving zero where the original gave +20. After the fix, all seven car-state differences in a captured moving tick went away.

Corner speeds

How fast to take a corner comes from a speed curve refreshed by 0752:1061..10B9. Its caller stores the product in the car's +50/+52. The corner solver inside it divides by the circuit's grip word. When the grip word is small, the first divide at 0752:AB56 overflows.

The trap

On the 8086 a divide overflow raises interrupt 0. The game hooks that interrupt to set a flag byte at SS:00C0 and return, leaving the registers as they were. The corner solver clears the flag at AB85 before its next divide, so an overflowed radius is read as "no corner limit". Nothing in the game notices. The port reproduces the behaviour and also counts these faults, purely as evidence: a run with a non-zero count drove on corner speeds the AI never meant to compute.

Traffic

The traffic check at 0752:3945..3A80 looks for a slower car to worry about. A flag at +7E bit 0x04 makes it return at once. Otherwise it clamps the car's speed between a floor and a ceiling, then steps through the track order towards the cars ahead, one entry at a time from its own place. It reads at most 26 entries and skips any record flagged out of the field. It stops at the first car that is not ahead of it, or once the gap is bigger than the car's speed shifted right by 10. The faster the car, the further ahead it looks.

For each candidate it checks three things: that the other car is inside a lateral corridor (the car's half-width at +3E plus 0x400, or a narrower 0x280 in some cases), that this car is faster, and that both are on the same route. A close car inside a lateral limit also yields a first braking estimate, written to +78. A car that passes every test sets bit 0x10 of +BC: traffic ahead.

Neighbours and the car ahead

Before the car loop, the neighbour pass at 0752:29E0 uses the fresh track order to find each car's spatial neighbours and records them in the entrant records and some low stack-segment scratch. The controls code at 0752:89A8 asks for "the next car" and gets the next car in track order, not the next slot in the table. The pointer at +7A names another entrant record: the car this one is tracking.

Contacts, walls and damage

After the car loop, 0752:3739..37BF scans backwards through pairs of cars that are close together. A vertical-separation test at B4F3 can rule a pair out early. For the rest, B518 prepares the contact, B024 runs a swept-box search, and B564 computes the response. The box test at ACD8..AE93 checks the four corners of one car in the other car's local coordinates. When the boxes overlap for the whole interval, AEC2 separates them along a separation vector.

Walls are handled inside each car's own update. The contact leaf at 0752:BF7E..C0F1 owns ordinary wall geometry. The impact at BA28..BDC2 moves the car back, reflects its velocity and applies an angular impulse. A projected-wall variant at BDC3..BF75 handles walls the track builds from other data. The pit lane keeps an implicit outer boundary even where the track has no wall flags: the ordinary path compares lateral position plus 512 against the width plus a strip term plus 80.

Damage uses one pair of services, B9B0 and B9D9, for both kinds of hit. For a car-to-car contact the impact strength is DS:08EA. For a wall it is half the absolute value of DS:08DC. A hit hard enough goes on to throw debris, raise a repair request and draw damage on the cockpit. The hardest wall impacts retire the car outright: after the signed thresholds at C079/C0BF, 0752:53B9 sets the retirement bits, reclassifies the field through 4FD0 and updates the on-screen list of runners.

From the port

These paths were reached one at a time, by driving. A scripted replay with the throttle held down stopped at frame 3,479 on a wall impact the port had not yet written. With that done it ran to frame 5,081, then stopped at the repair request. Next came the retired-car relocation, then the terminal contact at 53B9. Each stop was a guard that said exactly which original routine had been reached. None was skipped to make the run longer.

Pit stops

Pit stops run as a ten-state machine at 0752:4012..4354, with the state in the low nibble of +67. Times are in ticks of the game clock at DS:294F. The clock formatter divides by 60,000 per minute, so a tick is a millisecond. The pit lane runs between two track-record indices, DS:019C and DS:019E.

Finding the box. Each team has its own spot. In state 1 the car waits until it is at least 0x400 off the centre line and has reached track index team × 3 + 1 + DS:019A, where the team is +AD. So pit boxes are three track records apart, in team order. A separate reference step, 0752:3BBE, steers the car there: it offsets the car's track index by (9 − team) × 3 records, clamps the result to ten records inside each end of the pit lane, and converts it to a byte offset at 46 bytes per record. When the car arrives, the game zeroes its speed and six force words, sets its pit flags and starts a 400 ms timer.

On the jacks. In state 2 the game adds DS:0366 to the car's ride height at +8C each frame, up to a limit at DS:03F6. That reads as the car being lifted on jacks; it is our reading of the numbers, not a label in the code. It then starts the repairs, chooses tyres and sets the service deadline. State 5 lowers the car again at the same rate.

How long a stop takes. The service deadline depends on the session and the car:

The player's hands

The player's controls reach the car in three steps. First, the keyboard. The game's interrupt handler keeps a bitmap of held keys, updated through INT 15h at 8B6E:0533..0563. Then 0752:0005..00C5 decodes the player's key bindings against that bitmap into command words. The bindings pack bitmap positions, not raw scan codes. By default A is throttle, Z brake, , and . steer, and Space is the gear control.

Second, the player-input routine at 213F:29BD..2A35 reads the game port at I/O 0x201 and, if a joystick is set up, samples its axes through 8B6E:06DA. Third, four selectors turn whatever the player chose (keys, joystick axes or buttons) into the car record:

SelectorWritesDigital bit in +7F
Steering, 213F:2AF6+7C, clamped to ±0x0FFF from an axis0x08 right, 0x04 left, set beyond ±0x800
Throttle, 213F:2B99+9B, signed0x01, set at 0x40 or more

So +7F is a derived summary. The physics reads it alongside the analogue values: the throttle producer, for example, zeroes the throttle when bit 0x01 is clear.

The trap

The gear selectors lose information when they combine. A secondary selector alone and both selectors together both produce 0x90. But the routine also leaves the primary selector's value in a register, and a later feature routine uses it. The combined byte cannot recover it, so the port carries the primary value separately.

Driver aids

The game offers six driver aids, listed in its setup menu as Auto Brakes, Auto Gears, Self-Righting Spins, Indestructable (the game's spelling), Dotted "Best Line" and Suggested Gear. They are the low six bits of SS:1220. A second mask at DS:2251 says which aids are allowed. The effective set, the AND of the two, lives at DS:297D. The cockpit draws each aid as a 10 × 5 tile in one of three states: not allowed, allowed but off, and on.

In a race the function keys toggle aids. The command routine at 0752:E2BC checks six key edges, from command bit 0x80 down to 0x04. Each press flips one bit of SS:1220, recomputes DS:297D, redraws the tile and sets SS:0192 so that the next frame copies the new mask into each car. Two keys do more: the fifth also sets up the dotted-line feature and the sixth works out a gear recommendation. We read those two as the Best Line and Suggested Gear aids, which matches the menu order. That mapping is an inference.

Aid choices persist in F1PREFS.DAT. The file is 0x48A bytes of body plus a four-byte checksum, with the aid mask at offset 0x478. The checksum is two words: a plain sum of the bytes, and a running value rotated left three bits before each byte is added. A missing or invalid file leaves the executable's built-in default alone.

The dice

All the randomness in this chapter comes from one generator. It is a five-byte shift register at DS:2984..2988, drawn from by the far routine at 0752:04D7. A second copy of the routine at 0752:0509 serves another register at DS:08C3. Each draw forms a byte from two words of the register, then shifts every byte up one place and puts the new byte at the bottom:

ax = (s1 | s2<<8) >> 4
bx = (s3 | s4<<8) >> 1
al = al XOR bl
s4 = s3; s3 = s2; s2 = s1; s1 = s0; s0 = al

The routine returns the whole of AX, and callers mask the bits they want. A register of all zeros draws zero forever.

The generator is never seeded from a clock, so it is fully deterministic. But it is shared by everything: the track loader draws from it for trackside scenery, the pre-qualifying fallback for jitter, the parameter service for reliability, the pit logic for stop times. Its state at any moment depends on every draw made before. Reproducing one particular field or one particular pit stop means reproducing that whole history, menus included.

From the port

One lesson from the project: the static executable holds 37 at SS:1230, but the test scenario the original was captured in selects 12. Only when the port fed in the same menu choice did its calibration tables and random-generator state agree with the capture.

The per-circuit driving block

One more piece of data shapes how every car drives. A 38-byte block at DS:0204..0229 is decoded from the circuit file when the track loads. Its first ten bytes are the AI cars' shared setup, in the same shape as a player's car-setup record: front and rear wing (0 to 64), six gear ratios (a non-zero ratio is clamped to 16 to 80), a tyre compound (0 to 5) and a signed brake balance. Later words scale grip (at DS:020E), engine (DS:0216) and wing (DS:0218). The word at DS:0222 is the circuit spread the pre-qualifying fallback reads.

Because the block comes from the track at start-up, patching it in the executable does nothing. The port's alternative vehicle sets (Chapter 12) change the field by scaling these words after they are decoded, before the grid and the corner calibration read them. The original simulation then runs as normal. The engine scale is capped at 1.0 because the game's thrust arithmetic wraps negative above it.
