Inside Formula One Grand Prix

In 1991 Geoff Crammond and MicroProse shipped a Formula One simulator that ran a full 26-car field, sixteen real circuits and a filled-polygon 3D world on a PC of the day. This is an account of how it works, from the timer interrupt to the last pixel of the cockpit, and of how a small project spent twenty-four days making it run again, exactly, inside a modern Rust host.

What this is

The site has two parts. Part I takes the original game apart, one subsystem per chapter: the machine it runs on, how a circuit is stored and expanded, the car field and its physics, race control, the renderer, sound, and the menus and files. Where it helps, it gives the real addresses and record offsets, written as segment:offset in the executable's own run-time layout.

Part II is the story of the port, called Big Race: what we built, in what order, what went wrong, and the methods that made a faithful port possible. The most important of those is the oracle: the original executable running under an emulator, against which every ported routine was compared.

What this is not

Big Race is an unofficial project. It is not associated with or endorsed by the original game, its creators or its publishers. It reads a player's own copy of the game at run time and bundles none of it, and this site reproduces none of it either: no game code, data, artwork or screenshots. Every diagram here is new.
