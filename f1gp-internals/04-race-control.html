Inside Formula One Grand Prix Part I — The original

Chapter 04

Race control

The previous chapter moved the cars. This one covers the officials: what kind of session is running, when it starts and ends, who has completed which lap, who won, how the results and the Championship tables are built, and how the game pauses, replays and saves a race in progress.

One byte decides the session

Every session in the game, from a Quick Race to round 16 of a season, runs through the same engine. What changes its behaviour is one byte in the stack segment, SS:124A. The menu code at 15F2:C02A sets it.

ValueSessionWhat it changes
0x00PracticeTimed session; lap times shown; cars come in after runs
0x40QualifyingTimed session; best laps sorted into a grid
0x80RaceLap-limited; classification, gaps and the finish sequence

Race is the only value with the top bit set, so the code usually tests the byte as a signed number: negative means race, anything else is a timed session. That one sign test chooses between two branches of the clock, two ways of counting laps and two exits. You will see it again and again below.

A Grand Prix weekend is driven from the game's GRAND PRIX menu. Its five entries are Free Practice, Qualifying, Pre-Race Practice, Race and Abandon Event. Pre-Race Practice is the game's warm-up. The sources do not record which mode byte it uses, so this chapter says nothing more about it. A Quick Race skips straight to the race with choices the menu stores in the stack segment: grid position at SS:11A8, circuit at SS:11A9, car at SS:11AA, weather at SS:11AB and race distance at SS:11AC.

From the port

Just before it sets the mode, the menu hand-off at 15F2:C024 writes a duration word of 120. It does this for every session type. The port first wrote it only for races. Practice and Qualifying then started with no time on the clock and finished at once. The fix was to follow the original and write it for all three.

Getting to the green light

By the time a race starts, the track is loaded (Chapter 2) and the field is cut and placed on the grid (Chapter 3). The unlinked race caller at 0752:EC4B..ECE6 then runs a fixed sequence: preparation, the race branch at ECE7..ED23 (seeds, grid, first classification), the view and countdown set-up, the generated trackside features, the controls, and some final start-up gates. Preparation reuses the physics start-up routine without repeating its 720-call corner calibration.

Part of the race branch normalises the route. At 0752:ECFC..ED0A the game clears DS:2917, sets DS:0306 to 0x80 for a moment so that the route switch at 0701 leaves every car's track pointer alone, then clears it again. Without that guard the route switch would move the cars it is meant to leave in place.

The start lights are ordinary game state. A countdown at DS:290D runs 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. The red and green light states sit at DS:2923, and the light graphics are copied from resource rectangles by 213F:3B46. The lights clear at 0752:DB11. From there, the first race frame is entered at 0752:EDE0..EE52.

What happens in one frame

Each frame of a live session runs a set of services in a fixed order. The port captured the original at the boundaries between them, so the early part of the order is known exactly.

The trap

An earlier port change ran the one-car qualifying update before the DA48 services, because that looked tidier. A review of the original's call order showed the reverse: EE7F runs DA48 first and then the whole field at 5C2F. The change was reverted. In this project, the original's order is the specification, even when it looks odd.

How long is a race?

A race's length is set as a percentage of the real Grand Prix distance. The routine at 213F:02B9..030D turns that percentage into laps:

laps = ((full_laps × percent) / 50 + 1) >> 1
if percent == 5 or laps < 3:  laps = 3

The full distance in laps comes from the track, at DS:024E. The percentage is at SS:1226, and the result goes to SS:123C, which the rest of the game reads as "the lap limit". Dividing by 50, adding one and halving is a rounded divide by 100. For Suzuka the track holds 53. At 5 per cent that rounds to 3 laps, which is also the minimum.

The same routine can also reset the session. When DS:0B99 is non-zero it sets two words at DS:2943/2945 to 0xFFFF, clears effects and markers, clears DS:2917 and switches route.

The session clock

All timing hangs off one clock. The primary clock is a 32-bit count with a fractional word: low word at DS:294F, high word at DS:2951, fraction at DS:2953. A second, auxiliary clock sits just after it at DS:2955..2959. Each tick adds a step held at DS:223B..2245. Initialisation is at 0752:0909..0983 and the advance at 0984..0AED.

The clock counts in milliseconds. No single line of code says so, but everything agrees. The decimal formatter at 0752:0C17..0D3F divides by 6,000,000, 600,000, 60,000, 10,000, 1,000, 100 and 10, which are the places of a minutes-and-seconds display in milliseconds. The "never" deadline used below is 86,400,000, exactly 24 hours. Finished three-to-five-lap races end with the clock between about 400,000 and 530,000, seven to nine minutes.

In a timed session the clock drives the countdown on screen. The remaining time is the total at DS:2221/2223 less the clock, floored at zero. It is formatted into DS:298D, and any change raises display flags at DS:0040 and DS:003A. For the player's own car only, the current lap time is the clock minus the time the lap started, held in the car's +54/+56, and it is published to DS:295B/295D. The race-only display service at 0D40 is never entered in Practice or Qualifying.

Crossing the line

Each car decides for itself when it has completed a lap. That happens near the end of its own update in the field tick, in the tail at 0752:6976..6A7E. Two flag bits on the car's current track record choose one of three paths:

  • Lap-limit marker. In a race, if a car that has not finished is on its final lap (lap byte equal to the limit), the game calls 0752:CA25 to place the lap-limit trackside object and sets DS:2915. No lap is counted on this path.
  • Re-arming. The game clears the car's timing-line latch, bit 0x02 of +23, ready for the next crossing. Crossing the line does not by itself retire a car.
  • Counting. On a timing-line record, if the latch is set, nothing happens: a car sitting on the line is not counted twice. Otherwise the game sets the latch and counts the lap.

Timing. The lap-timing code at 0752:200E..20BF computes the lap time as the clock less +54, stores it in +40, and compares it with the best lap in +AE as a signed 32-bit value. From there 0752:20C4 writes the car's best lap into a table indexed by car number at SS:1800, four bytes per car, and keeps the working order at SS:17D8 sorted. The sort at 0752:4434 is stable, and compares the high word as signed and the low word as unsigned. In a timed session a new best lap also updates the on-screen position.

Counting. The counter at 0752:1FC7 increments the lap byte +22. If the car is the race leader (+AA equal to zero), it first copies the leader's old lap into DS:298B and SS:123E. The rest of race control reads "which lap is the leader on" from there.

Who pits, and when

In a race, 0752:1E80 runs next. From 1BF6 it decides whether to call the car in for a pit stop. It sets the car's stop-request flags (+23 bit 0x80, +B3 bit 0x04 and +97 bit 0x04) only when all of these hold:

  • the leader has not yet started the last lap;
  • the leader has reached a threshold lap held at DS:0250;
  • bit 0x01 of the car's +97 is still set (the pit-stop code clears it, subject to a lap test against the same threshold);
  • fewer than seven cars already have a request raised;
  • the car's team-mate has not already asked. The check at 0752:49AA..49CB looks up the paired car.

A pending request can also be withdrawn as the leader starts the final lap. So the original spreads its AI pit stops out, never sends more than seven cars in at once, and never brings both cars of a team in together. The stop itself is the state machine described in Chapter 3.

Timed sessions use the same flags differently. The branch at 0752:1F77..1FC6 calls a car in once it has done a run of laps. In Practice an AI car comes in after 10 laps. In Qualifying the threshold is 3. For a third, unnamed non-race mode, an AI car gets a one-in-eight random chance each lap once it has done 3. This reads like a real team's run plan: long runs in practice, short ones in qualifying.

The chequered flag

Finishing is spread across four places in the code. Here is the sequence in a race.

  1. The winner. In the same 1E80 path, at 1C78, a car that crosses the line with its lap byte above the limit becomes the winner. Its record offset goes into DS:28FF, unless bit 0x20 of its +23 is set. The car is marked finished: +5E gets bits 0x11 and +9A gets bit 0x01.
  2. Everyone else. Once DS:28FF is set, every car that crosses the line is marked finished too, whatever lap it is on. That is the real rule: after the winner takes the flag, each car's race ends at its next crossing.
  3. The scan. Among the pre-physics services, DA65 calls 1E48. It checks every entrant once every 16 calls and sets bit 0x80 of DS:2913 only when every car has either finished or retired.
  4. The grace period. On the next clock tick, 0984 sees the bit. It sets DS:2911 and DS:2903 to 0x80 and arms a deadline at SS:1250/1252: the clock plus 6,000, which is six seconds. In a linked two-machine race both machines' finish bits are needed, both to arm and to expire it.
  5. The exit. When the deadline passes, the clock service sets bit 0x40 of SS:124E. That is the terminal flag. The session loop sees it, stops the sound and fades the screen to black, then goes to the results.

Timed sessions end on the clock instead. There the clock service needs the remaining time to reach zero before it arms the same six-second grace, and on expiry it sets bit 0x20 of SS:124E rather than 0x40. The formatter drops the last digit, so "zero" shows once under a minute remains. In the port's test runs, Practice and Qualifying both ended at clock 7,146,677. That fits a 120-minute session that ends at the zero readout plus the grace period. This fit is our inference from the numbers, not a rule stated in the code.

The pre-physics services also run a finish-marshal routine, CDF4. It finds the leader by comparing signed 32-bit progress, counting retired cars as zero and resolving ties in favour of the later entrant. It then places a trackside object 80 units away through the same marshal-placement routine, CAB1, used for flags elsewhere.

How it works

Nothing in the original ends a race early. There is no "race over" timer and no forced retirement. The session ends only when every car has reached a terminal state by itself, finished or retired. The port keeps that rule strictly. It never forces a retirement, moves a car, or relaxes a check to make a test finish. When an automated run could not finish, the fix was always to drive better, for example steering through corners with real key presses instead of holding the throttle, never to loosen the ending.

From the port

One deliberate difference. The original freezes a finished car's lap counter. But a player who wins can keep driving while the rest of the field finishes, and in a five-lap race the dash then stayed at "6 of 5" for good. On 2026-09-22 the port changed this for one car only: the player's own car, when it is the winner. It counts each later crossing once, using the same timing-line latch, and redraws the dash. Every other finished car stays frozen, as in the original.

Retirement and the hand-over state machine

Cars leave a race by retiring, for example after a heavy crash (Chapter 3). The retirement code sets the car's flag bits, reclassifies the field through 4FD0, and updates the list of runners on screen. A player's retirement then goes through a delayed removal. 5394 stores the flags, 49CC handles driver availability and removes the car's model pair, the resident display at 213F:1BCB updates the icons, and the common physics resumes at 9B06. After that, E6E0 picks a new car for the camera.

Alongside this runs a small state machine the port calls the session state. Its state word is DS:2963, and its deadline, at DS:292F..2932, is compared with the clock.

StateRoutineBehaviour
0D25ENormal running. When the deadline expires, rescan for a candidate at D331.
1D369Advance a session icon each tick until the deadline.
2, 3D3BBRefresh the display, release the player, wait 5,000 ms.
4D41FWait 500 ms, start a fade, allow 1,000 ms more.
5D47FCopy the candidate into the display state, wait 5,000 ms.
6D4C2Write a display word, wait 5,000 ms.
7D50AActivate the next identity, or finish.

The rescan at D331 has three outcomes. If there is no candidate, the selection finishes. If the candidate is the same identity as before, the deadline is pushed out by 86,400,000 ms: never. If it is a different identity, the machine enters state 1 with a 15-second deadline.

The start deadline comes from 0752:D167..D25D. It counts the selected entrants and sums a per-car byte from each, divides the session's time budget between them, and applies some offsets. With one selected entrant or fewer, the deadline is the 24-hour "never".

The trap

The code only talks about identities, candidates and hand-overs. Our reading is that this is the original's support for several human players sharing one race, each driving in turn: the time is split between the selected humans, and a lone player never hands over. That is an inference from the arithmetic and the 24-hour special case. The sources do not state it.

Results and timing pages

When a session ends, the results screens read their data straight out of the game's memory. The port found that each page has its own sources, and that the obvious table is often the wrong one.

PageReads
Race Finishing TimesClassification order SS:1940; raw times SS:1982 (a high word of 0x8000 means no time); names SS:1A4A; car numbers SS:1A22; team and engine text SS:1E0A and SS:1F0E
Driver Lap TimesThe working order SS:17D8 and best laps SS:1800, kept apart from the race results
Fastest LapsCurrent track index SS:1236, lap count SS:123C, a flag at SS:1234, this event's fastest qualifying and race laps at SS:1278..1283, and a 90-byte record for the track at SS:2312 + track × 0x5A
Starting GridThe first ten entries of SS:17D8, with best laps from SS:1800; skips times of one hour (3,600,000) or more

Qualifying leaves two tables behind, and they must not be confused. SS:17D8 is a 40-byte working order used for lap times. SS:1940 is the ordered list of the 26 cars in the classification, and it becomes the race grid. The port once used the working order as the Championship's list of drivers. Reserve identities slipped in, and the season grew to 31 drivers. A regression test now fills SS:17D8 with reversed entries and checks that the grid still comes from SS:1940.

Before the results, two routines settle the final order. 0752:4E23..4F75 sorts the final timing order on top of the progress classification. D923..D96B produces the controlled driver's own result; outside a race it returns a fixed 0x8000. A car's finishing place is stored as twice its position at +AA.

The PRINT RESULTS menu lays out ten controls, from Starting Grid to Return to Results Menu, and is reachable from both the Non-Championship and Championship results menus. Result pages draw over artwork from BACKDROP.DAT: pair 12 for the generic results, pair 4 for the Drivers table, pair 2 for Constructors and pair 14 for Seasons Results.

The Championship

A season is sixteen rounds, always in the same order: Phoenix, Interlagos, Imola, Monte-Carlo, Montreal, Mexico City, Magny Cours, Silverstone, Hockenheim, Hungaroring, Spa-Francorchamps, Monza, Estoril, Barcelona, Suzuka and Adelaide. Each round is a full weekend of Practice, Qualifying and Race.

Scoring and countback

The original keeps a points table as 26 bytes at SS:28B4..28CD, one per finishing place, with a best-results limit at SS:28B2 (zero means count every result). The port awards 10, 6, 4, 3, 2 and 1 points to the first six places. The season history lives at SS:1558..17D7: 16 result bytes for each of 40 driver slots. The standings are rebuilt from those results each time a table is drawn.

The order is decided like this. Sort by points. If two drivers have the same non-zero points, compare their number of wins, then second places, and so on down to 26th. If everything is equal, the later driver gets bit 0x8000, and the table prints "=" in the position column. The Constructors table adds up both cars of each team, identified by car number, and combines their finishing counts in the same way.

Drawing the tables

The Drivers and Constructors pages are drawn by code at file offsets 0x717A5 and 0x71935, and the Seasons Results grid at 0x71ABD. The standings scroll. A signed cursor at DS:1B65 starts at −1. The page draws twelve slots from Y = 50 at a 10-pixel pitch, so position 1 first appears at Y = 60. The cursor is clamped between −10 and the number of scoring entries less two. Drivers with no points are left blank. Seasons Results lays out sixteen columns with rotated country names.

How we know

The port's Drivers, Constructors and Seasons Results pages match the original pixel for pixel. The only exception is a 368-pixel patch where the emulator's mouse pointer sat during the capture. The same exact comparison passes for the Championship Results menu, Race Finishing Times, Driver Lap Times and Fastest Laps.

The trap

The sources disagree on the size of the season's field. The seed data and the port's Championship code describe 26 cars cut from a pool of 34 for each circuit. A later full-season run (2026-09-21) found the offline game running a fixed field of thirteen teams, 26 drivers, each starting all sixteen rounds, and it superseded an earlier expectation of 27. How the two fit together is not settled in the sources.

Pause

Pause is polled once a frame by 0752:DCF9. Held keys live in a bitmap at SS:04CC, and the pause key's code is at DS:0066. The poll has three outcomes:

  • Continue, the normal case.
  • Message. If the pause key is held, or in a linked race the other machine has set bit 0x40 of DS:877F, the game shows the PAUSED message at DD94..DDD1.
  • Stop. If three particular keys are held together, the game jumps to DDD6 and runs the stop sequence.

A latch at DS:0071 waits for a held key to be released. While paused, the timer and keyboard interrupts keep running, but the race does not move. In one port test, 196 real timer interrupts arrived during a pause, and the race clock and all 26 cars were unchanged. The PAUSED panel changes 1,280 pixels on the upper screen. On resume, the frame that was interrupted is not re-run from the start. It continues from EE67.

From the port

The port first folded the message and stop outcomes into one "enter pause" result. That changed the order of events: in the original the stop check comes before the message. The port now returns a separate stop result so the sequence matches.

Replay and camera views

The original's instant replay is built from snapshots. The history writer at 0752:C40A..C4A2 fires on a deadline at DS:2231, re-armed each time by an interval at DS:012C. Each time it fires, it copies two ranges into the next slot of a ring in a scene bank: DS:0AAD..209A, which covers the entrant table and the list before it, and DS:28F7..298E, which holds the race globals. The copy runs as 350 batches of eight words followed by byte loops. The slot index is at DS:2229. The restore at C4A3..C50A brings back the oldest snapshot and saves the live clock so the replay knows where to stop.

The trap

The copy has to be done the original's way: eight words at a time, then bytes. A scene bank can overlap the live image, so a single large block copy would read bytes the copy had already overwritten, and give a different result.

The player also feeds the replay. The controls read during replay come from the snapshot, not the keyboard. The routine at 213F:29A2..29BC skips the hardware and, once the saved control deadline is reached, restores the control-method bytes to the selected cars.

Camera views are chosen by 0752:E576. Requests are bits in DS:0983, handled in a fixed order of priority at E7A0..E825. The keys are:

The external views move the scene by 0x1400 at E761, and C2D0 resets it after drawing. The camera's own record is at DS:099B. The stationary trackside camera is a small system of its own: placement at C713/C7DF, aiming along the route at C767 and at a live car at C94B, alignment at C819, and a marker search at C882. It also plays the sound of a car passing, through 213F:2E44. A view change fades through black. The call at E4FF resolves to the resident routine 213F:0046 and on to 8B6E:0143, with SS:08E6 set before function 6. The hand-over state machine uses the same black-fade call.

The same per-frame command routine, 0752:E2BC, handles the other in-race keys: G shows nearby gaps, D cycles the detail level (DS:0068, wrapping from 0 back to 3), - and = set the sound level (DS:006A, 0 to 2), and Enter requests or cancels a pit stop.

SAVE GAME

The original can save a session in progress. Its save is a raw copy of three memory ranges, made by 15F2:C5E0, followed by a checksum. The file is 26,736 bytes: a 26,732-byte body and a four-byte checksum.

The writer makes two changes as it copies. In the first range it replaces the word at SS:1232 with an operation code of 1 from DS:0918. Before copying the second range it seeds DS:096B and DS:096D with the main and pit segment values. The checksum is two words. One is a plain 16-bit sum of the bytes. The other starts at zero and, for each byte, rotates left three bits and adds the byte. The same checksum protects the preferences file (Chapter 3).

The third range begins at 0x11E6 in the scene bank. That is also where the replay ring wraps to. It is likely, though not confirmed, that a save carries the replay history with it.

From the port

The save format took three attempts. The first reading (2026-09-10) was a single copy starting at 381C:4C38. By 2026-09-14 that had fallen apart: the live range at that address began with a road-scene header, not a save header, and a careful trace found the three-range writer. Then on 2026-09-15 the original game loaded a file the port had written, said "GAME LOADED", and rejected it as "Invalid Data". Swapping ranges into a known-good save showed the checksum and header were fine. The fault was the two segment seeds, which the port had not written. With them added, the original loads the port's save and reaches the same restored frame.

The port adds its own checkpoint beside the raw save, because the raw format does not hold everything a live session needs: the lower screen, the sound state, the pause and replay state. Pressing S during a session writes both. A loaded raw save is checked for its scene header, operation code and checksum before anything live is touched. A checksum alone would also accept other files in the save folder.

The ceremony and the trophy

At the end of a season the game plays an animated ceremony. The animations are held in two files. CHAMP.DAT has 23 records: an LZW-compressed base frame followed by frames stored as byte runs XORed onto the one before, each with a VGA palette. Records 1 to 7 are the "WORLD CHAMPIONSHIP" podium and records 9 to 22 the champagne scene. TROPHY.DAT holds the sixteen-frame season-complete sequence.

Both are played by a resident dispatcher at 7339:0000, which looks up a script through a word table at 7339:002C. A capture from the original, loading a constructed final-season save through Load Game, followed the whole route. It ran from the results caller at 213F:0FA6 into the dispatcher, and the trophy sequence played. After Continue came about 45 captured steps: the results still visible, the trophy car scene, a fade of the car, a fade to black, the board scene coming in, the full board, and finally the SEASON COMPLETE menu. That menu offers Review, which goes back to the Championship results, and Save.

The trap

The route to CHAMP.DAT is not settled. The project's notes from 2026-09-19 disagree on which dispatcher entries lead to which file. A probe that ran a whole unlinked season with breakpoints on the candidate callers hit none of them. No capture yet shows the original reaching the CHAMP.DAT podium naturally, so this chapter does not claim a route for it.
