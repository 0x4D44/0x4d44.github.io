mdem · performance programme · June – October 2026

Chasing DOSBox-X

In June 2026 mdem abandoned its third x86 recompiler in three weeks. Its newest attempt ran DOOM ten times slower than DOSBox-X, and slower than mdem's own interpreter. By the end of September its x86-64 recompiler, DBX, ran DOOM twice as fast as DOSBox-X's dynamic core and Quake 1.4 times as fast. A second, portable recompiler went from 7× slower to 2.3× faster on Apple Silicon in nine days of August. This is the record of how that happened: what was measured, what went wrong, and which AI models did the work.

R = mdem net wall ÷ DOSBox-X net wall, equal work: DOOM -timedemo demo3 (2,134 gametics) · Quake timedemo demo1 (969 frames) · R < 1 means mdem is faster

03 · The DRC week

Twenty-nine experiments, 3 to 11 August

The portable recompiler entered the suite on 3 August at R = 7.01 on DOOM and 5.40 on Quake. Over the following nine days it ran a numbered chain of experiments (EXP-A002 to EXP-A054, then the W legs). Each one was measured as a PGO build on FLUX, then kept or reverted. Below is every recorded step in order. DOOM crossed parity on 6 August with the zero-victim SMC scan memo (A033). Quake followed on 11 August.

04 · After parity

New north stars: Half-Life 2 and host cost

Once both engines beat DOSBox-X, the 11 August options ladder set a new target: "keep Quake as the instrument; retire it as the banner". The new banner was Half-Life 2, a 2004 Direct3D 9 game with SSE-heavy maths, far from the DOS titles the recompilers were tuned on. From 22 September the goal was a 25 FPS floor, measured as the worst 5-second window of a fixed replay, on both engines. On DBX the floor rose from 10.3 to 23.1 FPS over two days of measured levers. The flag-parking lever was the first deliberate departure from DOSBox-X's emitted bytes. The DBX timing box then moved to CRUCIBLE (Ryzen 9 9950X3D), where the floor reached 41.8 FPS.

HL2 worst-5-second FPS · DBX on KILN · 27–28 September

Each bar adds one lever to the bar before it. The line is the 25 FPS goal. Same PGO build family and host throughout. Source: wrk_journals/… DBX HL2 floor campaign.

The metric changed with the target. Wall time on a shared box had misled the programme too often, so the September campaign judged levers by h/g, host instructions per guest instruction, which stays the same under load. It used c/g, host cycles per guest instruction, and FPS only as guards. The basket runner (29 September) measures h/g and c/g across HL2, CMR2, CMR3, Quake, GLQuake, DOOM and FastDoom on both hosts. A QEMU baseline the same day put the DRC within 6% of QEMU TCG on Quake (72.2 vs 68.1 h/g), but 5× behind on FastDoom's integer path.

05 · Who did the work

Seven model generations on one codebase

Agents sign their commits with a Co-Authored-By trailer naming their model. This section counts the 2,495 commits from 1 June to 3 October that touch the perf code. That covers the x86 recompilers (mddosem-dyncore, which holds DBX and DRC, and its deleted predecessors), the exact-machine JITs (mdjit-*), the perf suite, the PGO script and the perf-loop skill. A commit with two co-authors counts half to each. Every commit is pushed under the owner's account, so the trailer is the only attribution.
