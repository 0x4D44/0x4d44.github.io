About this atlas

The Second World War animates the war month by month from the Japanese invasion of China in 1937 to the surrender of Japan in 1945. Countries are tinted by who controlled them; bold lines trace the front where armies met; solid arrows mark the great offensives, dashed tracks the air raids and the carrier run to Pearl Harbor; markers flare at each battle on land, at sea and in the air, with sombre ringed markers for the Holocaust and other atrocities; and the curve below charts the Battle of the Atlantic — the quarterly merchant tonnage sent to the bottom by the U-boats, with red flashes for the ships going down. A running death toll climbs in the corner.

How to read it

  • Press play, or drag the time ribbon to scrub through the war.
  • Switch view between the European/Atlantic and Pacific theatres.
  • Hover a battle marker, a country or a ribbon tick for detail.
  • Colours: Axis ochre, the Soviet Union red (its own front and nearly half the war's dead), the Western Allies blue, liberated teal.

The war at sea ran far beyond the Atlantic — the carrier battles of the Pacific (Coral Sea, Midway, Leyte Gulf) and the duels of the Mediterranean (Taranto, Matapan) appear as sea markers in their theatres.

Sources & method

Dates, places and outcomes were assembled from standard references (Wikipedia, Britannica, naval-history.net, official campaign histories) and put through repeated adversarial fact-checking passes. Front-line positions are interpolated between dated keyframes and are necessarily schematic; the death toll is a rounded global estimate (~70–85 million).

Caveats

Country fills use modern borders — the only world atlas a static page can load — so the Soviet Union shows as its successor states, and the front-line overlay (not the fills) carries the true line of contact through the USSR. This is an illustration, not a substitute for a historical GIS.
