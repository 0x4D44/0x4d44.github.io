The parade ground · past-paper practice

Past papers: the firing range

The Exam drill taught you how a Higher Maths paper is marked. This deck lets you sit questions and be marked. Pick a topic, type your answers, press Mark my answer, and the page checks them against the marking scheme line by line, shows the worked solution with its marks, and keeps your running totals. When you are ready, draw a half-length Paper 1 (no calculator) or Paper 2 (calculator) and run the clock.

Read this first: original questions, not the real papers

Every question here was written for this site, modelled on the question types of the recent Higher Mathematics papers (2022 to 2026): the same four areas, broadly in proportion, the same command words (find, determine, show that, express in the form, solve, hence), the same habit of chaining parts so that (b) uses (a), and the same split between a non-calculator and a calculator paper. None is copied from a past paper, and none predicts what will be asked. The real papers and marking instructions are free on the SQA past-papers page; work through those too. A few parts (multiple choice, tick-all-that-apply) are a practice-only format: the real papers do not use them.

The automatic marker judges your answer by value, not by spelling: 1/2, 0.5 and 2/4 are the same, and (x+1)^2 equals x^2+2x+1. It is a practice aid, not an examiner: SQA awards marks for working, and this panel checks the results of each step. The worked solution shows where the real marks sit (•1, •2 …).

How to type your answers

Powers: x^2, x^(1/2), e^(2x) (always bracket a power that is more than one symbol). Roots and constants: sqrt(3), pi. Fractions: 3/4. Functions: sin(2x), ln(x). Multiplication can be left out: 2x(x-1). Equations: y = 2x + 3, in any arrangement. Lists: -1, 2, 3. Vectors: (3, -1, 2) or 3i - j + 2k. For an integral, add + c as you would in the exam. Angles: just the number, in the unit the question asks for. If the marker cannot read something, it says so and does not count it wrong.
