The gun deck · rates, gradients and areas

Calculus

Calculus is the mathematics of change. Differentiation finds how fast something is changing: the gradient of a curve, the speed of a shot, the rate at which a barrel fills. Integration runs the other way: from a rate back to an amount, or to an area. This deck covers every calculus skill in the course specification, about 15–40% of the marks.

On HMS Lodestone's gun deck, Captain Grant's gunners already know that a shot climbs fast, slows, hangs at the top and falls. Calculus lets the cadets say exactly how fast, at every instant.

1 · Differentiation: the gradient at a point CALC · differentiating functions

A straight line has one gradient everywhere. A curve's steepness changes as you move along it. The gradient of a curve at a point is the gradient of the tangent there: the straight line that just touches the curve and points the same way.

The derivative of $f(x)$, written $f'(x)$ or $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, is a new function that gives that gradient at every $x$. It also measures a rate of change: how fast $y$ is changing compared with $x$.

The gunner's sight: a tangent that slides along the curve

Move the point along the curve. The red tangent shows the gradient there, and as the point moves, the lower graph plots that gradient against $x$: that is the graph of $f'(x)$. Watch what $f'$ does at turning points.

The rule for powers

Differentiation from first principles is not in the course. You need the rule:

$$\text{if } f(x) = ax^n \text{ then } f'(x) = anx^{n-1}.$$

"Multiply by the power, then take one off the power." It works for any power: whole, negative or fractional. A constant on its own differentiates to 0 (a horizontal line has gradient 0), and each term of a sum is differentiated separately.

✎ Prepare before you differentiate

The rule only works on terms of the form $ax^n$. Rewrite everything first:

  • roots as powers: $\sqrt{x} = x^{\frac12}$, $\ \sqrt[3]{x^2} = x^{\frac23}$;
  • $x$ on the bottom as negative powers: $\dfrac{3}{x^2} = 3x^{-2}$, $\ \dfrac{1}{2x} = \tfrac12x^{-1}$;
  • brackets multiplied out: $(x+3)(x-1) = x^2 + 2x - 3$;
  • fractions split into separate terms: $\dfrac{x^2 + 4}{x} = x + 4x^{-1}$.

Worked example 1 · P1 · prepare, then differentiate

Differentiate $y = 4x^3 - \dfrac{2}{x} + \sqrt{x}$.

Show solution
  1. $y = 4x^3 - 2x^{-1} + x^{\frac12}$ Rewrite with powers of $x$.
  2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12x^2 + 2x^{-2} + \tfrac12x^{-\frac12}$ Differentiate each term. $-2 \times (-1) = +2$.
  3. $= 12x^2 + \dfrac{2}{x^2} + \dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ Positive powers are tidier, though not required unless asked.

Worked example 2 · P1 · split the fraction

$f(x) = \dfrac{2x^2 - 3}{x}$, $x \ne 0$. Find $f'(x)$.

Show solution
  1. $f(x) = \dfrac{2x^2}{x} - \dfrac{3}{x} = 2x - 3x^{-1}$ Divide each term on top by $x$.
  2. $f'(x) = 2 + 3x^{-2}$ Differentiate.
🎖 Cadet Sergeant Rahman asks…

"Can't I just differentiate the top and the bottom of a fraction separately?"

Flight Lieutenant Murray: "No. There's no rule like that at Higher. With $\frac{2x^2 - 3}{x}$, differentiating top and bottom gives $\frac{4x}{1} = 4x$, but the true answer is $2 + \frac{3}{x^2}$. Divide through first, so that every term is a single power of $x$."

Sine and cosine

Watch the tangent slide along $y = \sin x$ in the widget: the gradient is largest at $x = 0$, zero at the peaks and most negative at $x = \pi$. That is the cosine graph. When $x$ is in radians:

$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$$

So $\frac{d}{dx}(k\sin x) = k\cos x$.

$$\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x$$

Note the minus sign. $\frac{d}{dx}(k\cos x) = -k\sin x$.

The formulae list gives $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin ax) = a\cos ax$ and $\frac{d}{dx}(\cos ax) = -a\sin ax$. These rules are only true in radians.

Worked example 3 · P1 · trig derivative at an exact value

$f(x) = 5\sin x - 3\cos x$. Find the exact value of $f'\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 5\cos x + 3\sin x$ $-3 \times (-\sin x) = +3\sin x$.
  2. $f'\left(\tfrac{\pi}{3}\right) = 5 \times \tfrac12 + 3 \times \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} = \dfrac{5 + 3\sqrt3}{2}$ Exact values.

Rates of change

A derivative is a rate: $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is "how much $y$ changes per unit of $x$". If $s$ is the distance of a minecart along its track after $t$ seconds, then $\frac{ds}{dt}$ is its velocity. If $V$ is the volume of water in the tank, $\frac{dV}{dt}$ is how fast it is filling.

Worked example 4 · P1 · a velocity

A minecart's displacement after $t$ seconds is $s = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t$ metres. Find its velocity when $t = 4$.

Show solution
  1. $v = \dfrac{ds}{dt} = 3t^2 - 12t + 9$ Velocity is the rate of change of displacement.
  2. $t = 4$: $v = 48 - 48 + 9 = 9$ m s$^{-1}$ Substitute.
✔ Logbook: you must be able to…
  • rewrite roots, fractions and brackets as powers of $x$, then differentiate;
  • differentiate $k\sin x$ and $k\cos x$ (radians);
  • use a derivative as a rate of change.

2 · The chain rule CALC · composite functions

How do you differentiate $(3x - 1)^5$? You could multiply it out, but that is slow, and for $\sqrt{x^2 + 5}$ it's impossible. These are composite functions: an inner function inside an outer one. Their derivative is given by the chain rule:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \times \frac{du}{dx}, \qquad\text{or}\qquad \frac{d}{dx}f\big(g(x)\big) = f'\big(g(x)\big) \times g'(x).$$

In words: differentiate the outside (leaving the inside alone), then multiply by the derivative of the inside.

Think of gears in the capstan's gearbox. If the middle gear $u$ turns 3 times for every turn of $x$, and the last gear $y$ turns 80 times for every turn of $u$, then $y$ turns $3 \times 80 = 240$ times for every turn of $x$. Rates of change in a chain multiply.

The capstan's gearbox: rates multiply

Pick a composite function and a value of $x$. Each gear turns at the rate shown, and the last gear's speed is the product. Compare it with the gradient measured directly from the graph.

Worked example 5 · P1 · the chain rule four ways

Differentiate (a) $(3x - 1)^5$, (b) $\sqrt{x^2 + 5}$, (c) $\dfrac{1}{(2x + 1)^3}$, (d) $\cos^3 x$.

Show solution
  1. (a) $5(3x - 1)^4 \times 3 = 15(3x - 1)^4$ Outside: power 5. Inside: $3x - 1$ differentiates to 3.
  2. (b) $(x^2 + 5)^{\frac12} \to \tfrac12(x^2 + 5)^{-\frac12} \times 2x = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 5}}$ Rewrite the root as a power first.
  3. (c) $(2x + 1)^{-3} \to -3(2x + 1)^{-4} \times 2 = -6(2x + 1)^{-4}$ Negative power.
  4. (d) $(\cos x)^3 \to 3(\cos x)^2 \times (-\sin x) = -3\cos^2 x\sin x$ $\cos^3 x$ means $(\cos x)^3$: the inside is $\cos x$.

The trig rules on the formulae list are the chain rule in action: $\frac{d}{dx}\sin(3x - \frac{\pi}{4}) = \cos(3x - \frac{\pi}{4}) \times 3$.

⚠ Exam lookout · the inside derivative

The most common chain-rule error is forgetting to multiply by the derivative of the inside. A quick check: if the inside is just $x$, the extra factor is 1 and you are back to the ordinary rule. If the inside is anything else, there must be an extra factor.

✔ Logbook: you must be able to…
  • differentiate $(ax + b)^n$ and other composites, including roots and negative powers;
  • differentiate $\sin(ax + b)$, $\cos(ax + b)$ and powers of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$.

3 · Tangents, and increasing or decreasing CALC · properties of functions

The equation of a tangent

To find the equation of the tangent to $y = f(x)$ at $x = a$ you need a point and a gradient:

  1. Point: $y = f(a)$, so the point is $(a, f(a))$.
  2. Gradient: $m = f'(a)$.
  3. Equation: $y - f(a) = m(x - a)$.

Worked example 6 · P1 · from the specimen paper

A curve has equation $y = x^2 - 4x + 7$. Find the equation of the tangent to this curve at the point where $x = 5$.

Show solution
  1. $y = 25 - 20 + 7 = 12$, so the point is $(5, 12)$ Find the $y$-coordinate.
  2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 4$ Differentiate.
  3. $m = 2(5) - 4 = 6$ Gradient at $x = 5$.
  4. $y - 12 = 6(x - 5) \Rightarrow y = 6x - 18$ Equation of the line.

Worked example 7 · P1 · working backwards from a gradient

Find the points on $y = x^3 - 3x^2$ where the gradient of the tangent is 9.

Show solution
  1. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 6x = 9$ Set the derivative equal to the gradient.
  2. $3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 0 \Rightarrow 3(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3$ or $x = -1$ Solve.
  3. $(3, 0)$ and $(-1, -4)$ Find each $y$ from the curve's equation.
⚠ Exam lookout · from the 2026 course report

Asked for the gradient of a tangent, "a few candidates did additional unnecessary working and found the equation of the tangent line". Answer the question that is asked: a gradient is a number.

Increasing and decreasing

A function is strictly increasing where its graph goes up from left to right, which is where its gradient is positive: $f'(x) \gt 0$. It is strictly decreasing where $f'(x) \lt 0$.

Worked example 8 · P1 · find where a function decreases

Find the values of $x$ for which $f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x$ is strictly decreasing.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 6x - 12$ Differentiate.
  2. $f'(x) \lt 0$ for strictly decreasing State the condition: it carries a mark.
  3. $6(x - 2)(x + 1) \lt 0$ Factorise.
  4. $-1 \lt x \lt 2$ The $\cup$-shaped parabola $f'$ is negative between its roots. Sketch it to check.

Worked example 9 · P1 · never decreasing

Show that $f(x) = x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 5$ is never decreasing.

Show solution
  1. $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 6x + 3$ Differentiate.
  2. $= 3(x^2 + 2x + 1) = 3(x + 1)^2$ Factorise (or complete the square).
  3. $(x + 1)^2 \ge 0$ for all $x$, so $f'(x) \ge 0$ and $f$ is never decreasing. A square is never negative. Communicate.
⚠ Exam lookout · from the 2026 course report

On a question about where a function is strictly decreasing, "some candidates did not state the necessary condition ($f'(x) \lt 0$ or $12x^2 - 24x \lt 0$)". Write the condition down before you solve it.

✔ Logbook: you must be able to…
  • find the equation of a tangent at a given point;
  • find the point(s) where the tangent has a given gradient;
  • find where a function is increasing or decreasing, stating the condition on $f'(x)$.

To find the nature of each stationary point, use a nature table: check the sign of $f'(x)$ on each side of it. (Advanced Higher uses the second derivative; at Higher, the table is expected.)

Find the stationary points of $y = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5$ and determine their nature.

Sketching a curve

To sketch $y = f(x)$, find and label:

  • where it cuts the axes: the $y$-intercept ($x = 0$) and the roots ($y = 0$, often by factorising);
  • the stationary points and their nature;
  • what happens for large positive and negative $x$: the highest power dominates. For $x^3$, $y \to +\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$ and $y \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$; for $-x^4$, $y \to -\infty$ both ways.

Worked example 11 · P1 · a quartic with a point of inflection

Sketch $y = x^4 - 4x^3$.

Show solution
  1. Roots: $x^3(x - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow (0, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$; $y$-intercept $(0, 0)$ Axes.
  2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2(x - 3) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0$ or $x = 3$ Stationary points: $(0, 0)$ and $(3, -27)$.
  3. Signs of $4x^2(x - 3)$: at $x = -1$: $-$; at $x = 1$: $-$; at $x = 4$: $+$ $4x^2$ is never negative, so the sign is the sign of $x - 3$.
  4. $(0, 0)$ is a falling point of inflection ($-\ 0\ -$); $(3, -27)$ is a minimum ($-\ 0\ +$). Nature.
  5. As $x \to \pm\infty$, $y \to +\infty$ ($x^4$ dominates). Ends of the graph.

The graph of the derived function

You may be asked to sketch $y = f'(x)$ from the graph of $y = f(x)$, without an equation. Read off the gradient of $f$ as you move along it:

  • where $f$ has a stationary point, $f'$ crosses or touches the $x$-axis;
  • where $f$ is increasing, $f'$ is above the axis; where $f$ is decreasing, $f'$ is below;
  • where $f$ is steepest, $f'$ is furthest from the axis.

The derivative widget in chapter 1 draws exactly this. A cubic's derivative is a parabola; a quartic's is a cubic.

✔ Logbook: you must be able to…
  • find stationary points and justify their nature with a nature table;
  • sketch a curve showing intercepts, stationary points and behaviour for large $|x|$;
  • sketch $y = f'(x)$ from the graph of $y = f(x)$.

5 · Closed intervals and optimisation CALC · applying differential calculus

Greatest and least values on a closed interval

On a closed interval $a \le x \le b$, the greatest or least value of a function can occur at a stationary point or at an end of the interval. So evaluate $f$ at every stationary point inside the interval and at both endpoints, then compare.

The firing arc: greatest and least on a closed interval

The curve is $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5$. Move the ends of the interval. Sometimes the greatest value is the maximum turning point; sometimes it is an endpoint.

Worked example 12 · P1 · an endpoint wins

Find the greatest and least values of $f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5$ on the interval $-2 \le x \le 6$.

Show solution
  1. Stationary points at $x = -1$ and $x = 3$ (worked example 10), both inside the interval. $f'(x) = 3(x-3)(x+1) = 0$.
  2. $f(-1) = 10$, $f(3) = -22$ Values at the stationary points.
  3. $f(-2) = 3$, $f(6) = 216 - 108 - 54 + 5 = 59$ Values at the endpoints.
  4. Greatest value $59$ (at $x = 6$); least value $-22$ (at $x = 3$) Compare all four.
⚠ Exam lookout · from the 2026 course report

"Few candidates checked the endpoints of the closed interval and only focused on the nature of stationary points." Others "obtained incorrect values when evaluating the function". Evaluate carefully, with brackets round negatives.

Optimisation

An optimisation problem asks for the best: the largest volume, the least cost, the shortest time. The method:

  1. Get a formula for the quantity in terms of one variable (the question often gives it, or a "show that" part leads you there).
  2. Differentiate, set the derivative to zero, solve.
  3. Reject values that make no sense in context (a negative length, a box with no base).
  4. Justify that it is a maximum (or minimum) with a nature table.
  5. Answer the question, with units.

Copper sheathing: the biggest box from a sheet

The ship's carpenter cuts a square of side $x$ from each corner of a 24 cm by 15 cm copper sheet and folds up the sides to make an open box. Slide $x$ and watch the box fold and the volume graph trace out. Which $x$ gives the most volume?

Worked example 13 · P1 · the copper box

The box above has volume $V(x) = x(24 - 2x)(15 - 2x) = 4x^3 - 78x^2 + 360x$. Find the value of $x$ that gives the greatest volume, and the greatest volume.

Show solution
  1. $V'(x) = 12x^2 - 156x + 360$ Differentiate.
  2. $12x^2 - 156x + 360 = 0 \Rightarrow 12(x^2 - 13x + 30) = 0 \Rightarrow 12(x - 3)(x - 10) = 0$ Set $V'(x) = 0$ and solve.
  3. $x = 3$ (reject $x = 10$: the sheet is only 15 cm wide, so $x \lt 7.5$) Use the context.
  4. $V'(2) = 96 \gt 0$, $V'(4) = -72 \lt 0$: $+\ 0\ -$, a maximum. Justify the nature.
  5. $V(3) = 3 \times 18 \times 9 = 486$ cm³ Answer in context, with units.

Worked example 14 · P1 · a minimum

An open-topped water tank has a square base of side $x$ metres and holds 32 m³. Its surface area is $A = x^2 + \dfrac{128}{x}$. Find the dimensions that use the least metal.

Show solution
  1. $A = x^2 + 128x^{-1} \Rightarrow A' = 2x - 128x^{-2}$ Rewrite, then differentiate.
  2. $2x - \dfrac{128}{x^2} = 0 \Rightarrow 2x^3 = 128 \Rightarrow x^3 = 64 \Rightarrow x = 4$ Stationary point.
  3. $A'(3) = 6 - 14.2 \lt 0$, $A'(5) = 10 - 5.12 \gt 0$: $-\ 0\ +$, a minimum. Nature table.
  4. Base 4 m by 4 m, height $\frac{32}{16} = 2$ m; least area 48 m² Answer the question asked.

6 · Integration CALC · integrating functions

Integration undoes differentiation. If $F'(x) = f(x)$, then $F(x)$ is an integral (or antiderivative) of $f(x)$. Since a constant differentiates to zero, there is a whole family of answers, so we add an arbitrary constant of integration $C$:

$$\int f(x)\,dx = F(x) + C.$$
$$\int ax^n\,dx = \frac{ax^{n+1}}{n+1} + C$$

"Add one to the power, divide by the new power." $n \ne -1$.

$$\int (px + q)^n\,dx = \frac{(px + q)^{n+1}}{p(n+1)} + C$$

Also divide by $p$, the derivative of the inside. $n \ne -1$.

$$\int \cos ax\,dx = \tfrac1a\sin ax + C$$

On the formulae list.

$$\int \sin ax\,dx = -\tfrac1a\cos ax + C$$

On the formulae list. Watch the minus.

As with differentiation, rewrite roots and fractions as powers of $x$ before you integrate. And check your answer by differentiating it: you should get back to the original.

Worked example 15 · P1 · indefinite integrals

Find (a) $\displaystyle\int \left(\frac{3}{x^3} + \sqrt{x}\right)dx$, (b) $\displaystyle\int (2x + 1)^4\,dx$, (c) $\displaystyle\int 4\sin\left(2x - \tfrac{\pi}{3}\right)dx$.

Show solution
  1. (a) $\displaystyle\int (3x^{-3} + x^{\frac12})\,dx = \frac{3x^{-2}}{-2} + \frac{x^{\frac32}}{\frac32} + C = -\frac{3}{2x^2} + \frac23x^{\frac32} + C$ Rewrite, then add one to each power.
  2. (b) $\dfrac{(2x + 1)^5}{2 \times 5} + C = \dfrac{(2x + 1)^5}{10} + C$ Divide by the new power and by $p = 2$.
  3. (c) $4 \times \left(-\tfrac12\right)\cos\left(2x - \tfrac{\pi}{3}\right) + C = -2\cos\left(2x - \tfrac{\pi}{3}\right) + C$ The integral of $\sin$ is $-\cos$, divided by the 2.
⚠ Exam lookout · from the 2026 course report

On an indefinite integral, "a few candidates did not include a constant of integration at an appropriate point in their solution". Every indefinite integral needs $+\,C$.

Differential equations

An equation like $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2 - 2x$ gives the gradient of a curve but not the curve itself. Integrating gives the whole family $y = 2x^3 - x^2 + C$. One known point on the curve (an initial condition) picks out the single member you want.

One gradient, many curves: the constant of integration

Every curve in the family has the same gradient at each $x$, so they are vertical shifts of each other. Drag $C$ to pick one, or drag the point the curve must pass through and let the widget solve for $C$.

Worked example 16 · P1 · a curve from its gradient

A curve has $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2 - 2x$ and passes through $(1, 5)$. Find its equation.

Show solution
  1. $y = \displaystyle\int (6x^2 - 2x)\,dx = 2x^3 - x^2 + C$ Integrate. Include $C$.
  2. $5 = 2(1)^3 - (1)^2 + C \Rightarrow C = 4$ Use the point.
  3. $y = 2x^3 - x^2 + 4$ State the equation.
✔ Logbook: you must be able to…
  • integrate powers of $x$ (after rewriting), $(px + q)^n$, $p\sin(qx + r)$ and $p\cos(qx + r)$;
  • always include $+\,C$ in an indefinite integral;
  • solve $\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x)$ with an initial condition.

7 · Definite integrals and area CALC · applying integral calculus

A definite integral has limits, and gives a number:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \Big[F(x)\Big]_a^b = F(b) - F(a).$$

No $+\,C$ is needed, because it would cancel. Limits can be integers, fractions, surds or radians.

Worked example 17 · P1 · evaluate

Evaluate (a) $\displaystyle\int_1^3 (3x^2 - 2x)\,dx$, (b) $\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/4} 4\cos 2x\,dx$, (c) $\displaystyle\int_0^1 (2x + 1)^3\,dx$.

Show solution
  1. (a) $\Big[x^3 - x^2\Big]_1^3 = (27 - 9) - (1 - 1) = 18$ Integrate, then top limit minus bottom limit, each in brackets.
  2. (b) $\Big[2\sin 2x\Big]_0^{\pi/4} = 2\sin\tfrac{\pi}{2} - 2\sin 0 = 2$ Radians. $\sin\frac{\pi}{2} = 1$.
  3. (c) $\left[\dfrac{(2x + 1)^4}{8}\right]_0^1 = \dfrac{81}{8} - \dfrac18 = 10$ $3^4 = 81$ and $1^4 = 1$.
⚠ Exam lookout · from the 2026 course report

On a trig definite integral, a few candidates "attempted to expand the integrand in an invalid way or started to work in degrees before completing the integration", and some "did not calculate $\cos 0$ and $\cos\frac{\pi}{2}$ correctly without a calculator". The report also notes that some "did not use the formulae list effectively". The list gives you $\int\sin ax\,dx$: use it.

Area under a curve

Slice the region under a curve into thin vertical strips. Each strip is nearly a rectangle with area $f(x) \times$ width. As the strips get thinner, the total of their areas gets closer and closer to the definite integral. This is why integration finds area.

Planking the deck: strips that become an area

Choose a curve and the limits, then increase the number of strips. The total area of the strips approaches the definite integral. Strips below the $x$-axis count as negative, which is why an area below the axis needs care.

The important rule: a definite integral counts area above the $x$-axis as positive and area below as negative. So:

  • if the curve is below the axis, the integral is negative: the area is its size (drop the minus sign, and say why);
  • if the curve crosses the axis between the limits, split the integral at the root, and add the sizes of the two parts.

Worked example 18 · P2 · an area below the axis

Calculate the area enclosed by the curve $y = x^2 - x - 2$ and the $x$-axis.

Show solution
  1. $x^2 - x - 2 = (x - 2)(x + 1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1$, $x = 2$ The limits are the roots.
  2. $\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{2} (x^2 - x - 2)\,dx$ State the integral, including the $dx$.
  3. $= \left[\dfrac{x^3}{3} - \dfrac{x^2}{2} - 2x\right]_{-1}^{2} = \left(\dfrac83 - 2 - 4\right) - \left(-\dfrac13 - \dfrac12 + 2\right) = -\dfrac92$ Integrate and substitute.
  4. The region is below the $x$-axis, so the integral is negative: the area is $\dfrac92 = 4.5$ square units. Communicate why the area is positive.

Area between two curves

The area between an upper curve $y = f(x)$ and a lower curve $y = g(x)$, from $x = a$ to $x = b$, is

$$\int_a^b \big(f(x) - g(x)\big)\,dx \qquad\text{(upper minus lower).}$$

This works even if part of the region is below the $x$-axis, because only the gap between the curves matters. The limits are usually the $x$-coordinates of the points where the curves meet.

Between the swells: the area between two curves

The region lies between $y = x^2$ and the line $y = 2x + c$. Change $c$. The widget finds where they meet, sets up "upper minus lower" and evaluates the integral.

Worked example 19 · P1 · a line and a parabola

Find the area enclosed by $y = x^2$ and $y = 2x + 3$.
