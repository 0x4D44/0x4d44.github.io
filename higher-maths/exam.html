The quarterdeck · exam drill

Exam drill

Knowing the maths is half the job. The other half is showing it in the way markers reward, under time pressure, without a calculator for the first paper. This deck covers the formulae list, how marks are awarded, the command words, Paper 1 arithmetic, the mistakes markers see most, and two timed mini-papers.

1 · The formulae list

This is printed at the start of both papers. Know exactly what is on it, so you don't waste memory on these, and so you know what is not on it.

Circle

$x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$ represents a circle centre $(-g, -f)$ and radius $\sqrt{g^2 + f^2 - c}$.

$(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2$ represents a circle centre $(a, b)$ and radius $r$.

Scalar product

$\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = |\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$,

or $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3$.

Trigonometric formulae

$$\begin{aligned}\sin(A \pm B) &= \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B\\ \cos(A \pm B) &= \cos A\cos B \mp \sin A\sin B\\ \sin 2A &= 2\sin A\cos A\\ \cos 2A &= \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A\\ &= 2\cos^2 A - 1\\ &= 1 - 2\sin^2 A\end{aligned}$$

Standard derivatives and integrals

$f(x)$$f'(x)$$\int f(x)\,dx$
$\sin ax$$a\cos ax$$-\frac1a\cos ax + C$
$\cos ax$$-a\sin ax$$\frac1a\sin ax + C$

2 · How marks are awarded

Every question has a marking scheme that lists, mark by mark, what earns it: •1, •2, •3… Each mark is for one step: a strategy, a process, or a piece of communication. The official general marking principles are worth knowing, because they explain exactly how you gain and lose marks. These are summarised from the specimen papers.

PrincipleWhat it means for you
Positive markingYou collect marks for what is right. Nothing is subtracted for errors, so always attempt every part.
Working must be shown"A correct answer with no working receives no mark, unless specifically mentioned." Each line should follow from the one before.
Follow-throughAfter an error, the rest is still marked, and you can earn later marks if the difficulty is similar. One slip doesn't wreck a question.
"Show that"In a "show that" question the last mark is not given as a follow-through from an earlier error: you must reach the stated result legitimately.
Transcription errorsCopying a number wrongly from the paper (or from your own line above) usually loses the next process mark.
Simplify final answers$\frac{15}{12}$ must become $\frac54$; $\frac{4}{\frac13}$ must become 12; $\sqrt{64}$ must become 8. You are expected to know square roots up to $\sqrt{144}$.
Not penalisedWorking after a correct answer; correct working in the wrong part; sensible rounding; omitting units; "bad form" that is put right (such as missing brackets that you clearly meant); a repeated error within one question.
Multiple attemptsIf you leave two attempts with the same method and don't say which is your answer, the lower mark is awarded. Score out what you don't want marked.
Horizontal/vertical markingWhen a question has two pairs of answers, markers can award marks by pair or by part, whichever helps you.
⚠ From the course report: what to do in the exam
  • "Maintain and practise basic numerical skills regularly, particularly fractions and negative numbers."
  • Write consistent lines of working: "statements that are inconsistent from line to line do not gain full marks".
  • "Use brackets appropriately… particularly when calculating definite integrals, working with logarithms or trigonometric functions, and when substituting negative numbers into formulae."
  • "Use notation and symbols accurately… for example integral and vector notation."
  • "Score out working that does not form part of their final response", including in the additional space at the back.
  • "Use a calculator, and efficiently record their working" in Paper 2.

3 · Command words

The opening word tells you what the marker expects. In 2026 some candidates said the wording felt unfamiliar, and the course report advises giving candidates "opportunities to attempt unfamiliar and challenging questions". Whatever the words, the maths underneath is always one of the skills on this site.

WordWhat to do
Find, Calculate, Determine, EvaluateWork it out, showing working. "Evaluate" means get a number.
Show thatThe answer is given. Every step must be shown and true, and the last line must match the target exactly. If you can't do it, use the given result in the next part anyway.
HenceYou must use the previous part. Another method gets no credit.
Hence, or otherwiseUsing the previous part is the quickest way, but any valid method is accepted.
Express … in the form …Match the given form exactly, with the given conditions (for example $k \gt 0$, $0 \le \alpha \lt 360$).
SketchShape correct, with all key points labelled with coordinates (intercepts, turning points, asymptotes). Use the axes given.
State, Write downAn answer is enough; little or no working needed.
Explain, Justify, Give a reasonWrite a sentence in words that uses the mathematics: "because the discriminant is negative…", "because $-1 \lt a \lt 1$…".
Interpret, In contextSay what the number means in the situation, with units: "The tank holds about 267 litres in the long run."

4 · Paper 1: arithmetic without a calculator

Paper 1 is 55 marks with no calculator, and the 2026 report says "many candidates missed out on marks due to numerical inaccuracies". These are the skills that have to be automatic:

Fractions
$\frac34 - \frac56 = \frac{9 - 10}{12} = -\frac{1}{12}$
$\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{4}{9} = \frac23 \times \frac94 = \frac32$
Surds
$\sqrt{48} = \sqrt{16 \times 3} = 4\sqrt3$
$\frac{6}{\sqrt3} = \frac{6\sqrt3}{3} = 2\sqrt3$
Indices
$8^{\frac23} = (\sqrt[3]{8})^2 = 4$
$16^{-\frac12} = \frac{1}{4}$
Negatives
$(-2)^3 - 3(-2)^2 = -8 - 12 = -20$
$-x^2$ at $x = -3$ is $-9$

Gun drill: ten quick Paper 1 rounds

Ten quick questions on fractions, surds, indices, logs and exact values, chosen at random each time. Work each one out in your head or on paper before you pick. Gun crews drilled until loading was automatic, and so should you.

5 · Timing and strategy

  • About 1.4 minutes per mark in both papers (75 minutes for 55 marks; 90 minutes for 65 marks). A 4-mark question deserves about 5 to 6 minutes.
  • Questions get harder through each paper, but not smoothly: easy parts can come late. If you are stuck, move on and come back.
  • Part (b) often needs part (a). If you couldn't do (a), assume its result (especially in a "show that") and carry on.
  • Read the final instruction twice. Degrees or radians? Exact value or decimal? Coordinates or a position vector? An equation or a gradient?
  • Check with a quick substitution. Put your roots back into the equation, differentiate your integral, test a point on your tangent.

6 · Timed mini-papers

Two short papers in the exam style, about half the length of the real ones. Start the clock, work on paper, then reveal each marking scheme and give yourself the marks honestly. The schemes show the marks in •1, •2… form, as in the official marking instructions.

Mini Paper 1 (non-calculator): 31 marks, 42 minutes

Mini Paper 2 (calculator): 29 marks, 40 minutes

For more, the past papers and marking instructions are free. Papers from 2024 onwards have the current format (55 + 65 marks). Earlier papers (70 + 80 marks) test the same skills and are still excellent practice.
