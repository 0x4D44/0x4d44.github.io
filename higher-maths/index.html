Briefing room · read before your first watch

Welcome aboard, Cadet.

This is a complete, interactive guide to Higher Mathematics, the course awarded by Qualifications Scotland. It covers every skill in the course specification, starting from what you already know from National 5. Each chapter has clear explanations, diagrams, graphs you can drag and change, worked examples set out the way markers want them, and a short check at the end.

You will learn it with 1805 (Blockhaven) Squadron, the Air Cadets who already wrote up Higher Computing Science and Higher Physics. This time they are posted to HMS Lodestone, a 38-gun frigate built entirely from Minecraft blocks and named after the block that makes a compass point home. A frigate lives or dies by its navigation, and navigation is mathematics: the Sailing Master's chart is a coordinate grid, the gun crews depend on parabolas, the swell is a sum of waves, and the purser's ledger grows by percentages.

Four strands, five decks

The course specification lists skills under four headings: algebraic and trigonometric, geometric, calculus, and algebraic and geometric, plus reasoning skills that run through everything. This site sorts them into four teaching decks and an exam deck. Every chapter carries a small tag, like ALG · polynomials, so you can see which skill it covers.

≈ 30–45% of the marks · 8 chapters

Algebra & functions

Polynomials and the factor theorem, quadratics and the discriminant, functions and their graphs, logarithms and exponentials, recurrence relations.

 ≈ 15–35% · 4 chapters

Lines, circles & vectors

Gradient and m = tan θ, perpendicular lines and triangle centres, the equation of a circle and tangents, vectors in three dimensions and the scalar product.

 ≈ 10–25% · 5 chapters

Trigonometry

Radians and exact values, trig graphs, solving equations, the addition and double angle formulae, and the wave function.

 ≈ 15–40% · 7 chapters

Calculus

Differentiation and the chain rule, tangents, stationary points, curve sketching, optimisation, integration, areas and differential equations.

Exam drill

How marks are awarded, the formulae list, non-calculator technique, the mistakes markers see most, and a timed mixed paper.

The percentages are the official "approximate share of the marks across the papers" from the course specification. They add up to more than 100% because many questions mix strands: a tangent to a curve is both calculus and straight-line geometry.

How you are assessed

  • Both papers have short-answer and extended-response questions. There is no multiple choice. You write your working in an answer booklet.
  • Each paper starts with a formulae list: the circle equations, the scalar product, the addition and double angle formulae, and the derivatives and integrals of sin ax and cos ax. Everything else, such as the quadratic formula, the laws of logarithms, the chain rule and the exact values, you must know.
  • About 65% of the marks are aimed at grade C. The rest are harder steps aimed at grades A and B.
  • About 35% of the marks come from questions that assess operational and reasoning skills: choosing a strategy for an unfamiliar problem, and explaining or interpreting an answer. The other questions test operational (routine) skills only.
  • A correct answer with no working gets no marks, unless the question says otherwise. See how marks are awarded.

How to use this site

  1. Work through the decks in order if you can. Algebra comes first because every other deck uses it. Calculus uses the trigonometry deck too. Each chapter builds on the one before.
  2. Play with every widget. Drag the points, move the sliders, step through the algebra. Before you change anything, predict what the graph will do. Then check. Working like this builds the understanding that unfamiliar questions need.
  3. Try the worked examples yourself first. Cover the solution, write your own working, then open it. Solution steps are numbered, and the examples taken from the specimen papers show the official marks as •1, •2, … in the order of the marking instructions, so you can see exactly what earns each mark.
  4. Do the maths on paper. Paper 1 has no calculator. Whenever you see the P1 tag, do the arithmetic by hand. Fractions, surds and exact values must become automatic.
  5. Pass the ✈ pre-flight check at the end of each chapter. Multiple-choice questions mark themselves. For open questions you write your answer, reveal the marking scheme and mark yourself honestly.
  6. Finish with the Exam drill: how marking works, the formulae list, the most common errors and two timed mini-papers.
✎ Typing maths

In the open questions, type answers however you like (for example x^2 - 4x + 3 or sqrt(13)). Nothing is marked by the computer. The box is just a place to commit to an answer before you look.

Your rank

Every pre-flight check you pass counts towards promotion through the Air Cadet ranks, from Cadet to Cadet Warrant Officer. Progress is saved in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Decoding the theme

You'll see……which stands for
🧭 The chart table, the compass, bearings and the Sailing MasterCoordinates, gradients, straight lines and circles (Geometry). A chart is a coordinate grid, and a course is a straight line on it.
🟫 Minecraft blocks, cuboids and the lodestoneThree-dimensional vectors: every block is a unit cube, so a path through blocks is a sum of vectors. Algebra tiles for completing the square are blocks too.
🔥 Furnaces, hoppers and minecartsFunctions: a value goes in, a new value comes out. Two furnaces in a row make a composite function, and running one backwards is an inverse.
⚓ Cannon, shot and the gun deckParabolas, quadratics and optimisation. The path of a shot is a quadratic graph, and its gradient at any instant is a derivative (Calculus).
🌊 The swell, the tide and the ship's bellTrigonometric graphs and the wave function (Trigonometry).
📜 The purser's ledger and the log lineExponentials, logarithms and recurrence relations: money, stores and populations that grow or shrink by a percentage. (A ship's "log" and a logarithm share only a name.)
✈ Air Cadets, the Grob Tutor and the parade groundYour progress (ranks and pre-flight checks), the Tutor's climb used for rates of change in Calculus, and the aerobatic graphs in the banner at the top of this page.

Ship's glossary

None of these words will be in your exam. They are period flavour, explained here so they never get in the way.

Frigate
A fast warship of Nelson's era with one main gun deck, used for scouting and carrying messages. HMS Lodestone carries 38 guns.
Sailing Master
The officer responsible for navigation: the charts, the course, the ship's position and the log.
Log line / heaving the log
A knotted rope thrown over the stern to measure the ship's speed. Knots passing in a set time gave the speed "in knots". The ship's log book is named after it.
Purser
The officer who kept the ship's accounts and stores of food and drink.
Bearing
A direction measured clockwise from north, as a three-figure angle such as 045°.
Sextant
An instrument that measures the angle between the horizon and the Sun or a star, used to find latitude.
Bow chaser
A gun at the front (bow) of the ship that fires forwards.
Swell
Long, smooth waves that keep rolling after the wind that made them has dropped.
Lodestone
A naturally magnetic rock, used to magnetise compass needles. In Minecraft, a lodestone block makes a compass point to it.
Furnace, hopper, minecart
Minecraft blocks and items: a furnace turns one item into another, a hopper feeds items in, a minecart runs on rails.
Air Cadets, squadron, flight
The RAF Air Cadets is a youth organisation for 12 to 20 year olds. Cadets belong to a local numbered squadron, split into smaller groups called flights.
Grob Tutor
The small two-seat aircraft in which Air Cadets get flying experience.
Watch, night watch
A sailor's spell of duty. "Night watch" is also this site's dark-mode button.
Blockhaven, 1805 Squadron
The made-up Minecraft town and Air Cadet squadron of this site. 1805 is the year of Trafalgar.
